## Statement

$\text{Let } \Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2 \text{ be a bounded Lipschitz domain, } \omega > 0, \text{ and } \nu \neq \alpha \text{ positive with } \mathrm{Pr} = \nu/\alpha. \text{ Let } h_v, h_\kappa \text{ solve } i\omega h = D \nabla_\perp^2 h \text{ on } \Omega \text{ with } h = 1 \text{ on } \partial\Omega, \text{ at } D = \nu \text{ and } D = \alpha \text{ respectively. Then } \Theta := 1 - h_\kappa + \tfrac{\mathrm{Pr}}{1 - \mathrm{Pr}}\,(h_v - h_\kappa) \text{ is the unique solution of } (i\omega - \alpha\nabla_\perp^2)\Theta = i\omega(1 - h_v), \ \Theta|_{\partial\Omega} = 0. \text{ Consequently } g_{v\kappa} = \langle\Theta\rangle = \tfrac{(1 - f_\kappa) - \mathrm{Pr}(1 - f_v)}{1 - \mathrm{Pr}} \text{ and } 1 - \tfrac{g_{v\kappa}}{1 - f_v} = \tfrac{f_\kappa - f_v}{(1 - f_v)(1 - \mathrm{Pr})}, \text{ the second identity whenever } f_v \neq 1.$

## Hypotheses

H1 one and the same domain $\Omega$ carries both channel problems, with the same boundary condition, and the only difference between them is the diffusivity; H2 $\omega > 0$ and $\nu, \alpha > 0$ with $\nu \neq \alpha$, so $i\omega - D\nabla_\perp^2$ is invertible on $H^1_0(\Omega)$ and the solutions are unique; H3 $f_v \neq 1$, needed only for the second identity.

## In plain words

Take one channel of fixed cross-section, a disc, a square or any bounded shape with a Lipschitz boundary, and pose the same oscillating diffusion problem on it twice, once with the viscous diffusivity and once with the thermal one, each held at one on the wall. Their cross-section averages are the two channel functions. A third problem couples them: thermal diffusion whose source is the viscous profile, and its average is the cross term that carries the gain. The result says that when both problems live on the same cross-section and the two diffusivities differ, the coupled problem needs no solving of its own. Its solution is an explicit combination of the two simple solutions, so the cross term is a fixed algebraic combination of the two channel functions and the Prandtl number, and the gain bracket takes Rott's classical form: the difference of the two channel functions, divided by one minus the viscous channel function and by one minus the Prandtl number.

For the application that raised it, a model that has the two channel functions of one uniform pore gets the cross term exactly, with no third geometric quantity. The identity rests on the two problems sharing one cross-section. For a medium whose viscous and thermal channel functions cannot come from a single shared cross-section it does not apply, and the cross term needs a description of its own. The apparent division by zero at equal diffusivities is removable; MD_0016 gives the value there.

## Current notes

Proven informally in `proofs/MD_0011.md`: the substitution, uniqueness in $H^1_0$, the collapse of $\langle\Theta\rangle$ to the two channel averages and the bracket identity, with a Formalization blockers section. Two adversarial reviews by contexts that wrote none of it confirm every clause: an independent finite-difference solve of the cross problem on slab and disc (2026-09-16), and a later-day cold check (2026-10-08) that solved for $\Theta$ directly by a radial finite-volume method on the disc at Prandtl numbers straddling 1 and on a non-convex L-shaped domain the proof never uses, agreeing to discretization level, with the identities reducing to symbolic zero. Section 6 (the modal form) lies outside the Statement; its weights are $w_n \ge 0$, zero on modes of zero mean, and its poles sit only at the modes of nonzero mean. H3 ($f_v \ne 1$) is a theorem rather than a hypothesis, since $\operatorname{Re} f_v < 1$ strictly for every $\omega > 0$, and the Statement leaves $\langle\cdot\rangle$, $f_v$, $f_\kappa$, $g_{v\kappa}$ and the uniqueness class to the proof's section 1, which a Lean statement would have to make explicit.
