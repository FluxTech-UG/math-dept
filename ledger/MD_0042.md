## Statement

$\{w \in \mathbb{C}^N : \sum_{j} w_j (Dg)_j = 0 \text{ for every } g \in \mathbb{C}^N\} = \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{(1)_{j},\ ((-1)^j)_{j}\}$.

## Hypotheses

- **(D) Fourier derivative.** $N \ge 2$ is even and $L > 0$. For $n \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$ put $\bar n = n$ if $n < N/2$ and $\bar n = n - N$ if $n > N/2$, and the symbol $s_n = 2\pi i \bar n / L$ for $n \ne N/2$, $s_{N/2} = 0$. With $\omega = e^{2\pi i/N}$, $D \in \mathbb{C}^{N\times N}$ is $D_{jl} = \frac1N \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} s_n\, \omega^{(j-l)n}$ (indices $j, l \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}$), i.e. $D = F^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(s)F$ with $(Fg)_n = \sum_j g_j \omega^{-jn}$. Then $D$ has real entries and $D^{\top} = -D$ (consequences of the definition, not extra hypotheses), and $K := -D^2 = D^{\top}D$.

## In plain words

The plain sum and the alternating-sign sum are the only weightings of grid values that every spectral derivative leaves at zero: a weight vector gives zero against the derivative of every grid function exactly when it is a combination of the all-ones vector and the alternating vector. Every other Fourier mode has a nonzero wavenumber, so it survives differentiation and some grid function detects it.

For the application that raised it, a flux-form equation discretized with this derivative has exactly two independent linear conservation laws of this kind, the total (MD_0035) and the alternating total (MD_0041), no more and no fewer. A count of the linear conserved directions, and of the multipliers equal to one that they force on a period map (MD_0043), can rely on that number.
