## Statement

$\sigma_{\min}(\theta I - M) \le \varepsilon$, and $E := -(Mu - \theta u)u^{*}$ satisfies $\|E\|_2 \le \varepsilon$ and $(M + E)u = \theta u$.

## Hypotheses

- $d \ge 1$, $M \in \mathbb{C}^{d\times d}$, $\theta \in \mathbb{C}$, $u \in \mathbb{C}^d$ with $\|u\|_2 = 1$, $\varepsilon \ge 0$ with $\|Mu - \theta u\|_2 \le \varepsilon$. $\sigma_{\min}$ is the smallest singular value.

## In plain words

Suppose an iterative method hands back a number and a unit vector that almost form an eigenpair of a matrix: multiplying the vector by the matrix gives the number times the vector, up to a residual of size at most epsilon. Then the number is an exact eigenvalue of a matrix within epsilon of the original, and an explicit rank one correction of size at most epsilon makes the vector an exact eigenvector. Equivalently, the shifted matrix (the number times the identity, minus the matrix) has smallest singular value at most epsilon: the number lies in the epsilon-pseudospectrum. Nothing is assumed about the matrix; it need not be normal or diagonalisable.

For the consumer, which estimates eigenvalues of a non-normal matrix from residuals, this is what a small residual certifies: the estimate is exactly right for a slightly perturbed matrix. It does not certify that the original matrix has an eigenvalue near the estimate; for non-normal matrices that stronger reading is false (MD_0052, refuted by a two by two Jordan block). The result is checked in Lean.
