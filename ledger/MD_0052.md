## Statement

$M$ has an eigenvalue $\lambda$ with $|\lambda - \theta| \le \varepsilon$.

## Hypotheses

- $d \ge 1$, $M \in \mathbb{C}^{d\times d}$ arbitrary (not assumed normal or diagonalizable), $\theta \in \mathbb{C}$, $u \in \mathbb{C}^d$ with $\|u\|_2 = 1$, $\varepsilon \ge 0$ with $\|Mu - \theta u\|_2 \le \varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon < 1 - |\theta|$.

## In plain words

For a symmetric or otherwise normal matrix, an approximate eigenpair with residual epsilon guarantees a true eigenvalue within epsilon of the estimate. This entry claimed the same for every matrix, provided the residual is smaller than the estimate's distance to the unit circle. It is false. The two by two Jordan block (zero on the diagonal, one above it) has only the eigenvalue 0. With the estimate 1/10 and a suitably tilted unit vector, the residual is about 1/100, far inside the margin of 9/10 to the unit circle, yet the only eigenvalue is 1/10 away from the estimate, about ten times the residual. The witness is exact, in rational arithmetic, and the negation is also checked in Lean.

What survives is weaker: a small residual places the estimate in the epsilon-pseudospectrum, an exact eigenvalue of a matrix within epsilon (MD_0051). For the consumer, which reads eigenvalues of a non-normal matrix off iterative estimates and compares them with the unit circle, a residual alone does not locate an eigenvalue. Locating one needs the conditioning of the eigenvector basis, as in the Bauer-Fike bound for a diagonalisable matrix (MD_0053), or a separation argument: a resolvent bound on the circle (MD_0054) or a block residual that certifies a whole cluster (MD_0055).

## Current notes

Refuted, exactly and in Lean. counterexamples/MD_0052_jordan_residual.py: M = [[0, 1], [0, 0]], theta = 1/10, u proportional to (1, 1/10), eps^2 = 1/10100; the only eigenvalue 0 is at distance 1/10 > eps. A Lean file, not part of this library, proves `refuted : not claim` for the literal statement with theta = 5/12, u = (12/13, 5/13), eps = 1/5 (lean-review ACCEPT, claim checked word for word). A residual places theta only in the eps-pseudospectrum (MD_0051); locating an eigenvalue needs conditioning (MD_0053) or a separation argument (MD_0054, MD_0055).
