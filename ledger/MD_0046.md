## Statement

For every $\alpha > 0$ the Hermitian matrix $\alpha K + \operatorname{diag}(h)$ is positive definite. In particular, if $S \subset \{0,\dots,N-1\}$ and the complement of $S$ contains two indices of opposite parity, the principal submatrix $K_{SS}$ is positive definite (apply the first claim with $h$ the indicator of the complement of $S$ and $\alpha = 1$ to vectors supported on $S$).

## Hypotheses

- **(D) Fourier derivative.** $N \ge 2$ is even and $L > 0$. For $n \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$ put $\bar n = n$ if $n < N/2$ and $\bar n = n - N$ if $n > N/2$, and the symbol $s_n = 2\pi i \bar n / L$ for $n \ne N/2$, $s_{N/2} = 0$. With $\omega = e^{2\pi i/N}$, $D \in \mathbb{C}^{N\times N}$ is $D_{jl} = \frac1N \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} s_n\, \omega^{(j-l)n}$ (indices $j, l \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}$), i.e. $D = F^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(s)F$ with $(Fg)_n = \sum_j g_j \omega^{-jn}$. Then $D$ has real entries and $D^{\top} = -D$ (consequences of the definition, not extra hypotheses), and $K := -D^2 = D^{\top}D$.
- $h \in \mathbb{R}^N$, $h_j \ge 0$ for all $j$, and there are indices $j, k$ with $j - k$ odd and $h_j > 0$, $h_k > 0$.
