## Statement

Under H1 to H6 with H0' and H7, for every $t \ge 0$ covered by the accepted steps and every piece $u_n$ that applies at $t$,

$$ \big|\ell(u(t)) - \ell(y_0)\big| \le \delta \, t \, \max_{\theta \in [0,1]} \sum_{i=1}^s |\beta_i(\theta)|. $$

## Hypotheses

- **H0' (approximate first integral).** A real $\delta \ge 0$ with $|\ell(f(y))| \le \delta$ for every $y \in V$.
- **H1 (field and invariant).** $V$ is a real vector space (the request's $\mathbb{R}^d$ is the case $V = \mathbb{R}^d$), $\ell : V \to \mathbb{R}$ is linear, and $f : V \to V$ is any map. No regularity of $f$ is assumed.
- **H2 (coefficients).** An integer $s \ge 1$, real coefficients $a_{ij}$ and $b_i$ for $i, j \in \{1, \ldots, s\}$ (explicit or implicit: no triangularity is assumed), and dense-output weights $\beta_i : [0, 1] \to \mathbb{R}$, arbitrary functions (polynomials in the application).
- **H3 (accepted steps).** Sequences indexed by $n \in \mathbb{N} = \{0, 1, 2, \ldots\}$: real step sizes $h_n$ (positive in the application; the sign is not used), start points $y_n \in V$ with $y_0$ given, and stage derivatives $k_{n,1}, \ldots, k_{n,s} \in V$. The sequence lists accepted steps only: a rejected trial computes stages and changes no $y_n$, so it does not appear. The end point of step $n$ is $\tilde y_{n+1} = y_n + h_n \sum_{i=1}^s b_i k_{n,i}$.
- **H4 (stages, with FSAL).** For every $n$ and $i$, either $k_{n,i} = f\big(y_n + h_n \sum_{j=1}^s a_{ij} k_{n,j}\big)$ (the stage equation, with $k_{n,\cdot}$ any solution of it), or $n \ge 1$, $i = 1$ and $k_{n,1} = f(z_n)$ with $z_n \in \{\tilde y_n, y_n\}$ (first-same-as-last reuse of the previous step's last derivative, evaluated before or after the post-step map).
- **H5 (post-step map).** Maps $P_n : V \to V$ with $\ell(P_n(v)) = \ell(v)$ for all $v$, and $y_{n+1} = P_n(\tilde y_{n+1})$ for every $n$.
- **H6 (dense output).** On step $n$, $u_n(\theta) = y_n + h_n \sum_{i=1}^s \beta_i(\theta) k_{n,i}$ for $\theta \in [0, 1]$. With $t_0$ given and $t_{n+1} = t_n + h_n$, the piecewise output is $u(t) = u_n((t - t_n)/h_n)$ for $t \in [t_n, t_{n+1}]$ (both pieces apply at a node).
- **H7.** $t_0 = 0$, every $h_n > 0$, each $\beta_i$ is a polynomial, and $\beta_i(1) = b_i$.

## In plain words

When the right-hand side changes a linear quantity by at most delta at every state, one expects the quantity to drift by at most delta times the elapsed time, times a constant set by the interpolation weights. This entry claimed exactly that for any Runge-Kutta run with dense output: the drift at time t is at most delta times t times the largest summed absolute interpolation weight.

The claim is false. Explicit Euler with the interpolation weight 2s minus s squared at the fraction s of the step, applied to a constant right-hand side, drifts by 3/4 at the middle of a unit step, while the bound allows only 1/2. Inside a step the interpolant spends the whole step length times its weight at the read point, which can exceed the part of the step that has elapsed. The corrected statement is MD_0038, which charges the step holding the read point in full. For the application that raised it, a drift budget over a window has to count time to the end of the last step the window touches, not to the read point.

## Why it was raised

Raised by 1DSim4 through C2 of MDR:2026-10-17-rk-linear-invariant: a bound on the windowed ledger residual from a measured per-evaluation residual.

## Current notes

Refuted with an exact witness (counterexamples/MD_0034_dense_weight_overshoot.py): explicit Euler with $\beta_1(\theta) = 2\theta - \theta^2$, $f \equiv 1$, $\ell = \mathrm{id}$, $\delta = 1$, $h_n = 1$, read at $t = 1/2$, gives drift $3/4$ against the bound $1/2$. The bound charges the current step $\theta h_n$ while the dense output spends $h_n \sum_i |\beta_i(\theta)|$. The corrected bound is MD_0038.
