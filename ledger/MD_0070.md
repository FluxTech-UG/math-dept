## Statement

Let $p_j, r_j, c_k, \xi_k, a : [0, T] \to [0, \infty)$ be measurable and
bounded, $u \in L^2(0, T)$, initial states $\zeta_j(0) \in \mathbb{R}$ arbitrary, and states $\zeta_j$ absolutely continuous on $[0,T]$ satisfying their equations a.e. (Caratheodory solutions). (Y-form) If $\dot\zeta_j = -p_j(t)\zeta_j + \sqrt{r_j(t)}\,u$ and
$y = \sum_j \sqrt{r_j(t)}\,\zeta_j$, then
$u y = \frac{d}{dt}\tfrac12\sum_j\zeta_j^2 + \sum_j p_j\zeta_j^2$ a.e., so
$\int_0^T u y\,dt \ge -\tfrac12\sum_j\zeta_j(0)^2$. (Z-form) If
$\dot\zeta_k = -\xi_k(t)\zeta_k + \sqrt{c_k(t)\xi_k(t)}\,u$ and
$y = a(t)u + \sum_k\bigl(c_k(t)u - \sqrt{c_k(t)\xi_k(t)}\,\zeta_k\bigr)$, then
$u y - \frac{d}{dt}\tfrac12\sum_k\zeta_k^2 = a u^2 + \sum_k(\sqrt{c_k}\,u - \sqrt{\xi_k}\,\zeta_k)^2 \ge 0$
a.e. For constant coefficients both realise $\sum_j r_j/(s + p_j)$ and
$a + \sum_k c_k s/(s + \xi_k)$.

## Hypotheses

coefficients nonnegative, bounded, measurable in $t$ (no smoothness); finitely many terms.

## In plain words

Take one relaxation state that decays at a rate p(t) and is driven by the input through the gain square root of r(t), and read the output through the same gain. Then the power the input delivers, input times output, equals the rate of change of half the squared state plus p(t) times the squared state, which is never negative. Over any time interval the system therefore returns at most the energy it started with: it cannot create energy. This is the balanced realisation, the gain split evenly between input and output, and it holds for any finite sum of such states with rates and gains that change in time arbitrarily, as long as they stay nonnegative, bounded and measurable; no smoothness and no slow-variation condition are needed. A second form, a direct feedthrough plus high-pass relaxation terms, obeys the same kind of identity with the dissipation written as a sum of squares. With constant coefficients the two forms realise a sum of relaxation terms r over (s + p) and a constant plus terms c s over (s + xi).

For the consumer, which represents a memory term by a sum of relaxation modes whose coefficients vary in time, this decides that the balanced form is passive whatever the coefficients do. Weighting only the output or only the input instead brings in a condition on how fast the weights change (MD_0071 and MD_0086). The result is checked in Lean, with solutions taken in the absolutely continuous (Caratheodory) sense; under a weaker reading of the state equation the integral bound fails.
