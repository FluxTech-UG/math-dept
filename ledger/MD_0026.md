## Statement

Assume f : R x R^d x Theta -> R^d, C^{p+1}, T-periodic in its first argument t. P(y0; theta) = y(T; y0, theta), the exact period map. y*(theta) is a fixed point of P whose monodromy M = d_{y0} P(y*; theta) has 1 not in spec M. For a grid of [0, T] not depending on y0 or theta, P_h(y0; theta) is the discrete period map of the explicit RK method of order p. Claims: there are H0 > 0, a neighbourhood V of y*, and C1 (H0, V and C1 may depend on everything, M included), and a constant C2 depending only on bounds of the derivatives of f of order up to p+1 on a neighbourhood of the periodic orbit, on T and on the tableau (and not otherwise on M), such that for H <= H0: P_h has a unique fixed point y*_h in V; ||y*_h - y*|| <= C1 H^p; and d_theta y*_h = (I - d_{y0} P_h)^{-1} d_theta P_h satisfies ||d_theta y*_h - d_theta y*|| <= C2 H^p ||(I - M)^{-1}||^2.

## Hypotheses

- **H1.** Integers d, m, p >= 1. Theta is an open subset of R^m. f : R^d x Theta -> R^d is C^{p+1}; y0 : Theta -> R^d is C^1. An explicit Runge-Kutta method is given by a Butcher tableau (A, b, c) with A strictly lower triangular, of classical order p, with coefficients independent of theta; Psi_h(y, theta) denotes one step of size h. A grid is 0 = t_0 < t_1 < ... < t_N = T with steps h_n = t_{n+1} - t_n and H = max_n h_n. y_N(theta; h) = Psi_{h_{N-1}} o ... o Psi_{h_0} (y0(theta), theta). y(t; theta) is the exact solution of y' = f(y, theta), y(0) = y0(theta), assumed to exist on [0, T]. S(t; theta) solves S' = f_y(y(t; theta), theta) S + f_theta(y(t; theta), theta), S(0) = d_theta y0(theta). Norms on R^d and on d x m matrices are fixed (any choice; constants depend on it). For theta in Theta and delta > 0, K_delta is the closed delta-neighbourhood of the trajectory {y(t; theta) : 0 <= t <= T}. Here f also depends on t and is T-periodic in t.
- **H2.** 1 is not an eigenvalue of the monodromy M.

## Why it was raised

The repaired statement after the refutation of MD_0023 (MDR:2026-10-16-adaptive-grid-gradient line), for the same application in 1DSim3.

## Current notes

Revises MD_0023, whose factor 1/dist(1, spec M) fails (scalar witness, error ~ H/(2 delta^2)). The repair uses the squared resolvent norm. The adversarial reviewer measured err delta^2 / H -> 0.5 for a = 1e-2 to 1e-4, stable over N = 400 to 3200; a non-normal monodromy is untested.

Refuted on 2026-10-17 by the exact witness listed under evidence (Euler on a two-rate grid for a scalar problem with f_yy = 2: the O(R H) shift of the discrete fixed point feeds through f_yy into the sensitivity, e1(a) = lim (d_theta y*_h - 1/a)/H in closed form with e1 a^3 -> 1, so e1/R^2 ~ 1/a is unbounded; certified for C2 in {50, 150, 1000} through the audited hand lemma L0 to L3, every C2 by the rule a = 1/(4 ceil C2)). The repaired statement is MD_0032, which revises this one: the bound with the cubed resolvent norm, uniform over grids.
