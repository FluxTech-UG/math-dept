## Statement

**Setting.** $C$ is a finite set of configurations, $|C| = N \ge 1$, and $c_0 \in C$. $M : C \to C$ is a self-map and $R(c_0) = \{M^k c_0 : k \ge 0\}$. The **orbit walk** visits $c_0, M c_0, \dots, M^{N-1} c_0$ in $N$ turns. **Memoryless search** draws $X_1, \dots, X_N$ independent and uniform on $C$. A target $s \in C$ is drawn independently of $X_1, \dots, X_N$; $M$ and $c_0$ are fixed (for random $(M, c_0)$ independent of $s$, read every claim conditionally on $(M, c_0)$).

**Statement.** (a) If $s$ is uniform on $C$, then $\Pr[\exists k < N : M^k c_0 = s] = |R(c_0)|/N$ and $\Pr[\exists i \le N : X_i = s] = 1 - (1 - 1/N)^N \ge 1 - 1/e$; hence if the orbit walk hits with strictly greater probability than memoryless search, then $|R(c_0)| > (1 - 1/e)\, N$. (b) If $s$ has an arbitrary distribution $\pi$ on $C$, then $\Pr[\exists k < N : M^k c_0 = s] = \pi(R(c_0))$ and $\Pr[\exists i \le N : X_i = s] = 1 - (1 - 1/N)^N$; hence a strict gain of the orbit walk over memoryless search requires $\pi(R(c_0)) > 1 - 1/e$.

## Hypotheses

- **H1.** $C$ finite and nonempty, one map $M$ (the case of a single input letter).
- **H2.** The target is independent of the memoryless draws, and in (a) uniform; $M$ and $c_0$ are fixed (equivalently, independent of the target and conditioned on).
- **H3.** "Within $N$ turns" counts the $N$ visited points $M^0 c_0, \dots, M^{N-1} c_0$; counting $M^N c_0$ as well changes nothing, since these $N$ points already exhaust $R(c_0)$ (MD_0104).

## In plain words

Search a finite set of N configurations for a hidden target with N tries, in one of two ways: walk the orbit of one fixed map from a start point, or draw N configurations independently and uniformly at random. Against a target drawn uniformly, the walk hits with probability equal to the fraction of the set its orbit covers, while random sampling hits with probability 1 - (1 - 1/N)^N, which is never below 1 - 1/e, about 63 percent. So the walk does better only if its orbit covers more than about 63 percent of the set. Against a target with any other distribution, the walk's chance is the target's probability of lying on the orbit, and it does better only if that probability exceeds 1 - 1/e.

For the consumer, a deterministic orbit search beats memoryless search on an unstructured target only when the orbit nearly fills the space, or when the target is known to sit on the orbit with high probability; without such structure, random sampling is at least as good. The proof is machine-checked in Lean, with both probabilities computed from the sample space rather than assumed.
