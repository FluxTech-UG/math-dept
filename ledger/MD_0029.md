## Statement

Assume f in C^{p+1} and g : R^d x Theta -> [g_min, g_max], 0 < g_min, g in C^{p+1}. Consider in the independent variable s: dy/ds = g(y, theta) f(y, theta), dt/ds = g(y, theta), (y, t)(0) = (y0(theta), 0). Integrate with the explicit RK method (order p) at fixed step Delta s, producing (y_n, t_n) at s_n = n Delta s, until the first N with t_N >= T. The method has a continuous extension u_n(sigma), sigma in [0, 1], polynomial in sigma, u_n(0) = (y_n, t_n), u_n(1) = (y_{n+1}, t_{n+1}), of uniform order >= p, and C^1-glued: for every n, (d/dsigma) u_n(0) = Delta s F(y_n, t_n) and (d/dsigma) u_n(1) = Delta s F(y_{n+1}, t_{n+1}), where F(y, t) = (g f, g)(y, theta) is the right-hand side of the s-system (as for a Hermite or an FSAL extension). Let s* in [s_{N-1}, s_N] be the first point at which the t-component of the continuous extension equals T, and yhat(T; theta) the y-component there. Let Theta' be an open subset of Theta with compact closure in Theta, and K the union over theta in the closure of Theta' of the sets K_delta(theta). Claim: there is Delta s0 > 0, depending only on g_min, g_max, the Lipschitz constant of g in y on K, sup over K of ||f||, and the method (tableau and extension), such that for every fixed Delta s in (0, Delta s0], the t-component of every u_n has strictly positive sigma-derivative on [0, 1] (so s* is unique in its interval and the crossing is transversal) and theta -> yhat(T; theta) is C^1 on Theta'.

## Hypotheses

- **H1.** Integers d, m, p >= 1. Theta is an open subset of R^m. f : R^d x Theta -> R^d is C^{p+1}; y0 : Theta -> R^d is C^1. An explicit Runge-Kutta method is given by a Butcher tableau (A, b, c) with A strictly lower triangular, of classical order p, with coefficients independent of theta; Psi_h(y, theta) denotes one step of size h. A grid is 0 = t_0 < t_1 < ... < t_N = T with steps h_n = t_{n+1} - t_n and H = max_n h_n. y_N(theta; h) = Psi_{h_{N-1}} o ... o Psi_{h_0} (y0(theta), theta). y(t; theta) is the exact solution of y' = f(y, theta), y(0) = y0(theta), assumed to exist on [0, T]. S(t; theta) solves S' = f_y(y(t; theta), theta) S + f_theta(y(t; theta), theta), S(0) = d_theta y0(theta). Norms on R^d and on d x m matrices are fixed (any choice; constants depend on it). For theta in Theta and delta > 0, K_delta is the closed delta-neighbourhood of the trajectory {y(t; theta) : 0 <= t <= T}.
- **H2.** g as in the Statement, bounded away from zero; the continuous extension has uniform order at least p and is C^1-glued at the nodes.
- **H3'.** Delta s <= Delta s0, with Delta s0 depending only on g_min, g_max, Lip_y(g) on K, sup_K ||f|| and the method, as in the Statement.

## Current notes

Revises MD_0025, whose S1 witness (triage/MD_0025_s1_witness_hermite_inflection.py) is exact: Euler with cubic Hermite at Delta s = 1 and a slope ratio g_1/g_0 = 4 across the step gives a t-component with a double zero of the sigma-slope at the crossing, so yhat has an infinite difference quotient. The review verdict was NARROW: the Statement fixed Delta s with no smallness, and for Euler with Hermite the normalised t-slope stays positive exactly when the slope ratio beta = g_1/g_0 < 4, which beta - 1 <= Delta s Lip_y(g) g_max sup|f| / g_min forces for Delta s < 3 g_min / (Lip_y(g) g_max sup|f|); for a general explicit RK the normalised slopes tend to 1 uniformly as Delta s -> 0. Of the two narrowings offered (H3, positive sigma-slope assumed; H3', a step bound that implies it), the lead chose H3' because MD_0022's claim (b) already lived under Delta s <= Delta s0 and it is the form 1DSim3 applies; the positive slope is part of the claim here. The S0 rows of MD_0025 apply unchanged.
