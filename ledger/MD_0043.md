## Statement

$\nabla G(x^\ast)^{\top} D\Phi(x^\ast) = \nabla G(x^\ast)^{\top}$. Moreover, if $G_1, \dots, G_r : U \to \mathbb{R}$ are each differentiable at $x^\ast$ with $G_i(\Phi(x)) = G_i(x)$ for every $x \in U$, and $\nabla G_1(x^\ast), \dots, \nabla G_r(x^\ast)$ are linearly independent, then $\dim \ker\big(D\Phi(x^\ast)^{\top} - I\big) \ge r$.

## Hypotheses

- $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ open, $\Phi : U \to U$, $x^\ast \in U$ with $\Phi(x^\ast) = x^\ast$, $\Phi$ (Fréchet) differentiable at $x^\ast$.
- $G : U \to \mathbb{R}$ differentiable at $x^\ast$ with $G(\Phi(x)) = G(x)$ for every $x \in U$.

## In plain words

Take a map with a fixed point, such as the map that follows a periodic orbit once around (its derivative at the fixed point is the monodromy matrix), and a quantity the map conserves. Differentiating "the quantity after one application equals the quantity before" at the fixed point shows that the gradient of the conserved quantity is a left eigenvector of the map's derivative with eigenvalue one. With r conserved quantities whose gradients there are linearly independent, eigenvalue one has at least r independent left eigenvectors, so its eigenspace has dimension at least r.

For the application that raised it, every conservation law of the dynamics forces a multiplier equal to one, whatever the stability of the orbit; those multipliers have to be expected and accounted for, and can be deflated away (MD_0044), rather than read as marginal stability. Only differentiability at the fixed point and conservation on an open set around it are used; the map need not send that set into itself.

## Current notes

The hypothesis that Phi maps U into U is not used; openness of U, the fixed point and conservation on U suffice.
