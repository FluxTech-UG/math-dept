## Statement

Let $f \in C^2(\mathbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R}^n)$, consider $\dot x = f(x,\mu)$, and let
$(\gamma_s, T_s, \mu_s)$ be a $C^1$ family of nonconstant periodic orbits with
$(\gamma_s', T_s', \mu_s') \ne 0$ (primes are $d/ds$), $\gamma_s$ a $T_s$-periodic solution at $\mu = \mu_s$. If $\mu'(s_0) = 0$ and the family is not locally a reparametrisation
of a single orbit, then the monodromy matrix of $\gamma_{s_0}$ has the eigenvalue 1 with algebraic
multiplicity at least 2. If the multiplicity is exactly 2 and the fold is nondegenerate ($\mu''(s_0) \ne 0$), then for $s \ne s_0$ near $s_0$ the monodromy has exactly one real multiplier $m(s) \ne 1$ near 1, and $m$, extended by $m(s_0) := 1$, crosses 1 with $m'(s_0) \ne 0$.

## Hypotheses

$f \in C^2$; orbits nonconstant; the trivial multiplier is the one along $f(\gamma, \mu)$.

## In plain words

A periodic orbit of an autonomous system always has the multiplier 1, for the direction along the flow. The textbook picture of a fold of cycles, where a branch of periodic orbits turns back as the parameter varies, is that a second multiplier joins it at 1 and, past the fold, moves off through 1 at a nonzero rate. This entry claimed that picture for any smooth family of periodic orbits whose parameter has a turning point. Both halves are false as stated. In the first counterexample, a family of circles whose radius shrinks to a minimum and grows again is parametrised so that the starting point also slides along each circle; the parameter then has a turning point only because the parametrisation turns back on itself, sweeping the same circles twice, and 1 remains a simple multiplier. Without a phase condition that pins where each orbit of the family starts, the family's tangent can be the flow direction itself. In the second counterexample, a planar cycle times a pitchfork in an extra direction gives a double multiplier at 1 and a nondegenerate turning point, yet the nearby multiplier touches 1 and turns back without crossing, because a transversality condition on the parameter is missing. Both witnesses are exact.

For the consumer, which locates folds of a branch of periodic orbits by watching a multiplier pass through 1, this decides that a turning point of the parameter alone neither forces a double unit multiplier nor a crossing. MD_0089 carries the statement with a phase condition and a fold transversality condition added.

## Current notes

Refuted (exact), both clauses. `counterexamples/MD_0083_phase_drift_family.py`: $r' = r(\mu - r^2)$, $\theta' = 1$, family $\mu_s = 1 + s^2$, $\gamma_s(t) = \sqrt{1+s^2}(\cos(t+s), \sin(t+s))$, $T_s = 2\pi$; at $s_0 = 0$ $\mu' = T' = 0$, the orbits are distinct circles, and the monodromy is $\operatorname{diag}(e^{-4\pi}, 1)$, so 1 is simple: without a phase condition the tangent $\gamma'$ may be the flow direction itself. `counterexamples/MD_0083_pitchfork_of_cycles.py`: the planar cycle times $z' = z(\mu - z^2)$, family $\gamma_s = (\cos t, \sin t, s)$, $\mu_s = s^2$; the multiplicity is exactly 2 and $\mu'' = 2$, but $m(s) = e^{-4\pi s^2}$ touches 1 without crossing: fold transversality $w^\top P_\mu \ne 0$ is missing. MD_0089 carries the statement with a phase condition and the transversality condition.
