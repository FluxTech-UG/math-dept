## Statement

For every $g \in \mathbb{C}^N$, $\sum_{j=0}^{N-1} (-1)^j (Dg)_j = 0$.

## Hypotheses

- **(D) Fourier derivative.** $N \ge 2$ is even and $L > 0$. For $n \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$ put $\bar n = n$ if $n < N/2$ and $\bar n = n - N$ if $n > N/2$, and the symbol $s_n = 2\pi i \bar n / L$ for $n \ne N/2$, $s_{N/2} = 0$. With $\omega = e^{2\pi i/N}$, $D \in \mathbb{C}^{N\times N}$ is $D_{jl} = \frac1N \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} s_n\, \omega^{(j-l)n}$ (indices $j, l \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}$), i.e. $D = F^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(s)F$ with $(Fg)_n = \sum_j g_j \omega^{-jn}$. Then $D$ has real entries and $D^{\top} = -D$ (consequences of the definition, not extra hypotheses), and $K := -D^2 = D^{\top}D$.

## In plain words

On a periodic grid with an even number of points, the spectral derivative with the Nyquist mode zeroed has a second vanishing sum besides the plain total: summing its values with alternating signs, plus, minus, plus, minus, also gives exactly zero, for any data. The alternating pattern is the Nyquist mode itself, and the derivative carries no Nyquist content because that mode's wavenumber is set to zero.

Together with MD_0035, this gives a flux-form equation on the grid two linear conservation laws: the total and the alternating total both stay constant in time. The second has no counterpart in the continuous equation and is easy to overlook. For the application that raised it, where each conserved direction forces a multiplier equal to one on a period map (MD_0043), it has to be counted; MD_0042 shows there are no others of this kind.
