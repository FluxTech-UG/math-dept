## Statement

**Setting.** $V$ is a finite-dimensional vector space over a field $F$, $M : V \to V$ is linear with minimal polynomial $\mu_M$, $c_0 \in V$, and $R(c_0) = \{M^k c_0 : k \ge 0\}$. The Krylov subspace is $\mathcal K(M, c_0) = \operatorname{span}\{M^k c_0 : k \ge 0\}$.

**Statement.** (i) $R(c_0) \subseteq \mathcal K(M, c_0)$. (ii) $\dim \mathcal K(M, c_0) \le \deg \mu_M$. (iii) Every linear functional $\ell \in V^*$ with $\ell \circ M = \ell$ satisfies $\ell(v) = \ell(c_0)$ for every $v \in R(c_0)$. (iv) If $\ell_1, \dots, \ell_r \in V^*$ are linearly independent and each satisfies $\ell_i \circ M = \ell_i$, then $R(c_0) \subseteq A = \{v \in V : \ell_i(v) = \ell_i(c_0),\ i = 1, \dots, r\}$, and $A$ is an affine subspace whose direction $\bigcap_i \ker \ell_i$ has dimension $\dim V - r$ (codimension $r$).

## Hypotheses

- **H1.** $V$ finite-dimensional over a field.
- **H2.** $M$ linear; a single map (the request's case $|U| = 1$).

## In plain words

Apply one linear map over and over to a vector of a finite-dimensional space. The entry proves that the resulting orbit is far more constrained than the space it lives in. All its points lie in the span of the iterates, the Krylov subspace, whose dimension is at most the degree of the map's minimal polynomial; a map that satisfies a polynomial of low degree confines every orbit to a low-dimensional subspace. And every linear quantity the map conserves, a functional whose value is unchanged by applying the map, keeps its starting value at every point of the orbit; with r independent conserved quantities the orbit lies on a flat slice through the starting point whose dimension is r less than that of the whole space.

For the consumer, these are two independent ways to shrink the region a search along one linear map's orbit can ever visit: the minimal polynomial bounds the subspace, and each conserved functional removes one more dimension. The proof is machine-checked in Lean; the third part is the orbit consequence of MD_0043, which reads a conserved functional as a left eigenvector of the map with eigenvalue one.
