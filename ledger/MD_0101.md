## Statement

**Protocol (common to this request).** A network is $\dot x = f(x,u)$ on a forward-invariant $X \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ with input $u \in \{0,1\}^2$ piecewise constant, readout $\rho : X \to \mathbb{R}$ and threshold $\theta$; or, in the Boolean class, $x_{t+1} = F(x_t, u_t)$ with $x \in \{0,1\}^k$, synchronous update, the two inputs as source nodes, readout one internal node $r$. A rest state $x^*$ is an equilibrium (fixed point) of the input-free dynamics $u = (0,0)$. For a training input $v \in \{0,1\}^2$, window lengths $T_1, T_3$ and gap $T_2$, the **test readout** $\phi_v(T_2)$ is the readout trajectory on the test window of the run that starts at $x^*$, applies $v$ for $T_1$, then $(0,0)$ for $T_2$, then $(0,1)$ for $T_3$. The **pretest readout** $\phi_0$ is the readout trajectory under $(0,1)$ for $T_3$ from $x^*$. A readout trajectory **evokes** when it exceeds $\theta$ strictly (in the Boolean class, equals 1) at some time of its window. **Associative conditioning** at $(T_1,T_2,T_3)$ holds when: $\phi_0$ does not evoke; the readout under $(1,0)$ for $T_3$ from $x^*$ evokes; $\phi_{(1,1)}(T_2)$ evokes; and neither $\phi_{(1,0)}(T_2)$ nor $\phi_{(0,1)}(T_2)$ evokes. It is **persistent** when it holds for all sufficiently large $T_2$.

**Statement.** In the Boolean class with $k$ internal nodes, inputs as source nodes, and readout an internal node $r$, say $(F, r)$ **passes at** $(W, G)$ when there is a fixed point $x^*$ of $F(\cdot,(0,0))$ such that associative conditioning holds at $(T_1,T_2,T_3) = (W, G, W)$ from $x^*$. Then: (a) for $k = 1$ (the only case below $k = 2$ with a readout node; $k = 0$ has none), no $(F, r)$ passes at any $(W, G)$ with $W$ odd and $G \ge 1$, and no $(F, r)$ passes at both $(W, G)$ and $(W+1, G)$ for any $W, G \ge 1$; (b) for $k = 2$, the network $M_{t+1} = M_t \lor (u_1 \land u_2)$, $R_{t+1} = u_1 \lor (u_2 \land M_t)$ with readout $R$ and rest state $(0,0)$ passes at every $(W, G)$ with $W \ge 1$, $G \ge 1$. Counting the two input nodes, the minimal network that passes robustly (at an odd window $W$ for some gap, or at two consecutive windows $W$ and $W+1$ with the same gap) has four nodes; this sentence is a corollary of (a) and (b), not a separate claim.

## Hypotheses

- **H1.** Synchronous update; inputs are source nodes (their values are set by the protocol, not by $F$).
- **H2.** Windows and gap are measured in update steps; $W \ge 1$ and $G \ge 1$.
- **H3.** "Evokes" means $x_r = 1$ at some step of the window, the readout being evaluated on the states produced during the window (after each update).
- **H4.** The rest state is a fixed point of $F(\cdot,(0,0))$, existentially quantified in the pass predicate.

## Why it was raised

Successor of MD_0097, whose part (a) is refuted at even training windows by a one-node toggling network; this entry restates the minimality claim with the parity loophole closed.

## Current notes

Allocated 2026-10-25 as the successor of MD_0097 after its part (a) fell to the one-node network $x' = (x \oplus u_1) \lor (x \land u_2)$ at even windows. S2 enumeration (triage/MD_0101.md): for $W, G$ in 1..9 no one-node table passes at odd $W$, and none at two consecutive $W$; the two-node witness passes at every tested $(W,G)$. Part (a) for all $W, G$ is a finite-state argument (one node, two states, periodic input) that a prover can close; part (b) for all $W, G$ is an induction on $W$ and $G$ over the witness. Both are natural Lean `decide`-plus-induction targets. The consecutive-window clause of (a) follows from the odd-window clause (one of $W$, $W+1$ is odd) and is kept as the form the four-nodes corollary uses. S1 (triage/MD_0101.md): under a constant input a one-node map is one of four maps on two states, so the pass predicate depends on $(W, G)$ only through the classes $W = 1$, odd $W \ge 3$, even $W \ge 2$, and not on $G \ge 1$; the finite check over 256 tables at $W \in \{1,2,3,4\}$, $G = 1$ decides (a) for all $(W,G)$.
