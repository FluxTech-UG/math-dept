## Statement

For every $g \in \mathbb{C}^N$,

$$ \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} (D g)_j = 0. $$

In particular this holds for $g = \Pi(a, b)$ with $\Pi$ the 3/2-rule dealiased product defined in H3 and H4 of MD_0039, since $\Pi(a, b) \in \mathbb{C}^N$.

## Hypotheses

- **H1.** $N \ge 2$ is even, $L > 0$, $\omega = e^{2\pi \mathrm{i}/N}$; indices $j, m \in \{0, \ldots, N-1\}$.
- **H2 (transform).** $\hat g_m = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} g_j \, \omega^{-jm}$.
- **H3 (wavenumbers, Nyquist zeroed).** $\kappa_m = 2\pi m / L$ for $0 \le m < N/2$, $\kappa_{N/2} = 0$, and $\kappa_m = 2\pi (m - N)/L$ for $N/2 < m < N$.
- **H4 (derivative).** $(D g)_j = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{m=0}^{N-1} \mathrm{i} \kappa_m \, \hat g_m \, \omega^{jm}$. For real $g$ this is the real-FFT derivative with the Nyquist mode zeroed.
- **H5.** Real data is the case $g \in \mathbb{R}^N \subset \mathbb{C}^N$; the grid is uniform, which is built into H2.

## In plain words

On a uniform periodic grid, the spectral derivative computed with the discrete Fourier transform, with the Nyquist mode set to zero, always sums to zero over the grid points, for any data, real or complex. This is the discrete form of the fact that the derivative of a periodic function integrates to zero over one period: summing over the grid keeps only the constant Fourier mode, and differentiation multiplies that mode by wavenumber zero. The proof needs neither an even number of points, nor a positive period length, nor the Nyquist convention.

For the application that raised it, any term written in flux form, the spectral derivative of some grid function, contributes nothing to the grid total of the quantity it updates, so the rectangle-rule integral of that quantity is conserved by the term exactly, up to round-off. This includes the case where the differentiated function is a 3/2-rule dealiased product. The product-rule expansion, a times the derivative of b plus b times the derivative of a, is not the derivative of a single grid function and is covered separately by MD_0039.

## Why it was raised

Raised by 1DSim4 through C3 of MDR:2026-10-17-rk-linear-invariant: flux-form terms of a pseudospectral right-hand side integrate to zero under the rectangle rule on a periodic grid.

## Current notes

Proved in Lean (MathDept/Results/MD_0035.lean): summing over the grid kills every mode except $m = 0$ (orthogonality of the $N$-th roots of unity), and $\kappa_0 = 0$ kills that one. Even $N$, $L > 0$ and the Nyquist convention are unused. The expanded product-rule form $\Pi(a, Db) + \Pi(b, Da)$, which is not of the form $Dg$, is MD_0039.
