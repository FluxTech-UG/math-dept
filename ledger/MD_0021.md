## Statement

Assume the setting of C2 with tau fixed, but drop the hypothesis that the decision pattern is constant on U; keep eps >= kappa tau for every eps evaluated at an unclipped trial step, at every theta. Let B be the set of theta in Theta at which the decision pattern is not locally constant. Claims: (a) B has Lebesgue measure zero. (b) theta -> y_N(theta; h(theta, tau)) is C^1 on Theta \ B. (c) There is C'', with the dependence of C' in C2 (not on tau), such that for every theta_b in B, with H the largest accepted step over a neighbourhood of theta_b, limsup over theta, theta' -> theta_b of ||y_N(theta; h(theta, tau)) - y_N(theta'; h(theta', tau))|| <= C'' H^{p+1}. (d) For each fixed grid h, theta -> d_theta y_N(theta; h) is continuous wherever defined.

## Hypotheses

- **H1.** Integers d, m, p >= 1. Theta is an open subset of R^m. f : R^d x Theta -> R^d is C^{p+1}; y0 : Theta -> R^d is C^1. An explicit Runge-Kutta method is given by a Butcher tableau (A, b, c) with A strictly lower triangular, of classical order p, with coefficients independent of theta; Psi_h(y, theta) denotes one step of size h. A grid is 0 = t_0 < t_1 < ... < t_N = T with steps h_n = t_{n+1} - t_n and H = max_n h_n. y_N(theta; h) = Psi_{h_{N-1}} o ... o Psi_{h_0} (y0(theta), theta). y(t; theta) is the exact solution of y' = f(y, theta), y(0) = y0(theta), assumed to exist on [0, T]. S(t; theta) solves S' = f_y(y(t; theta), theta) S + f_theta(y(t; theta), theta), S(0) = d_theta y0(theta). Norms on R^d and on d x m matrices are fixed (any choice; constants depend on it). For theta in Theta and delta > 0, K_delta is the closed delta-neighbourhood of the trajectory {y(t; theta) : 0 <= t <= T}.
- **H2.** The embedded error estimate, the controller and the decision pattern as defined in the Statement.

## In plain words

The setting is the same adaptive Runge-Kutta solve with a step-size controller, at a fixed tolerance, but the parameter may now cross the places where the controller's accept and reject decisions change. The entry claimed four things about the set of such switching parameters: it has measure zero, the final state is smooth off it, the final state jumps across it by at most a constant times the largest step to the power p plus one, and the fixed-grid derivative is continuous. The counterexample breaks the first claim. Its right-hand side is twice differentiable but not analytic: a decay rate equal to one exactly on a fat Cantor set, a closed set of measure one half with no interior, and slightly above one off it. The first trial step then accepts exactly on the Cantor set and is rejected everywhere off it, so every point of the set is a switching point and the switching set has measure at least one half.

The other three claims are not decided by this witness. For the application that raised it, smooth data alone does not make decision switches rare. The repair line, MD_0024 and its successors, asks for analytic or transversal data instead.
