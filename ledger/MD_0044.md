## Statement

(i) $MW \subseteq W$; (ii) $\chi_M(z) = (z-1)^r\,\chi_{M|_W}(z)$; (iii) for every linear map $P : \mathbb{C}^d \to \mathbb{C}^d$ with $P^2 = P$ and range $W$, $\chi_{PMP}(z) = z^r\,\chi_{M|_W}(z)$. Here $\chi_A(z) = \det(zI - A)$ and $\chi_{M|_W}$ is the characteristic polynomial of the restriction $M|_W : W \to W$ (degree $d - r$; for $r = d$, $W = \{0\}$ and $\chi_{M|_W} = 1$).

## Hypotheses

- $d \ge 1$, $M \in \mathbb{C}^{d\times d}$, $1 \le r \le d$, and $l_1, \dots, l_r \in \mathbb{C}^d$ linearly independent with $l_i^{\top} M = l_i^{\top}$ for each $i$.
- $W = \{v \in \mathbb{C}^d : l_i^{\top} v = 0 \text{ for all } i\}$.

## In plain words

Take a square matrix and one row vector that the matrix leaves unchanged when it acts from the left, the way the gradient of a conserved quantity is left fixed by the linearised period map of a periodic orbit. Such a matrix has 1 as an eigenvalue. Now cut the space down to the vectors that row vector annihilates. The matrix maps that smaller subspace into itself, and its characteristic polynomial is exactly one factor (z - 1) times the characteristic polynomial of the matrix on the subspace. With r independent fixed row vectors the same holds with r factors of (z - 1). If, instead of restricting, one sandwiches the matrix between any projection onto the subspace, the r unit eigenvalues turn into r zeros and every other eigenvalue stays where it was, whichever projection is used. Any further eigenvalue at 1 belongs to the restricted map and stays visible.

For the consumer, which computes the multipliers of a periodic orbit when conserved functionals pin some of them at 1, this licenses deflation: project the conserved directions out, read the remaining multipliers off the projected matrix, and know that r of its zeros stand for the deflated multipliers and the rest of its spectrum is the genuine one, with multiplicities, independent of the projection chosen. The result holds over any field and is checked in Lean.
