## Statement

For MD_0090's weights $w_k \ge 0$ and rates $a_k = \nu\xi_k$, $b_k = \alpha\xi_k$, the cascade $\zeta_{k,1}' = a_k(u - \zeta_{k,1})$, $\zeta_{k,2}' = b_k(\zeta_{k,1} - \zeta_{k,2})$, $y = \sum_k w_k\zeta_{k,2}$ has transfer function $K$. If $a_k(t), b_k(t) \ge 0$ and $w_k(t) \ge 0$ vary in time (measurable, locally integrable rates) and $\zeta(0) = 0$, then (a statement about the time-varying system alone) $|y(t)| \le (\sup_t\sum_k w_k(t))\,\|u\|_\infty$. Truncated at $N$ terms with the static remainder $r_N := \sum_{k > N} w_k$ added as a memoryless term $r_N u$, the frozen transfer function $K_N(s) := \sum_{k \le N} w_k a_kb_k/((s+a_k)(s+b_k)) + r_N$ satisfies $K_N(0) = K(0)$ and $|K(i\omega) - K_N(i\omega)| \le \omega\sum_{k>N} w_k(1/a_k + 1/b_k)$ for every real $\omega$; for the disc this is $O(\omega(1+1/\mathrm{Pr})/(\alpha N^3))$ in units $R = 1$.

## Hypotheses

MD_0090's weights and rates; $u \in L^\infty$.

## In plain words

Take a transfer function written as an infinite sum, with nonnegative weights, of two-pole low-pass terms, each term the response of two first-order lags in series. The entry makes three claims about simulating it with a bank of such two-stage cascades. The bank reproduces the transfer function exactly. When the rates and weights vary in time but stay nonnegative, the output never exceeds the largest total weight times the largest input. And when the bank is cut after N terms and the discarded weight is added back as an instantaneous gain, the zero-frequency gain stays exact and the error at frequency omega is at most omega times a tail sum that shrinks quickly as N grows.

The entry is refuted by a sign slip in the last claim. The bound is written with omega rather than its absolute value, so at every negative frequency its right side is negative while its left side, a modulus, is not; the counterexample shows this in the disc case with one term kept, at omega equal to minus one. Everything else survives: the exact realisation, the time-varying bound, and the truncation bound with the absolute value of the frequency are proved in the attached proof, and MD_0095 carries them as the statement to cite. For the application that raised it, the truncated bank with a static remainder is sound, with the error read as growing with the size of the frequency, and citations go to MD_0095.
