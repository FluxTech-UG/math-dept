## Statement

$\text{With } \Omega, \omega \text{ fixed, let } F(D) := \langle h_D \rangle \text{ where } i\omega h_D = D\nabla_\perp^2 h_D, \ h_D = 1 \text{ on } \partial\Omega, \text{ so } f_v = F(\nu), f_\kappa = F(\alpha). \text{ Then } F \text{ is holomorphic in } D \text{ on a neighbourhood of } (0,\infty), \text{ and as } \nu \to \alpha \text{ the bracket extends continuously with } \lim_{\mathrm{Pr}\to 1} \tfrac{f_\kappa - f_v}{(1 - f_v)(1 - \mathrm{Pr})} = \tfrac{\left[\,dF/d\log D\,\right]_{D=\alpha}}{1 - F(\alpha)} = -\left[\tfrac{d\log(1 - F)}{d\log D}\right]_{D=\alpha}, \text{ and } g_{v\kappa}\big|_{\mathrm{Pr}=1} = 1 - F(\alpha) + \alpha F'(\alpha). \text{ The limit is finite. For parallel plates of half-gap } y_0, \ F(z) = \tanh z / z \text{ with } z = y_0\sqrt{i\omega/\alpha}, \text{ and the limit is } \tfrac{1}{2}\,\tfrac{F(z) - \operatorname{sech}^2 z}{1 - F(z)}.$

## Hypotheses

H1 the single-geometry setting of C1, so that $f_v$ and $f_\kappa$ are one function of the diffusivity evaluated twice; H2 $\omega > 0$, which gives $F(\alpha) \neq 1$ and keeps the denominator away from zero.

## In plain words

The special case is equal viscous and thermal diffusivity, Prandtl number one, where the closed form of MD_0011 for the cross term divides zero by zero. The entry claimed five things there: the channel function is analytic in the diffusivity, its denominator never vanishes, the gain bracket has a finite limit given by a logarithmic derivative of the channel function, parallel plates have a closed form for that limit, and the cross term itself equals one minus the channel function plus the diffusivity times its derivative. The last clause has the wrong sign. It even contradicts the entry's own bracket clause, since the two together would force the derivative term to vanish, and that term has strictly positive real part at every frequency. The counterexample evaluates both values against a modal sum that carries no sign convention at all: the printed and correct values differ by 165 percent of the true value on parallel plates and by 399 percent on the disc.

Everything else survives and is proved: analyticity, a denominator that never vanishes, the finite bracket limit and the plate formula. MD_0016 restates the value clause with a minus sign. For the application that raised it, the gain bracket still needs no special case at Prandtl number one; only the value of the cross term at that point is taken from MD_0016.

## Current notes

Refuted as printed: the value clause carries $+\alpha F'(\alpha)$ and the truth is $-\alpha F'(\alpha)$. `counterexamples/MD_0012_sign_of_derivative_term.py` certifies it numerically at the disc against MD_0011's modal form; the four other clauses are proved in `proofs/MD_0012.md` and carried by MD_0016, which supersedes this entry.
