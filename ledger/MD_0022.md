## Statement

Assume f in C^{p+1} and g : R^d x Theta -> [g_min, g_max], 0 < g_min, g in C^{p+1}. Consider in the independent variable s: dy/ds = g(y, theta) f(y, theta), dt/ds = g(y, theta), (y, t)(0) = (y0(theta), 0). Integrate with the explicit RK method (order p) at fixed step Delta s, producing (y_n, t_n) at s_n = n Delta s, until the first N with t_N >= T. The method has a continuous extension u_n(sigma), sigma in [0, 1], polynomial in sigma, u_n(0) = (y_n, t_n), u_n(1) = (y_{n+1}, t_{n+1}), of uniform order >= p. Let s* in [s_{N-1}, s_N] be the unique point at which the t-component of the continuous extension equals T, and yhat(T; theta) the y-component there. Claims: (a) theta -> yhat(T; theta) is C^1 on all of Theta. (b) There are Delta s0 > 0 and C''', depending on bounds of the derivatives of f, g of order up to p+1 on a neighbourhood of the trajectory, the C^1 norm of y0, T, g_min, g_max and the method, such that for Delta s <= Delta s0: ||d_theta yhat(T; theta) - S(T; theta)|| <= C''' Delta s^p. (c) d_theta yhat is computed from the same fixed-step method applied to the augmented variational system of the s-system plus the root-shift term (d/dsigma u_{N-1})(sigma*) d_theta sigma* at s*, with no controller state.

## Hypotheses

- **H1.** Integers d, m, p >= 1. Theta is an open subset of R^m. f : R^d x Theta -> R^d is C^{p+1}; y0 : Theta -> R^d is C^1. An explicit Runge-Kutta method is given by a Butcher tableau (A, b, c) with A strictly lower triangular, of classical order p, with coefficients independent of theta; Psi_h(y, theta) denotes one step of size h. A grid is 0 = t_0 < t_1 < ... < t_N = T with steps h_n = t_{n+1} - t_n and H = max_n h_n. y_N(theta; h) = Psi_{h_{N-1}} o ... o Psi_{h_0} (y0(theta), theta). y(t; theta) is the exact solution of y' = f(y, theta), y(0) = y0(theta), assumed to exist on [0, T]. S(t; theta) solves S' = f_y(y(t; theta), theta) S + f_theta(y(t; theta), theta), S(0) = d_theta y0(theta). Norms on R^d and on d x m matrices are fixed (any choice; constants depend on it). For theta in Theta and delta > 0, K_delta is the closed delta-neighbourhood of the trajectory {y(t; theta) : 0 <= t <= T}.
- **H2.** g as in the Statement, bounded away from zero; the method carries a continuous extension of uniform order at least p.
