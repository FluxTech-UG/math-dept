## Statement

(i) If $G(x) = l^{\top}x$ for some $l \in \mathbb{R}^d$, then $l^{\top}(\Phi(x) - x) = 0$ for every $x \in U$. (ii) If $G \in C^2(U)$ and $B := \sup_{y \in U}\|\nabla^2 G(y)\|_2 < \infty$, then $|\nabla G(x)^{\top}(\Phi(x) - x)| \le \tfrac12 B\,\|\Phi(x) - x\|_2^2$ for every $x \in U$.

## Hypotheses

- $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ open and convex, $\Phi : U \to U$ any map.
- $G : U \to \mathbb{R}$ with $G(\Phi(x)) = G(x)$ for every $x \in U$.

## In plain words

Suppose a map conserves a linear quantity, a fixed weighted sum of the coordinates. Then the step the map takes from any point, its image minus the point, has zero weighted sum: the residual of a fixed point search is exactly orthogonal to the conserved direction. When the conserved quantity is curved rather than linear, the same is true up to a second order error: the component of the step along the gradient of the conserved quantity is at most half the curvature bound times the squared length of the step. Nothing is assumed about the map beyond conserving the quantity; the linear case needs no convexity or openness of the domain either.

For the consumer, which finds periodic orbits by driving the shooting residual (the period map's output minus its input) to zero while conserved quantities make the problem degenerate, this says the residual already lies in the subspace that remains after the conserved directions are deflated, exactly for linear invariants and up to a term quadratic in the residual for curved ones. Projecting the residual onto that subspace therefore discards nothing at first order. The result is checked in Lean.
