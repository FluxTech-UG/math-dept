## Statement

For $\kappa \ge 0$, $\ell > 0$, $m > 0$ consider $\dot r = r(\sigma - \kappa r + \ell r^2 - m r^3)$,
$r \ge 0$. The positive equilibria form the curve $\sigma(r) = \kappa r - \ell r^2 + m r^3$. (a) The
curve has a fold (a critical point at $r > 0$) iff $\ell^2 > 3m\kappa$. (b) An equilibrium $r_* > 0$ is linearly stable iff $\sigma'(r_*) > 0$. When $\ell^2 > 3m\kappa$ let $r_+$ be the larger critical point of $\sigma$; the stable large-amplitude branch is $\{r > r_+\}$ and it ends at the fold $r_+$, where the reverse threshold $\sigma_{\mathrm{rev}} := \sigma(r_+) = \min_{r \ge r_+}\sigma(r)$ is attained. $\sigma_{\mathrm{rev}}$ is
negative (the large orbit persists below linear onset $\sigma = 0$) iff $\ell^2 > 4m\kappa$.
(c) With $m = 0$ there is no stable equilibrium with $r > \kappa/(2\ell)$, and solutions starting
above the unstable branch grow without bound.

## Hypotheses

the truncated amplitude equation as written; real coefficients.

## In plain words

The entry concerns the simplest model of an oscillation whose amplitude obeys one equation: the amplitude's relative growth rate is a polynomial in the amplitude, made of the linear growth rate, a damping term that grows with amplitude, a boosting term that grows faster, and a saturating term that wins at large amplitude. Steady amplitudes lie on one curve, which gives for each amplitude the linear growth rate at which it is steady. The entry claimed when that curve turns back (a fold), that a steady amplitude is stable where the curve rises, and when the large stable oscillation outlives linear onset: it survives after the linear growth rate has turned negative, so a strong enough kick sustains an oscillation the linear test calls decaying. Each answer was a simple inequality between the coefficients.

The entry is refuted on a boundary case of its first clause. It called any flat point of the curve a fold. When the boosting coefficient squared equals exactly three times the product of the damping and saturating coefficients, the curve has a flat point that is only an inflection, its slope touching zero without changing sign, so a "fold" exists while the claimed strict inequality fails (the counterexample takes damping 3, boosting 3 and saturation 1, with the flat point at amplitude 1). With fold read as a point where the slope changes sign the clause holds, and MD_0088 carries that corrected statement. The counterexample says nothing against the stability and hysteresis clauses, but this entry does not establish them either, so the application that raised it cites the corrected entry, not this one.
