# MD_0012: the unit-Prandtl limit is a logarithmic diffusivity derivative
<!-- role: record | status: current 2026-09-16 | entry: MD_0012 -->

The setting, the notation and the modal representation are `proofs/MD_0011.md` sections 1 and 6, and
are not restated. $s := i\omega$ with $\omega > 0$; $\{(w_n,\mu_n)\}$ is the Dirichlet data of
$\Omega$ with $w_n > 0$ and $\sum_n w_n = 1$; $\langle\Phi\rangle = \sum_n w_n/\mu_n < \infty$.

**Verdict up front.** Every clause of the Statement is proved below except one, which is false as
printed and false by internal inconsistency with the Statement's own preceding clause: the correct
unit-Prandtl value is

$$ g_{v\kappa}\big|_{\mathrm{Pr}=1} \;=\; 1 - F(\alpha) \;-\; \alpha F'(\alpha),
\qquad \text{not} \qquad 1 - F(\alpha) + \alpha F'(\alpha). \tag{1} $$

Section 5 proves the minus sign three independent ways and section 6 exhibits the contradiction with
the bracket clause. The consumer-facing content, the bracket limit and its finiteness, is unaffected:
it is the clause that is right.

## 1. $F$ is holomorphic on the right half plane

By MD_0011 (16),

$$ 1 - F(D) \;=\; \sum_{n} \frac{w_n\,s}{s + D\mu_n}, \qquad F(\nu) = f_v, \quad F(\alpha) = f_\kappa. \tag{2} $$

Fix $\varepsilon > 0$ and let $D$ range over $H_\varepsilon := \{D \in \mathbb{C} : \operatorname{Re} D > \varepsilon\}$.
Each term is holomorphic there, its only pole being at $D = -s/\mu_n$, which has real part $0$. On
$H_\varepsilon$, $|s + D\mu_n| \ge \operatorname{Re}(D)\mu_n \ge \varepsilon\mu_n$, so

$$ \sum_n \left|\frac{w_n s}{s+D\mu_n}\right| \;\le\; \frac{\omega}{\varepsilon}\sum_n \frac{w_n}{\mu_n}
\;=\; \frac{\omega\,\langle\Phi\rangle}{\varepsilon} \;<\; \infty, \tag{3} $$

a bound independent of $D$. The series converges uniformly on $H_\varepsilon$, so by Weierstrass its
sum is holomorphic there; $\varepsilon$ was arbitrary, so $F$ is holomorphic on $\{\operatorname{Re} D > 0\}$,
a neighbourhood of $(0,\infty)$. That is the first clause.

The same series gives $F$ on the real axis directly, so $F'(D) = dF/dD$ below always means this
holomorphic derivative.

## 2. $F(\alpha) \neq 1$, so the denominator never vanishes

Each term of (2) at $D > 0$ real is

$$ \frac{w_n\,i\omega}{i\omega + D\mu_n} \;=\; w_n\,\frac{\omega^2 + i\omega D\mu_n}{D^2\mu_n^2 + \omega^2},
\qquad \operatorname{Re} \;=\; \frac{w_n\,\omega^2}{D^2\mu_n^2+\omega^2} \;>\; 0. \tag{4} $$

Summing, $\operatorname{Re}\big(1 - F(D)\big) > 0$ strictly for every $D > 0$ and every $\omega > 0$.
Hence $F(D) \neq 1$. This is H2 doing its work, and it also shows that MD_0011's H3 ($f_v \neq 1$) is
automatic in this setting rather than an extra assumption.

## 3. The bracket limit

Put $\nu = \mathrm{Pr}\,\alpha$ and hold $\Omega$, $\omega$ and $\alpha$ fixed, so
$f_v = F(\mathrm{Pr}\,\alpha)$ and $f_\kappa = F(\alpha)$ are one holomorphic function of
$\mathrm{Pr}$ near $\mathrm{Pr} = 1$. MD_0011 (15) gives, for $\mathrm{Pr} \neq 1$,

$$ B(\mathrm{Pr}) \;:=\; \frac{f_\kappa - f_v}{(1-f_v)(1-\mathrm{Pr})}
\;=\; \frac{F(\alpha) - F(\mathrm{Pr}\alpha)}{\big(1 - F(\mathrm{Pr}\alpha)\big)(1-\mathrm{Pr})} . \tag{5} $$

The numerator and the factor $(1-\mathrm{Pr})$ both vanish at $\mathrm{Pr} = 1$, and the remaining
factor $1 - F(\alpha) \neq 0$ by section 2. By l'Hopital in $\mathrm{Pr}$, or equally by dividing the
convergent Taylor series $F(\alpha) - F(\mathrm{Pr}\alpha) = \alpha F'(\alpha)(1-\mathrm{Pr}) + O((1-\mathrm{Pr})^2)$
by $(1-\mathrm{Pr})$,

$$ \lim_{\mathrm{Pr}\to 1} B(\mathrm{Pr}) \;=\; \frac{\alpha F'(\alpha)}{1 - F(\alpha)}
\;=\; \frac{\big[\,dF/d\log D\,\big]_{D=\alpha}}{1 - F(\alpha)}
\;=\; -\left[\frac{d\log(1-F)}{d\log D}\right]_{D=\alpha}, \tag{6} $$

the last equality because $d\log(1-F)/d\log D = -(dF/d\log D)/(1-F)$. The limit is finite by
sections 1 and 2: $F'(\alpha)$ is a derivative of a holomorphic function at an interior point and
$1 - F(\alpha) \neq 0$. $B$ therefore extends continuously (indeed holomorphically) across
$\mathrm{Pr} = 1$. That is the second, third and fourth clauses.

The removable singularity is structural, not accidental. MD_0011 (17) writes $g_{v\kappa}$ with no
$1-\mathrm{Pr}$ in it at all, and $B = 1 - g_{v\kappa}/(1-f_v)$ is then a ratio of two functions that
are separately regular at $\mathrm{Pr} = 1$.

## 4. Explicit closed forms, and finiteness in the concrete

**Parallel plates**, $\Omega = \{|y| < y_0\}$. $h_D = \cosh(ky)/\cosh(ky_0)$ with $k^2 = s/D$, so with
$z := y_0\sqrt{s/D}$,

$$ F = \frac{\tanh z}{z}, \qquad \frac{dF}{dz} = \frac{\operatorname{sech}^2 z - F}{z},
\qquad \frac{dz}{d\log D} = -\frac{z}{2}, $$

hence $dF/d\log D = -\tfrac{z}{2}\cdot\tfrac{\operatorname{sech}^2 z - F}{z} = \tfrac{1}{2}(F - \operatorname{sech}^2 z)$ and

$$ \lim_{\mathrm{Pr}\to 1} B \;=\; \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{F(z) - \operatorname{sech}^2 z}{1 - F(z)} , \tag{7} $$

which is the Statement's last clause. It is finite for every $\omega > 0$ because $1 - F \neq 0$ by
section 2, and $F$ and $\operatorname{sech}^2$ are entire in $z$ away from the poles of $\tanh$, none
of which is reached since $z$ lies on the ray $\arg z = \pi/4$.

**Circular tube** of radius $R$. $h_D = I_0(kr)/I_0(kR)$ and
$\langle h_D\rangle = 2I_1(z)/(z I_0(z)) =: F$ with $z = R\sqrt{s/D}$. From $I_1' = I_0 - I_1/z$ and
$I_0' = I_1$,

$$ \frac{dF}{dz} \;=\; \frac{2}{z}\Big(1 - F - \tfrac{1}{4}z^2F^2\Big),
\qquad \frac{dF}{d\log D} \;=\; -\frac{z}{2}\frac{dF}{dz} \;=\; -\Big(1 - F - \tfrac{1}{4}z^2F^2\Big), \tag{8} $$

so

$$ \lim_{\mathrm{Pr}\to 1} B \;=\; -1 + \frac{z^2F^2}{4\,(1-F)} . \tag{9} $$

Both (7) and (9) were checked against $B$ evaluated at $\mathrm{Pr} = 1 \pm 10^{-6}$ and agree to
twelve digits; the values are in `triage/MD_0011_interpolant_probe.py`'s reference functions, whose
$\mathrm{Pr} = 1$ branch is exactly these formulas and is certified against the modal sum (17) to
$1.8\times10^{-10}$ by that probe's test T1.

## 5. The value of $g_{v\kappa}$ at $\mathrm{Pr} = 1$, and its sign

**Route A, l'Hopital on MD_0011 (14).** With $N(\mathrm{Pr}) := (1-F(\alpha)) - \mathrm{Pr}\big(1 - F(\mathrm{Pr}\alpha)\big)$
and $g_{v\kappa} = N(\mathrm{Pr})/(1-\mathrm{Pr})$, one has $N(1) = 0$ and

$$ N'(\mathrm{Pr}) = -\big(1 - F(\mathrm{Pr}\alpha)\big) + \mathrm{Pr}\,\alpha F'(\mathrm{Pr}\alpha),
\qquad N'(1) = -\big(1-F(\alpha)\big) + \alpha F'(\alpha), $$

so, dividing by $\tfrac{d}{d\mathrm{Pr}}(1-\mathrm{Pr}) = -1$,

$$ g_{v\kappa}\big|_{\mathrm{Pr}=1} \;=\; \big(1-F(\alpha)\big) - \alpha F'(\alpha). \tag{10} $$

**Route B, the resolvent.** Let $L_D := s - D\nabla_\perp^2$ on $H^1_0(\Omega)$, so
$1 - F(D) = s\,\langle L_D^{-1}1\rangle$ and, at $\nu = \alpha$, MD_0011 (5) reads
$L_\alpha\Theta = s\,L_\alpha^{-1}s\,1$, that is $\Theta = s^2 L_\alpha^{-2}1$ and
$g_{v\kappa}|_{\mathrm{Pr}=1} = s^2\langle L_\alpha^{-2}1\rangle$. Differentiating
$L_D^{-1}$ and using $\nabla_\perp^2 = (s - L_D)/D$,

$$ \frac{d}{dD}L_D^{-1} = L_D^{-1}\nabla_\perp^2 L_D^{-1} = \frac{1}{D}\Big(s\,L_D^{-2} - L_D^{-1}\Big),
\qquad \frac{d}{dD}\big(1-F\big) = \frac{1}{D}\Big(s^2\langle L_D^{-2}1\rangle - \big(1-F\big)\Big), $$

so $-\alpha F'(\alpha) = g_{v\kappa}|_{\mathrm{Pr}=1} - (1-F(\alpha))$, which is (10) again.

**Route C, the modal sum.** MD_0011 (17) at $\nu = \alpha$ gives
$g_{v\kappa}|_{\mathrm{Pr}=1} = \sum_n w_n s^2/(s+\alpha\mu_n)^2$, while
$1 - F(\alpha) = \sum_n w_n s/(s+\alpha\mu_n)$ and
$\alpha F'(\alpha) = \sum_n w_n s\alpha\mu_n/(s+\alpha\mu_n)^2$. Since
$s\alpha\mu_n = s(s+\alpha\mu_n) - s^2$,

$$ \big(1-F(\alpha)\big) - \alpha F'(\alpha)
= \sum_n w_n\left[\frac{s}{s+\alpha\mu_n} - \frac{s(s+\alpha\mu_n) - s^2}{(s+\alpha\mu_n)^2}\right]
= \sum_n \frac{w_n s^2}{(s+\alpha\mu_n)^2}, $$

which is $g_{v\kappa}|_{\mathrm{Pr}=1}$ term by term. The three routes are independent: one is a
limit, one is operator calculus, one is a term-by-term identity.

## 6. The Statement's two clauses contradict each other

The Statement asserts both
$\lim_{\mathrm{Pr}\to1} B = \alpha F'(\alpha)/(1-F(\alpha))$ and
$g_{v\kappa}|_{\mathrm{Pr}=1} = 1 - F(\alpha) + \alpha F'(\alpha)$. But
$B = 1 - g_{v\kappa}/(1-f_v)$ identically, so the two together force

$$ \frac{\alpha F'(\alpha)}{1-F(\alpha)} \;=\; 1 - \frac{1 - F(\alpha) + \alpha F'(\alpha)}{1 - F(\alpha)}
\;=\; -\,\frac{\alpha F'(\alpha)}{1-F(\alpha)}, $$

that is $\alpha F'(\alpha) = 0$. That is false for every $\omega > 0$. By (2),
$\alpha F'(\alpha) = \sum_n w_n\, s\,\alpha\mu_n/(s+\alpha\mu_n)^2$, and with $t := \alpha\mu_n > 0$
each term has argument

$$ \arg\frac{i\omega t}{(i\omega + t)^2} \;=\; \frac{\pi}{2} - 2\arctan\frac{\omega}{t}
\;\in\; \Big(-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}\Big), $$

so every term has strictly positive real part and hence so does the sum:
$\operatorname{Re}\big(\alpha F'(\alpha)\big) > 0$, never zero. For the slab at $y_0 = 1$,
$\alpha = 1$, $\omega = 3$ it is $0.39354 - 0.06294\,i$.

So the Statement as frozen is false in that clause and true in the others. The failure is a sign, it
is confined to one clause, and it does not touch the bracket, which is the quantity the consumer
substitutes. MD_0012 should therefore be settled as refuted-as-printed with a successor entry
carrying `revises: MD_0012` and the minus sign of (10), rather than as proved.

## 7. What this decides for the consumer

A single-geometry closure needs no special case at $\mathrm{Pr} = 1$. The bracket is holomorphic in
$\mathrm{Pr}$ across $1$, with the value (6), and the clean way to evaluate it near the crossing is
not to guard the removable singularity at all but to evaluate $g_{v\kappa}$ from MD_0011 (17), where
$1-\mathrm{Pr}$ never appears. The refusal to march across the crossing is unnecessary for any
single-geometry medium. It is necessary for a two-parameter medium, and that is MD_0013, which is a
different statement about a different object.

## Formalization blockers

- Sections 3, 5 and 6 are one-variable complex analysis over a fixed holomorphic $F$ and are within
  reach today: `Complex.differentiableAt`, `deriv`, and `HasDerivAt.div` give (6) and (10) once $F$ is
  supplied as an abstract holomorphic germ with $F(\alpha) \neq 1$. Stated that way, MD_0012 needs no
  PDE at all and is the cheapest S4 in this group of five. The honest statement to formalize is: for
  $F$ holomorphic near $\alpha > 0$ with $F(\alpha) \neq 1$, the function
  $\mathrm{Pr}\mapsto (F(\alpha)-F(\mathrm{Pr}\alpha))/((1-F(\mathrm{Pr}\alpha))(1-\mathrm{Pr}))$
  extends holomorphically across $1$ with value $\alpha F'(\alpha)/(1-F(\alpha))$.
- What that abstraction does NOT capture is that $F$ is the average of the channel function, which is
  section 1 and needs `proofs/MD_0011.md`'s missing $H^1_0(\Omega)$. Section 1's own content, uniform
  convergence of (2) and Weierstrass, is available (`TendstoUniformlyOn` plus
  `DifferentiableOn.tendstoUniformlyOn` style lemmas exist in Mathlib as
  `TendstoLocallyUniformlyOn.differentiableOn`), but it takes the modal data as given.
- The closed forms (7) and (9) need `Complex.tanh` (present) and modified Bessel $I_0, I_1$ (absent
  from Mathlib). The slab clause is formalizable; the tube clause is not, until Bessel functions land.
