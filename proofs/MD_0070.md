# MD_0070: the balanced realisation is passive for arbitrary time-varying nonnegative coefficients
<!-- role: record | status: current 2026-10-18 | entry: MD_0070 -->

Informal proof (S3, prover). An attempt record, not a status. Outcome: closed, no GAP, under the
standard (Caratheodory) reading of "$\dot\zeta = \dots$" fixed in Section 0. Section 0 also records that
the weaker reading "differentiable a.e. with the equation a.e." breaks the Y-form integral clause
(explicit witness), so the reading is load-bearing and the Lean statement must encode it.

## 0. Setting and the reading of the state equation

Fix $T > 0$, finitely many indices $j$ (Y-form) or $k$ (Z-form). The coefficients
$p_j, r_j, c_k, \xi_k, a$ are measurable, bounded, with values in $[0,\infty)$ (Hypotheses). Let $M$ bound
all of them. $u \in L^2(0,T) \subset L^1(0,T)$ (finite interval, Cauchy-Schwarz).

**Reading (R).** "$\dot\zeta_j = F_j(t, \zeta, u)$" means: $\zeta_j : [0,T] \to \mathbb R$ is absolutely
continuous and $\dot\zeta_j(t) = F_j(t,\zeta(t),u(t))$ for a.e. $t$; equivalently,
$\zeta_j(t) = \zeta_j(0) + \int_0^t F_j\,ds$ with $F_j(\cdot,\zeta(\cdot),u(\cdot)) \in L^1$. This is the
Caratheodory notion of solution (Hale, *Ordinary Differential Equations*, Ch. I, Sec. 5), the only one
under which a measurable right-hand side has a solution theory.

**Existence and uniqueness under (R)** (not claimed by the Statement, recorded so the hypothesis is not
vacuous). Each equation is scalar linear, $\dot\zeta = -\alpha(t)\zeta + \beta(t)u(t)$ with
$\alpha \in \{p_j, \xi_k\}$ and $\beta \in \{\sqrt{r_j}, \sqrt{c_k\xi_k}\}$ bounded measurable and
nonnegative ($\sqrt{\cdot}$ is continuous, so $\beta$ is measurable; $\beta \le \max(\sqrt M, M)$). With
$E(t) := \exp(-\int_0^t \alpha)$ (absolutely continuous, positive), the unique solution is
$\zeta(t) = E(t)\bigl(\zeta(0) + \int_0^t E(s)^{-1}\beta(s)u(s)\,ds\bigr)$, since $\beta u \in L^1$.

**Why (R) matters (witness against the weak reading).** If "$\dot\zeta = F$" is read only as "$\zeta$ is
differentiable a.e. and the equation holds a.e.", take one Y-mode with $p \equiv 0$, $r \equiv 1$,
$T = 1$, $\zeta(0) = 0$, $u = \mathbf 1_{[2/3,1]}$, and $\zeta(t) = \int_0^t u - G(t)$ with $G$ the Cantor
function. Then $\dot\zeta = u = -p\zeta + \sqrt r\,u$ a.e. (since $G' = 0$ a.e.) and $y = \zeta$, but
$\int_0^1 u y = \int_{2/3}^1 (t - 2/3)\,dt - \int_{2/3}^1 G = 1/18 - 1/4 = -7/36 < 0 = -\tfrac12\zeta(0)^2$
(self-similarity $G(t) = \tfrac12 + \tfrac12 G(3t-2)$ on $[2/3,1]$ gives $\int_{2/3}^1 G = 1/6 + 1/12$).
The pointwise a.e. identities survive this reading (Steps 1 and 3 below are pointwise); only the
integral clause needs absolute continuity. The lead's brief and the Statement's word "states" both point
to (R); everything below assumes it.

## 1. Y-form pointwise identity (uses: (R), $r_j \ge 0$)

Let $N_Y \subset [0,T]$ be the null set off which every $\zeta_j$ is differentiable with
$\dot\zeta_j(t) = -p_j(t)\zeta_j(t) + \sqrt{r_j(t)}\,u(t)$ (finite union of null sets, finitely many $j$),
and off which $u(t)$ is a fixed representative value. For $t \notin N_Y$, the ordinary product rule at the
point $t$ gives that $V(t) := \tfrac12\sum_j\zeta_j(t)^2$ is differentiable at $t$ with
$$\dot V = \sum_j \zeta_j\dot\zeta_j = -\sum_j p_j\zeta_j^2 + u\sum_j\sqrt{r_j}\,\zeta_j = -\sum_j p_j\zeta_j^2 + u y .$$
So $u y = \dot V + \sum_j p_j\zeta_j^2$ a.e. The square root is real because $r_j \ge 0$; no other
property of the coefficients is used here.

## 2. Y-form integral bound (uses: Step 1, (R), boundedness, $u \in L^2$, $p_j \ge 0$)

(a) *$V$ is absolutely continuous.* Each $\zeta_j$ is absolutely continuous on the compact $[0,T]$, hence
bounded, and a product of absolutely continuous functions on a compact interval is absolutely continuous
(Royden and Fitzpatrick, *Real Analysis*, 4th ed., Ch. 6, Sec. 6.4, the closure of AC under products on a
closed bounded interval; directly: $|fg(x) - fg(x')| \le \|f\|_\infty|g(x)-g(x')| + \|g\|_\infty|f(x)-f(x')|$).
So $V$ is absolutely continuous, and by the fundamental theorem of calculus for absolutely continuous
functions (Royden and Fitzpatrick, Ch. 6, Sec. 6.5: $f$ is AC iff $f' \in L^1$ and $f(b) - f(a) = \int_a^b f'$),
$\dot V \in L^1$ and $\int_0^T \dot V = V(T) - V(0)$.

(b) *Integrability of the other terms.* $\zeta_j$ is continuous, so bounded by some $Z$; $p_j\zeta_j^2$ is
measurable and bounded by $MZ^2$, so in $L^1$. $y = \sum_j \sqrt{r_j}\zeta_j$ is measurable and bounded,
so $u y \in L^1$ (since $u \in L^1$). Step 1 then makes $u y - \sum_j p_j\zeta_j^2 = \dot V$ a.e.
consistent with (a).

(c) Integrate Step 1 over $[0,T]$:
$$\int_0^T u y\,dt = V(T) - V(0) + \int_0^T\sum_j p_j\zeta_j^2\,dt \ \ge\ -V(0) = -\tfrac12\sum_j\zeta_j(0)^2 ,$$
because $V(T) \ge 0$ and $p_j \ge 0$ makes the last integral nonnegative. $\square$

The bound is sharp: $p \equiv 0$, $r \equiv 1$, $\zeta(0) = 1$, $u \equiv -1$ on $[0,1]$ gives $\zeta(t) = 1 - t$
and $\int u y = -1/2$ (checked in the probe).

## 3. Z-form pointwise identity and sign (uses: (R), $c_k, \xi_k, a \ge 0$)

Off a null set $N_Z$ (as in Step 1), with $W := \tfrac12\sum_k\zeta_k^2$, the product rule gives
$\dot W = \sum_k\zeta_k\dot\zeta_k = \sum_k\bigl(-\xi_k\zeta_k^2 + \sqrt{c_k\xi_k}\,\zeta_k u\bigr)$, and
$$u y - \dot W = a u^2 + \sum_k\bigl(c_k u^2 - \sqrt{c_k\xi_k}\,\zeta_k u\bigr) + \sum_k\bigl(\xi_k\zeta_k^2 - \sqrt{c_k\xi_k}\,\zeta_k u\bigr)
= a u^2 + \sum_k\bigl(c_k u^2 - 2\sqrt{c_k\xi_k}\,u\zeta_k + \xi_k\zeta_k^2\bigr).$$
Since $c_k, \xi_k \ge 0$: $\sqrt{c_k\xi_k} = \sqrt{c_k}\sqrt{\xi_k}$, $c_k = (\sqrt{c_k})^2$,
$\xi_k = (\sqrt{\xi_k})^2$, so each bracket is $(\sqrt{c_k}\,u - \sqrt{\xi_k}\,\zeta_k)^2$. Hence
$u y - \dot W = a u^2 + \sum_k(\sqrt{c_k}\,u - \sqrt{\xi_k}\,\zeta_k)^2 \ge 0$ a.e., using $a \ge 0$ for the
sign. $\square$ (Integrating as in Step 2 then gives $\int_0^T u y \ge -\tfrac12\sum_k\zeta_k(0)^2$ for the
Z-form too; the Statement does not ask for it.)

## 4. Constant coefficients: the transfer functions (uses: $r_j, c_k, \xi_k \ge 0$)

With constant coefficients and zero initial state, Laplace transform each scalar equation
($\operatorname{Re}s > 0$, where every pole $-p_j \le 0$, $-\xi_k \le 0$ lies to the left):

- Y: $\hat\zeta_j = \frac{\sqrt{r_j}}{s + p_j}\hat u$, $\hat y = \sum_j\sqrt{r_j}\hat\zeta_j = \sum_j\frac{r_j}{s+p_j}\hat u$.
- Z: $\hat\zeta_k = \frac{\sqrt{c_k\xi_k}}{s+\xi_k}\hat u$,
  $\hat y = \bigl(a + \sum_k\bigl(c_k - \frac{c_k\xi_k}{s+\xi_k}\bigr)\bigr)\hat u = \bigl(a + \sum_k\frac{c_k s}{s+\xi_k}\bigr)\hat u$,
  using $\sqrt{c_k\xi_k}^2 = c_k\xi_k$. (For $\xi_k = 0$ the term is $c_k s/s = c_k$, a constant
  feedthrough, matching the decoupled state; the identity is read as rational functions.)

Both are checked symbolically by sympy in the probe. $\square$

## Numeric checks (remark; `triage/MD_0070_s1_probe.py`, seed 70)

400 random piecewise-constant histories per form (1 to 4 modes, 1 to 11 pieces on $[0,1]$, coefficients
zero with probability 0.2 to 0.5 per piece to hit the boundary $p = r = \xi = c = a = 0$, input piecewise
constant), states solved in closed form per piece, integrals by adaptive quadrature: worst relative
energy-balance residual $5.9\mathrm e{-15}$ (Y) and $1.1\mathrm e{-15}$ (Z); worst pointwise relative residual
$1.5\mathrm e{-14}$ (Y), $1.2\mathrm e{-14}$ (Z); minimum of $\int u y + \tfrac12|\zeta(0)|^2$ over the Y draws
$1.3\mathrm e{-2} > 0$; minimum pointwise $u y - \dot W$ over the Z draws $0$ (attained on pieces with
$u = 0$ or a zero coefficient). Sharp case $\int u y = -1/2$ exactly.

## Formalization blockers

- **The reading (R) must be in the frozen Lean type.** A hypothesis of the form "`∀ᵐ t, HasDerivAt ζ (rhs t) t`"
  alone is the weak reading, under which the Y integral clause is false (Section 0 witness). The type must
  add absolute continuity, or state the state equation in integral form
  `∀ t ∈ Icc 0 T, ζ t = ζ 0 + ∫ s in 0..t, rhs s` with `IntervalIntegrable rhs`. The integral form is the
  easier one in Mathlib. (I have not opened the S4 Lean file; the lead should check its hypothesis against
  this.)
- **FTC for $\tfrac12\zeta^2$ with an a.e. derivative.** Mathlib's `intervalIntegral.integral_eq_sub_of_hasDerivAt`
  family wants a derivative at every interior point. From the integral form, two routes: (i) prove
  $\zeta(T)^2 - \zeta(0)^2 = 2\int_0^T\zeta F$ from $\zeta(t) = \zeta(0) + \int_0^t F$: the left side is
  $2\zeta(0)\int_0^T F + (\int_0^T F)^2$, the right side is $2\zeta(0)\int_0^T F + 2\int_0^T\int_0^t F(s)F(t)\,ds\,dt$,
  and the double integral over the triangle is half the integral over the square by symmetry (Fubini), which needs only `MeasureTheory.integral_prod`
  and avoids derivatives; or (ii) Lebesgue differentiation of the indefinite integral a.e. plus Mathlib's
  absolutely-continuous FTC, if present in the pinned Mathlib (search `AbsolutelyContinuousOnInterval`
  before relying on it). Route (i) is self-contained.
- **Square roots.** `Real.sqrt_mul` (needs `0 ≤ c`), `Real.mul_self_sqrt`, `Real.sq_sqrt`. Available.
- **Transfer functions.** Pure field algebra in `s` (`field_simp; ring` with `s + p ≠ 0`); the Laplace
  transform itself need not be formalized if the clause is stated as the rational-function identity
  $C(sI - A)^{-1}B + D = \dots$ for diagonal $A$.
- No missing theorem is needed; the only hard part is the measure-theoretic bookkeeping of Step 2.
