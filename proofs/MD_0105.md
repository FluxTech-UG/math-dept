# MD_0105: commuting holonomies give polynomial growth of the reachable set
<!-- role: record | status: current 2026-10-25 | entry: MD_0105 -->

Informal proof attempt (S3). An attempt record, not a status. All three parts of the Statement are proved as written. No hypothesis is found missing. "$C$ nonempty" in H1 is automatic (it contains $c_0$) and "$C$ finite" is used only so that $R_k(c_0)$ is a finite set whose size makes sense; neither bound uses $N$. H2 is used only in (a), H3 is the definition of $R_k$ (Section 5).

## 0. Conventions

- A word of length $k$ is $w = (u_1, \dots, u_k) \in U^k$ (H3: one letter per turn, exactly $k$ turns). Its action on $c_0$ is $w \cdot c_0 = M_{u_k} \circ \cdots \circ M_{u_1}(c_0)$, $u_1$ applied first. Define $\Phi_k : U^k \to C$, $\Phi_k(w) = w \cdot c_0$. By definition $R_k(c_0) = \Phi_k(U^k)$, the image of $\Phi_k$. For $k = 0$ the only word is the empty one and $R_0(c_0) = \{c_0\}$.
- $n = |U| \ge 1$ (H1). $\mathrm{Sym}^k(U)$ is the set of multisets of size $k$ with elements in $U$; $\mu : U^k \to \mathrm{Sym}^k(U)$ sends a word to the multiset of its letters. $\mu$ is surjective (list any multiset in some order).
- The stars-and-bars count: $|\mathrm{Sym}^k(U)| = \binom{n + k - 1}{k} = \binom{k + n - 1}{n - 1}$. The second equality is $\binom{m}{j} = \binom{m}{m-j}$ with $m = k + n - 1$, $j = k$, $m - j = n - 1 \ge 0$; it uses $n \ge 1$ (H1). For $n = 0$ the subtraction $n - 1$ would be truncated and the formula would fail at $k = 0$.

## 1. Lemma (swap invariance)

**Lemma 1.** Assume H2. If the word $w'$ is a permutation of the word $w$ (same letters with the same multiplicities, in a different order), then $w' \cdot c = w \cdot c$ for every $c \in C$.

*Proof.* Every permutation of $\{1, \dots, k\}$ is a product of adjacent transpositions, so it suffices to treat $w' = (u_1, \dots, u_{i+1}, u_i, \dots, u_k)$, the swap of positions $i$ and $i+1$. Write $c' = M_{u_{i-1}} \circ \cdots \circ M_{u_1}(c)$ for the state before position $i$. The two words agree up to position $i - 1$, so both reach $c'$. At positions $i, i+1$ the first word produces $M_{u_{i+1}}(M_{u_i}(c'))$ and the second $M_{u_i}(M_{u_{i+1}}(c'))$; these are equal by H2 applied to $u = u_{i+1}$, $v = u_i$. From position $i + 2$ on the two words agree letter by letter, so they apply the same maps to the same state and end at the same state. $\square$

(Only commutation of pairs of letters that occur in the word is used. This is the form `List.Perm.foldl_eq'` takes in Lean core.)

## 2. Part (a)

Assume H2. By Lemma 1, $\Phi_k(w)$ depends on $w$ only through $\mu(w)$: if $\mu(w) = \mu(w')$ then $w'$ is a permutation of $w$, so $\Phi_k(w) = \Phi_k(w')$. Hence there is a map $\bar\Phi_k : \mathrm{Sym}^k(U) \to C$ with $\Phi_k = \bar\Phi_k \circ \mu$ (for a multiset $s$, pick any word $w$ with $\mu(w) = s$, which exists by surjectivity of $\mu$, and set $\bar\Phi_k(s) = \Phi_k(w)$; this is well defined by the previous sentence). Therefore
$$R_k(c_0) = \Phi_k(U^k) = \bar\Phi_k(\mu(U^k)) \subseteq \bar\Phi_k(\mathrm{Sym}^k(U)),$$
and the image of a finite set under a map has at most as many elements as the set, so
$$|R_k(c_0)| \le |\mathrm{Sym}^k(U)| = \binom{k + n - 1}{n - 1}$$
by the stars-and-bars count of Section 0 (H1, $n \ge 1$). The right side is $\frac{(k+1)(k+2)\cdots(k+n-1)}{(n-1)!}$, a polynomial in $k$ of degree $n - 1$ with no dependence on $N$. $\square$

## 3. Part (b)

No assumption on the maps. $R_k(c_0) = \Phi_k(U^k)$ is the image of a set with $|U^k| = n^k$ elements, so $|R_k(c_0)| \le n^k$. $\square$

## 4. Part (c): a witness

Fix $U$ finite nonempty and $k \ge 0$. Let $C$ be the set of words over $U$ of length at most $k$, $C = \bigcup_{j=0}^{k} U^j$, finite with $N = \sum_{j \le k} n^j \ge n^k$. Let $c_0 = ()$, the empty word, and for $u \in U$ let
$$M_u(x) = \begin{cases} x u & \text{if } |x| < k \quad (\text{append } u \text{ at the end}), \\ x & \text{if } |x| = k .\end{cases}$$
$M_u$ maps $C$ into $C$ (appending raises the length by one, to at most $k$).

**Claim.** For a word $x \in C$ and a word $y = (y_1, \dots, y_m)$ with $|x| + m \le k$, applying $M_{y_1}$ first, then $M_{y_2}$, through $M_{y_m}$ to $x$ gives the concatenation $x y$.

*Proof.* Induction on $m$. $m = 0$: nothing is applied and $x y = x$. Step: $|x| < |x| + m \le k$, so $M_{y_1}(x) = x y_1$, which has length $|x| + 1$ with $(|x| + 1) + (m - 1) \le k$; by induction the remaining letters give $(x y_1)(y_2 \cdots y_m) = x y$. $\square$

With $x = c_0$ and $m = k$ the Claim gives $\Phi_k(w) = w$ (the word $w$ itself, as an element of $C$). So $\Phi_k$ is injective and $|R_k(c_0)| = |U^k| = n^k$. $\square$

For a version with $C$ in a fixed small universe (the Lean form), replace $U$ by $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ through a bijection $e : U \to \{0, \dots, n-1\}$ and let $M_u$ append $e(u)$; $\Phi_k(w) = e \circ w$, still injective since $e$ is.

*Why the witness does not commute.* By (a), for $n \ge 2$ and $k \ge 2$ we have $\binom{k + n - 1}{n - 1} < n^k$ (the word $(u, v)$ and $(v, u)$ with $u \ne v$ have the same multiset), so maps attaining $n^k$ cannot all commute. Directly: for $k \ge 2$ and $u \ne v$, $M_v M_u(()) = (u, v) \ne (v, u) = M_u M_v(())$.

## 5. Remark: (a) is sharp (outside the Statement)

Let $C = \{0, 1, \dots, k\}^U$ (a count per letter), $c_0 = 0$, and $M_u(x) = x + e_u$ capped at $k$ in coordinate $u$: $M_u(x)_u = \min(x_u + 1, k)$, $M_u(x)_v = x_v$ for $v \ne u$. Distinct $u \ne v$ act on different coordinates, so $M_u M_v = M_v M_u$; $M_u$ commutes with itself trivially. A word $w$ of length $k$ has each letter count $\le k$, so no cap is ever hit and $\Phi_k(w)$ is the letter-count vector of $w$, which determines $\mu(w)$ and is determined by it. So $\bar\Phi_k$ is injective and $|R_k(c_0)| = |\mathrm{Sym}^k(U)| = \binom{k + n - 1}{n - 1}$: (a) is attained for every $k$ and every $n$ (with $N = (k+1)^n$). A cap on the total instead ($C = \{x \in \mathbb{N}^U : \sum_v x_v \le K\}$, $M_u$ adds $e_u$ when the total is below $K$ and fixes $x$ otherwise) does not commute: at a state with total $K - 1$, $M_u M_v(x) = x + e_v \ne x + e_u = M_v M_u(x)$ for $u \ne v$. The S1 search found the same: under commutation the largest $|R_k|$ is $\min(N, \binom{k+n-1}{n-1})$.

Finding for the lead: none of the hypotheses is missing. "$C$ nonempty" is redundant. "$U$ nonempty" is not needed for the truth of (a) over $\mathbb{N}$ either (with $n = 0$, $R_0 = \{c_0\}$ has size $1 = \binom{0}{0}$ in truncated arithmetic and $R_k = \emptyset$ for $k \ge 1$), but it keeps the binomial the ordinary one, as the entry says; the Lean statement keeps `[Nonempty U]` because the entry stipulates it.

## Formalization blockers

- **What Mathlib has.** `Sym U k` with `Sym.card_sym_eq_choose` ($|\mathrm{Sym}^k(U)| = \binom{n+k-1}{k}$) and `Nat.choose_symm_add` for the glue to $\binom{k+n-1}{n-1}$; `Finset.card_image_le` and `Finset.card_le_card` for both bounds; `Fintype.card_fun`, `Fintype.card_fin` for $|U^k| = n^k$; `List.ofFn_injective` for (c); `List.finite_length_le` gives finiteness of the length-bounded words; core `List.Perm.foldl_eq'` is Lemma 1 for a left fold with the commutation hypothesis stated on the list's members.
- **What is missing.** A lemma that a word in pairwise-commuting maps depends only on the multiset of its letters (S0 found none); `List.Perm.foldl_eq'` covers it in one line once words are lists via `List.ofFn`. The factorisation through `Sym` is avoided by mapping each multiset $s$ to the fold over `s.val.toList` (a list with multiset $s$, so a permutation of any word with multiset $s$, `Multiset.coe_toList` and `Quotient.exact`/`Multiset.coe_eq_coe`).
- **Formalizable today.** All three parts, with no `native_decide`. The only care point is universes: (c) quantifies $C$ in `Type`, so the witness uses words over `Fin n` through `Fintype.equivFin U`, not words over $U$.
