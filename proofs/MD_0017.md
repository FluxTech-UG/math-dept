# MD_0017: the static gain, with a bounded witness pair
<!-- role: record | status: current 2026-09-16 | entry: MD_0017 -->

MD_0017 is MD_0014 with the slab replaced by the square duct, so that both witness domains are
bounded and Lipschitz in $\mathbb{R}^2$ as the hypotheses require. Sections 1 to 3 of
`proofs/MD_0014.md`, which prove the expansion, the exact $O(\omega)$ cancellation and
$\langle\Psi\rangle = \langle\Phi_v\Phi_\kappa\rangle$, carry over verbatim: none of them mentions
the slab. This file derives the square's two moments, which MD_0014 does not, and assembles the new
witness.

## 1. What is inherited

From `proofs/MD_0014.md`:

$$ g_{v\kappa}(\omega) = -\frac{\omega^2}{\nu\alpha}\langle\Psi\rangle + O(\omega^3), \qquad
\langle\Psi\rangle = \langle\Phi_v\Phi_\kappa\rangle = \langle\Phi^2\rangle, \qquad
\chi(\Omega) := \frac{\langle\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Phi\rangle^2} > 1, \tag{1} $$

together with the disc's exact moments $\langle\Phi\rangle = R^2/8$ and
$\langle\Phi^2\rangle = R^4/48$, hence $\chi(\text{disc}) = 4/3$, and the modal identities
$\langle\Phi\rangle = \sum_n w_n/\mu_n$, $\langle\Phi^2\rangle = \sum_n w_n/\mu_n^2$ with
$w_n = c_n^2/|\Omega|$, $c_n = \int_\Omega e_n$.

## 2. The square duct's Dirichlet data

Let $Q_a = (-a,a)^2$. The Dirichlet eigenbasis of $(-a,a)$ is
$\{a^{-1/2}\cos(m\pi x/2a)\}_{m \text{ odd}} \cup \{a^{-1/2}\sin(k\pi x/a)\}_{k\ge1}$, and
$Q_a$'s is the set of products. **Only the odd-cosine products contribute to any moment**, because
$c_n = \int_{Q_a} e_n$ factorizes and every sine factor is odd about its axis, so its integral over
$(-a,a)$ vanishes. Restrict to odd $m, n \ge 1$ and write

$$ e_{mn}(x,y) = \frac{1}{a}\cos\frac{m\pi x}{2a}\cos\frac{n\pi y}{2a},
\qquad \mu_{mn} = \Big(\frac{\pi}{2a}\Big)^2\big(m^2+n^2\big). \tag{2} $$

Normalization: $\int_{-a}^{a}\cos^2(m\pi x/2a)\,dx = a$ for odd $m$, since
$\int_{-a}^{a}\cos(m\pi x/a)\,dx = 0$; so $\|e_{mn}\|_{L^2}^2 = a^{-2}\cdot a\cdot a = 1$.

Means: $\int_{-a}^{a}\cos(m\pi x/2a)\,dx = \tfrac{4a}{m\pi}\sin\tfrac{m\pi}{2} = \pm\tfrac{4a}{m\pi}$
for odd $m$, so

$$ c_{mn} = \frac{1}{a}\cdot\frac{4a}{m\pi}\cdot\frac{4a}{n\pi}\cdot(\pm1) = \pm\frac{16a}{mn\pi^2},
\qquad w_{mn} = \frac{c_{mn}^2}{|Q_a|} = \frac{256a^2}{m^2n^2\pi^4}\cdot\frac{1}{4a^2}
= \frac{64}{m^2n^2\pi^4}. \tag{3} $$

Note $w_{mn}$ does not depend on $a$, which it must not: it is a squared normalized mean.

Parseval check, which also confirms that the discarded sine modes carry nothing:

$$ \sum_{m,n \text{ odd}} w_{mn} = \frac{64}{\pi^4}\Big(\sum_{m \text{ odd}}\frac{1}{m^2}\Big)^2
= \frac{64}{\pi^4}\Big(\frac{\pi^2}{8}\Big)^2 = 1. \tag{4} $$

Substituting (2) and (3) into the modal identities,

$$ \langle\Phi\rangle_{Q_a} = \sum \frac{w_{mn}}{\mu_{mn}}
= \frac{256\,a^2}{\pi^6}\sum_{m,n \text{ odd}}\frac{1}{m^2n^2(m^2+n^2)}, \tag{5} $$

$$ \langle\Psi\rangle_{Q_a} = \sum \frac{w_{mn}}{\mu_{mn}^2}
= \frac{1024\,a^4}{\pi^8}\sum_{m,n \text{ odd}}\frac{1}{m^2n^2(m^2+n^2)^2}. \tag{6} $$

Both converge absolutely: the summands are $O(m^{-2}n^{-2}(m^2+n^2)^{-1})$ and
$O(m^{-2}n^{-2}(m^2+n^2)^{-2})$ against a doubly summable $m^{-2}n^{-2}$.

Numerically, summing over odd indices to $m, n < M$ and increasing $M$:

| $M$ | $\langle\Phi\rangle/a^2$ | $\langle\Psi\rangle/a^4$ | $\chi$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 201 | 0.1405770013 | 0.027240168395 | 1.3784189595 |
| 801 | 0.1405770147 | 0.027240168395 | 1.3784186960 |
| 2001 | 0.1405770149 | 0.027240168395 | 1.3784186921 |
| 4001 | 0.1405770150 | 0.027240168395 | 1.3784186919 |

so $\langle\Phi\rangle_{Q_a} = 0.140577015\,a^2$ and $\langle\Psi\rangle_{Q_a} = 0.0272401684\,a^4$,
and $\chi(Q_a) = 1.3784187$. (6) is already stable to twelve significant digits at $M = 201$;
(5), whose terms decay one power more slowly, is stable to seven there (relative error
$9.7\times10^{-8}$).

## 3. An independent derivation of $\langle\Phi\rangle_{Q_a}$

The double sum is not the only route, and a second one is worth having because the whole witness
turns on $\langle\Phi\rangle$ being exactly matched. Solve the Poisson problem by separating in one
direction only. Write $\Phi = \tfrac12(a^2-y^2) + W$ with $\Delta W = 0$, $W = 0$ on $y = \pm a$ and
$W = -\tfrac12(a^2-y^2)$ on $x = \pm a$. Expanding in the $y$-Dirichlet cosine basis, with
$k_m := m\pi/2a$,

$$ \int_{-a}^{a}\big(a^2-y^2\big)\cos(k_m y)\,dy = \frac{4(-1)^{(m-1)/2}}{k_m^3}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\tfrac12\big(a^2-y^2\big) = \sum_{m \text{ odd}} b_m \cos(k_m y), \quad
b_m = \frac{16a^2(-1)^{(m-1)/2}}{m^3\pi^3}, \tag{7} $$

the integral by two integrations by parts using $\cos(m\pi/2) = 0$ and
$\sin(m\pi/2) = (-1)^{(m-1)/2}$ for odd $m$. Hence

$$ \Phi = \sum_{m \text{ odd}} b_m \cos(k_m y)\left[1 - \frac{\cosh(k_m x)}{\cosh(m\pi/2)}\right]. \tag{8} $$

Averaging, with $\int_{-a}^{a}\cos(k_m y)\,dy = (-1)^{(m-1)/2}\,4a/(m\pi)$ and
$\int_{-a}^{a}[1 - \cosh(k_m x)/\cosh(m\pi/2)]\,dx = 2a - (4a/m\pi)\tanh(m\pi/2)$, the two signs
squaring to $+1$,

$$ \langle\Phi\rangle_{Q_a} = \frac{1}{4a^2}\sum_{m \text{ odd}}\frac{64a^3}{m^4\pi^4}
\left[2a - \frac{4a}{m\pi}\tanh\frac{m\pi}{2}\right]
= \frac{32a^2}{\pi^4}\sum_{m \text{ odd}}\frac{1}{m^4} - \frac{64a^2}{\pi^5}\sum_{m \text{ odd}}\frac{\tanh(m\pi/2)}{m^5}, $$

and with $\sum_{m \text{ odd}} m^{-4} = \pi^4/96$,

$$ \boxed{\ \langle\Phi\rangle_{Q_a} = \frac{a^2}{3} - \frac{64\,a^2}{\pi^5}\sum_{m \text{ odd}}\frac{\tanh(m\pi/2)}{m^5}. \ } \tag{9} $$

This series converges like $M^{-4}$, against the double sum's $M^{-3}$: $\tanh(m\pi/2) \to 1$, so the
terms are $O(m^{-5})$ and only the deviation $1 - \tanh(m\pi/2)$ is geometric. It gives
$0.140577014955\,a^2$, agreeing with (5) to thirteen significant digits. Two independent representations, one double
sum and one single sum, land on the same number.

## 4. The witness

Take $R^2 = 8\langle\Phi\rangle_{Q_a} = 1.12461612\,a^2$. Then by MD_0014 (8),

$$ \langle\Phi\rangle_{\text{disc}} = \frac{R^2}{8} = \langle\Phi\rangle_{Q_a}, \tag{10} $$

so the two domains feed any putative function of $(\langle\Phi_v\rangle, \langle\Phi_\kappa\rangle)$
the identical argument pair, H1 making the two potentials equal on each. But by MD_0014 (9),

$$ \frac{\langle\Psi\rangle_{\text{disc}}}{\langle\Psi\rangle_{Q_a}}
= \frac{R^4/48}{\chi(Q_a)\langle\Phi\rangle_{Q_a}^2}
= \frac{64\langle\Phi\rangle_{Q_a}^2/48}{\chi(Q_a)\langle\Phi\rangle_{Q_a}^2}
= \frac{4/3}{\chi(Q_a)} = \frac{1.333333}{1.3784187} = 0.9672920, \tag{11} $$

that is, the two $\langle\Psi\rangle$ differ by the factor $1.033814$, or $3.38$ percent. No function
$\Xi$ can satisfy $\langle\Psi\rangle = \Xi(\langle\Phi_v\rangle,\langle\Phi_\kappa\rangle)$ for both.
MD_0017 is proved, with both witness domains bounded and Lipschitz in $\mathbb{R}^2$: the open disc
is smooth, the open square is Lipschitz although its boundary has four corners, and Lipschitz is all
H2 asks.

**The cost of the repair.** MD_0014's slab pair separates by $11.1$ percent and this one by $3.38$
percent, so the bounded witness is about three times weaker. It is still a separation by a factor
that no arithmetic can close, and it is inside the hypotheses, which the stronger one is not.

**What is NOT proved, here or in MD_0014.** Only $\chi > 1$. No lower bound above $1$ is proved for
any class of domains, and in particular the condition $\chi/\alpha_\infty \ge 6/5$ that
`triage/MD_0011_interpolant_probe.py`'s mixture needs to be solvable is an unproved assumption about
real pores, supported by a rectangle survey (`proofs/MD_0014.md` section 5) and nothing more.

## Formalization blockers

- Section 4 is formalizable once section 2 is: it is arithmetic on two real numbers plus the
  inequality $\chi(Q_a) \neq 4/3$.
- The obstruction is that $\chi(Q_a)$ is given by a convergent series with no closed form, so
  "$\chi(Q_a) \neq 4/3$" must be certified by a rigorous interval, not by a floating-point sum. That
  is reachable: bound the tail of (5) and (6) by comparison with
  $\sum_{m > M}\sum_n m^{-2}n^{-2}(m^2+n^2)^{-1} \le \tfrac{\pi^2}{8}\sum_{m>M} m^{-4}$ and similarly
  for (6), then evaluate the head exactly in $\mathbb{Q}$ and carry $\pi$ through
  `Mathlib.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.Pi.Bounds`. Tedious, entirely mechanical, and it would make
  MD_0017 the first entry in this group with a genuinely complete Lean proof of its headline clause.
  The head at $M = 21$ already separates $\chi(Q_a)$ from $4/3$ by more than the tail bound.
- The disc's $R^2/8$ and $R^4/48$ are `intervalIntegral` on polynomials, as in
  `proofs/MD_0014.md`'s blockers.
- What remains out of reach is the same missing $H^1_0(\Omega)$ and Dirichlet spectrum that blocks
  MD_0011 and MD_0014, needed to connect the modal data of section 2 to the Poisson potentials at
  all. The abstract route, taking $\{(w_n,\mu_n)\}$ as given, sidesteps it and is what should be
  formalized first.
