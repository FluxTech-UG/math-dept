# MD_0073: the left-right eigenvector budget sums exactly to the eigenvalue
<!-- role: record | status: current 2026-10-18 | entry: MD_0073 -->

Informal proof of both clauses of MD_0073. Notation and hypotheses are the entry's: $A = \sum_{e=1}^E A_e$
in $\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}$, $Av = \lambda v$, $w^*A = \lambda w^*$, $w^*v \ne 0$,
$s_e = w^*A_e v/(w^*v)$. Finite dimension is used throughout (determinants, adjugates, polynomials).

## Clause 1: the sum

Uses only $w^*v \ne 0$ (to define $s_e$); neither simplicity nor $v, w \ne 0$ beyond what
$w^*v \ne 0$ already forces.

By linearity of $X \mapsto w^*Xv$,
$$\sum_e s_e = \frac{w^*\big(\sum_e A_e\big)v}{w^*v} = \frac{w^*Av}{w^*v} = \frac{\lambda\, w^*v}{w^*v} = \lambda,$$
using $Av = \lambda v$ in the third step. (Equally $w^*A = \lambda w^*$ would do; only one of the two
eigen-relations is needed for this clause.) The degenerate split $E = 0$ is covered: then $A = 0$,
$\lambda = 0$ (since $v \ne 0$), and the empty sum is $0$.

The clause holds for non-simple $\lambda$ as well, whenever $w^*v \ne 0$ (for instance a semisimple
double eigenvalue with a non-orthogonal pair; the probe checks one exactly).

## Clause 2: the derivative, $\lambda$ algebraically simple

Fix $e$ and put $B := A_e$, $p(\varepsilon, z) := \det(zI - A - \varepsilon B)$, a polynomial in two
complex variables.

**Step 1 (simplicity gives a nonzero $z$-derivative).** $p(0,z) = \chi_A(z)$, and $\lambda$ is a simple
root, so $\partial_z p(0,\lambda) = \chi_A'(\lambda) = \prod_{\mu \ne \lambda}(\lambda - \mu) \ne 0$
(product over the other eigenvalues with algebraic multiplicity). Hypothesis used: algebraic simplicity.

**Step 2 (existence, uniqueness and analyticity of $\lambda(\varepsilon)$).** By Step 1 and the
holomorphic implicit function theorem there are $\delta, r > 0$ and an analytic
$\lambda(\cdot)$ on $|\varepsilon| < \delta$ with $\lambda(0) = \lambda$ and, for each such $\varepsilon$,
$\lambda(\varepsilon)$ the only zero of $p(\varepsilon,\cdot)$ in the disc $|z - \lambda| < r$. (Equivalently,
by Rouché: choose $r$ so that the closed disc holds no other eigenvalue of $A$; for small $|\varepsilon|$
the root count of $p(\varepsilon,\cdot)$ in the disc equals that of $\chi_A$, which is one.) So "the
unique eigenvalue of $A + \varepsilon B$ near $\lambda$" in the Statement is well defined and
differentiable, and
$$\lambda'(0) = -\frac{\partial_\varepsilon p(0,\lambda)}{\partial_z p(0,\lambda)}.$$

**Step 3 (both partials through the adjugate).** Jacobi's formula $d\det M = \operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(M)\, dM)$ with
$M = zI - A - \varepsilon B$ gives, at $(0,\lambda)$ with $K := \operatorname{adj}(\lambda I - A)$,
$$\partial_z p = \operatorname{tr} K, \qquad \partial_\varepsilon p = -\operatorname{tr}(K B).$$

**Step 4 ($K$ is a nonzero multiple of $v w^*$).** Simplicity gives geometric multiplicity one, so
$\operatorname{rank}(\lambda I - A) = N - 1$; hence some $(N-1)$-minor is nonzero and $K \ne 0$. From
$(\lambda I - A)K = K(\lambda I - A) = \det(\lambda I - A) I = 0$, every column of $K$ lies in
$\ker(\lambda I - A) = \operatorname{span}(v)$ and every row of $K$ in the left kernel
$\operatorname{span}(w^*)$. (Both kernels are one-dimensional and $v, w \ne 0$ because $w^*v \ne 0$, so
$v$ and $w^*$ span them.) Hence $K = c\, v w^*$ with $c \ne 0$. For $N = 1$, $K = (1)$ and $v w^*$ is a
nonzero scalar, consistent.

**Step 5 (assemble).** $\operatorname{tr} K = c\, w^*v$ and $\operatorname{tr}(KB) = c\, w^*Bv$, so
$$\lambda'(0) = \frac{c\, w^*A_e v}{c\, w^*v} = s_e .$$
Step 1 gives $c\,w^*v = \chi_A'(\lambda) \ne 0$, which re-derives $w^*v \ne 0$ under simplicity: for a simple
eigenvalue the hypothesis of the sum clause is automatic. The value of $s_e$ is invariant under rescaling
$v$ and $w$ independently, so the choice of eigenvectors does not matter.

This is the first-order formula of Kato, *Perturbation Theory for Linear Operators*, Ch. II §2 (the
derivative of a simple eigenvalue, $\lambda'(0) = \operatorname{tr}(B P)$ with $P = v w^*/(w^*v)$ the
eigenprojection); its hypothesis, an isolated eigenvalue of algebraic multiplicity one of a matrix
family analytic in $\varepsilon$, is met since $A + \varepsilon A_e$ is affine in $\varepsilon$. The
argument above is self-contained and does not depend on the citation.

## Numeric checks

`triage/MD_0073_s1_probe.py` (seeded): exact sympy boundary cases ($E = 1$, a zero part appended, a
semisimple double eigenvalue with $w^*v = 1$) all give $\sum s_e = \lambda$; 400 random complex splittings
($N \le 12$, $E \le 5$, a quarter strongly non-normal) give worst
$|\sum_e s_e - \lambda|/\max(1,|\lambda|) = 6.4\times10^{-15}$ and worst central-difference error
$|\Delta\lambda/\Delta\varepsilon - s_e|/\max(1,|s_e|) = 3.3\times10^{-8}$ at $h = 10^{-6}$ (cases with
$|w^*v|/(\|w\|\|v\|) > 10^{-3}$; smallest seen $4.3\times10^{-2}$). No counterexample.

## Formalization blockers

- Clause 1 is a one-line `Matrix` / `dotProduct` computation (`Matrix.sum_mul`, `Matrix.mulVec`
  linearity, `Finset.sum_div`); no blocker beyond stating $w^*$ as `star w ⬝ᵥ` or `vecMul (star w)`.
- Clause 2 as stated mentions "the unique eigenvalue near $\lambda$", which needs either the holomorphic
  implicit function theorem or a root-continuity statement for polynomials in Lean. Mathlib's
  `HasStrictDerivAt.implicitFunction`-style API is for real/normed settings and would need assembling for
  a two-variable polynomial; root continuity (Rouché) is not, to my knowledge, in Mathlib. The cleanest
  formal route avoids both: state the derivative of the analytic branch via the polynomial identity
  $p(\varepsilon,\lambda(\varepsilon)) = 0$ and Steps 3 to 5, taking existence and uniqueness of the branch
  from an implicit-function lemma. The pieces Steps 3 to 5 need, Jacobi's formula for the derivative of a
  determinant and the rank-one structure of the adjugate at corank one, are the expected Mathlib gaps
  (`Matrix.adjugate`, `Matrix.mul_adjugate`, `Matrix.adjugate_mul` exist; a corank-one adjugate lemma and
  the trace-of-adjugate form of $\chi_A'$ are what I would expect to have to build). These names were not
  hovered in this stage; the S4 prover owns that check.
