# MD_0016: the corrected unit-Prandtl limit
<!-- role: record | status: current 2026-09-16 | entry: MD_0016 -->

MD_0016 is MD_0012 with the sign of the derivative term corrected and the domain class widened to
$\mathbb{R}^d$, $d \ge 1$. The proof is `proofs/MD_0012.md` unchanged except where noted here, so
this file records only what is different. $s := i\omega$; the modal data $\{(w_n,\mu_n)\}$ is
MD_0011 section 6.

## 1. Nothing in the parent proof uses $d = 2$

`proofs/MD_0012.md` section 1 (holomorphy of $F$ on $\{\operatorname{Re} D > 0\}$ by uniform
convergence), section 2 ($\operatorname{Re}(1-F) > 0$ hence $F \neq 1$), section 3 (the bracket
limit) and section 5 (the three routes to the value) rest on exactly three facts about $\Omega$:

- the Dirichlet Laplacian on $\Omega$ has discrete spectrum $0 < \mu_1 \le \mu_2 \le \cdots \to \infty$
  with an $L^2$-orthonormal eigenbasis;
- $\sum_n w_n = 1$, which is Parseval applied to the constant function $1$;
- $w_n \ge 0$, which is $w_n = c_n^2/|\Omega|$ for a real eigenbasis, and which section 2's strict
  sign argument uses;
- $\sum_n w_n/\mu_n < \infty$.

All four hold for a bounded Lipschitz domain in $\mathbb{R}^d$ for every $d \ge 1$. The spectrum is
discrete by Rellich to Kondrachov compactness of $H^1_0(\Omega) \hookrightarrow L^2(\Omega)$ and the
spectral theorem for the compact self-adjoint resolvent; the last is cheaper than that and needs only
Poincare, since $\mu_n \ge \mu_1 > 0$ gives $\sum_n w_n/\mu_n \le 1/\mu_1 < \infty$ from
$\sum_n w_n = 1$ alone (it equals $\langle\Phi\rangle$, but that identity is not what makes it
finite). The dimension appears nowhere else: no trace theorem beyond
$h_D - 1 \in H^1_0$, no boundary regularity beyond Lipschitz, no Sobolev embedding exponent. So H1
may be stated for $d \ge 1$ at no cost, and MD_0016 does.

The gain is that $d = 1$ admits **parallel plates as the bounded interval $(-y_0, y_0)$**. Under
MD_0012's H1, which imports MD_0011's bounded Lipschitz $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$, the slab
$\{|y| < y_0\} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ is unbounded and the Statement's own plates clause names a
domain its hypotheses exclude. MD_0016 removes that tension rather than living with it. The same
defect in MD_0014 is repaired the other way, by replacing the witness (MD_0017); the two repairs are
independent and either would do here.

## 2. The value, and the sign

`proofs/MD_0012.md` section 5 proves

$$ g_{v\kappa}\big|_{\mathrm{Pr}=1} \;=\; \big(1 - F(\alpha)\big) - \alpha F'(\alpha) \tag{1} $$

three independent ways: l'Hopital on MD_0011 (14); the resolvent identity
$\tfrac{d}{dD}L_D^{-1} = D^{-1}(sL_D^{-2} - L_D^{-1})$ with $L_D = s - D\Delta$; and the term-by-term
identity $\sum_n w_n[\tfrac{s}{s+\alpha\mu_n} - \tfrac{s\alpha\mu_n}{(s+\alpha\mu_n)^2}]
= \sum_n \tfrac{w_n s^2}{(s+\alpha\mu_n)^2}$, using $s\alpha\mu_n = s(s+\alpha\mu_n) - s^2$. The
right side of that third identity is MD_0011 (17) at $\nu = \alpha$, which contains neither
$1 - \mathrm{Pr}$ nor a derivative and therefore cannot inherit a sign convention from either.

Consistency with the bracket, which is the clause MD_0012 got right and which MD_0016 restates:

$$ 1 - \frac{g_{v\kappa}|_{\mathrm{Pr}=1}}{1-F(\alpha)}
= 1 - \frac{(1-F(\alpha)) - \alpha F'(\alpha)}{1-F(\alpha)} = \frac{\alpha F'(\alpha)}{1-F(\alpha)}
= \lim_{\mathrm{Pr}\to1}\frac{f_\kappa - f_v}{(1-f_v)(1-\mathrm{Pr})}. \tag{2} $$

With the printed $+\alpha F'(\alpha)$ the same computation returns $-\alpha F'(\alpha)/(1-F(\alpha))$,
so the two clauses force $\alpha F'(\alpha) = 0$; and
$\alpha F'(\alpha) = \sum_n w_n s\alpha\mu_n/(s+\alpha\mu_n)^2$ has every term carrying
$w_n > 0$ of argument $\pi/2 - 2\arctan(\omega/\alpha\mu_n) \in (-\pi/2, \pi/2)$, and at least one
such term exists because $\sum_n w_n = 1$; the terms with $w_n = 0$ contribute nothing. Hence
$\operatorname{Re}(\alpha F'(\alpha)) > 0$ strictly and never zero. That is the refutation, certified
at the disc of radius 1 and the plates of half-gap 1, both at $\alpha = 1$, $\omega = 3$, by
`counterexamples/MD_0012_sign_of_derivative_term.py`.

## 3. The two closed forms

Both follow from $dF/d\log D = -\tfrac{z}{2}F'(z)$ with $z = L\sqrt{s/D}$, since
$\log z = \text{const} - \tfrac12\log D$, substituted into (1) as
$g_{v\kappa}|_{\mathrm{Pr}=1} = 1 - F - dF/d\log D$.

**Plates**, $L = y_0$, $F = \tanh z/z$, $F'(z) = (\operatorname{sech}^2 z - F)/z$:

$$ \frac{dF}{d\log D} = -\frac{z}{2}\cdot\frac{\operatorname{sech}^2 z - F}{z} = \frac{F - \operatorname{sech}^2 z}{2},
\qquad g_{v\kappa}\big|_{\mathrm{Pr}=1} = 1 - F - \tfrac12\big(F - \operatorname{sech}^2 z\big). \tag{3} $$

**Disc**, $L = R$, $F = 2I_1(z)/(zI_0(z))$. From $I_1' = I_0 - I_1/z$, $I_0' = I_1$ and
$I_1/I_0 = zF/2$,

$$ F'(z) = \frac{2}{z}\Big(1 - F - \tfrac14 z^2F^2\Big), \qquad
\frac{dF}{d\log D} = -\Big(1 - F - \tfrac14 z^2 F^2\Big),
\qquad g_{v\kappa}\big|_{\mathrm{Pr}=1} = 2(1-F) - \tfrac14 z^2F^2. \tag{4} $$

Both are certified against the modal sum by `triage/MD_0011_interpolant_probe.py` test T1 at
$\mathrm{Pr} = 1.0$, which is exactly the $\mathrm{Pr} = 1$ branch of its reference functions: the
tolerance the test enforces is $10^{-9}$ and the observed worst difference is $1.8\times10^{-10}$.
The counterexample script's own convergence guard certifies them to $10^{-9}$.

## Formalization blockers

Identical to `proofs/MD_0012.md`'s, with one simplification and one addition.

- Simplification: widening to $d \ge 1$ makes the plates clause a statement about an interval, so
  (3) is formalizable the moment the abstract setting exists, with `Complex.tanh` supplying
  everything. The disc clause (4) still waits on modified Bessel functions, absent from Mathlib.
- The cheapest honest S4 remains the abstract one: for $F$ holomorphic near $\alpha > 0$ with
  $F(\alpha) \neq 1$, prove that $\mathrm{Pr}\mapsto((1-F(\alpha)) - \mathrm{Pr}(1-F(\mathrm{Pr}\alpha)))/(1-\mathrm{Pr})$
  extends holomorphically across $1$ with value $1 - F(\alpha) - \alpha F'(\alpha)$, and that the
  associated bracket has value $\alpha F'(\alpha)/(1-F(\alpha))$. That is `deriv`, `HasDerivAt.div`
  and `Complex.differentiableAt`, with no PDE, and it captures the whole of the corrected clause and
  its consistency with the bracket. What it does not capture is that $F$ is the average of the
  channel function, which is section 1 and needs the missing $H^1_0(\Omega)$.
- Addition: section 1's "nothing uses $d = 2$" is a claim about the parent proof, not a theorem.
  Formalizing the abstract version makes it vacuous, because the abstract version never mentions a
  domain at all. That is an argument for doing the abstract version first.
