S3 attempt, 2026-10-17 (claude-opus-5-5, prover). Outcome: **claim (b) looks false as stated.**
This file proves (a), the frozen-grid half of (c), and the hinted cancellation: on the
fixed-decision set the controller term equals $\sum_n r_n\,dh_n/d\theta$ with $\|r_n\| \le C_1 H^p$
and $C_1$ traced. Claim (b) then follows from one more estimate,
$\sum_n \|dh_n/d\theta\| \le C_2$ uniformly in $\tau$ and $U$. That estimate does not follow from the
hypotheses. Section 6 gives a controller satisfying every hypothesis for which
$\|R\|/H$ grows like $10^{100}$ per decade of $\tau$ (numeric, 40 to 200 digits, checked by central
differences). The entry is therefore not settled by this file. Section 7 names the missing
hypothesis.

## 0. Reading of the statement

- "(S)" and "H0 as in C1(ii)" are not defined in the entry. (S) is read as the standing setting
  H1. H0 is defined here (Lemma 0) as a step bound under which the numerical solution, all RK
  stages and the variational solutions stay in a compact set where the derivative bounds hold.
  This is the role C1(ii) plays; nothing below depends on C1's text.
- $K_\delta$ depends on $\theta$; "derivative bounds of $f$ and $E$ on $K_\delta$" is read as
  bounds on $\{(y,\theta): \theta \in U, y \in K_\delta(\theta)\}$ uniform over the $\theta$
  considered. A bound that varies with $\theta$ makes $C'$ vary the same way, no more.
- Constants. $B$ bounds the derivatives of order $\le p+1$ of $f$ and of order $\le p+1$ of $E$
  on that set; $L = \sup\|f_y\|$, $F_0 = \sup \|f\|$, $F_\theta = \sup\|f_\theta\|$;
  $\beta = \sum_{i,j}|a_{ij}| + \sum_i|b_i|$ for the tableau; $Y_0 = \sup_U\|\partial_\theta y_0\|$.

## 1. Lemmas on one step

**Lemma 1 (local error with $C^p$ field).** Let $F$ be $C^p$ on a convex open set with derivatives
of order $\le p$ bounded by $B_F$, $\varphi_h$ its flow, $\Psi_h$ an explicit RK step of classical
order $p$ (H1), and $l_h(z) = \Psi_h(z) - \varphi_h(z)$. For $h \le H_0$ (stages in the set),
$\|l_h(z)\| \le C_l h^{p+1}$ and $\|\partial_h l_h(z)\| \le C_l h^p$, $C_l$ depending only on $p$,
$\beta$, $B_F$.

*Proof.* Stages $k_i(h) = F(z + h\sum_{j<i} a_{ij}k_j(h))$ are $C^p$ in $h$ by induction on $i$
(A strictly lower triangular, H1), and $\Psi_h = z + h\sum b_ik_i(h)$, so
$\Psi^{(p)}(s) = p\sum b_i k_i^{(p-1)}(s) + s\sum b_i k_i^{(p)}(s)$. Since $k_i^{(p)}$ involves
derivatives of $F$ of order $\le p$ only, $\|\Psi^{(p)}(s) - \Psi^{(p)}(0)\| \le C s$. The flow is
$C^{p+1}$ in $h$ with $\varphi^{(p+1)}$ an elementary-differential expression in $F$ of order $\le p$,
so $\|\varphi^{(p)}(s) - \varphi^{(p)}(0)\| \le Cs$. Classical order $p$ is
$l^{(j)}(0) = 0$ for $j \le p$, hence $\|l^{(p)}(s)\| \le Cs$ and the integral Taylor remainder
$l(h) = \int_0^h \frac{(h-s)^{p-1}}{(p-1)!} l^{(p)}(s)\,ds$ gives both bounds (for $p=1$ the second
is $l' = l^{(1)}$ directly). $\square$

The point of Lemma 1 is that $C^p$, not $C^{p+1}$, suffices. It is applied to three fields, each of
which is $C^p$ because $f$ is $C^{p+1}$ (H1):

1. $F = f(\cdot,\theta)$: gives $l_h$, $\partial_h l_h$ for the step itself.
2. $F(z, Z) = (f(z), f_y(z) Z)$ on $K_\delta \times \{\|Z\| \le 2e^{LT}\}$: an explicit RK step
   commutes with differentiation in the initial value (differentiate the stage equations), so its
   step is $(\Psi_h(z), D_z\Psi_h(z)Z)$, and the flow is $(\varphi_h(z), D\varphi_h(z)Z)$. Hence
   $\|D_z l_h(z)\| \le C_l h^{p+1}$.
3. $F(z, Y) = (f(z), f_y(z)Y + f_\theta(z))$ on $K_\delta \times \{\|Y\| \le S_{\max}+1\}$,
   $S_{\max} = (Y_0 + TF_\theta)e^{LT}$ (Gronwall bound on $\|S\|$, uses the $C^1$ norm of $y_0$):
   the same commutation shows that the frozen-grid derivative $\partial_\theta y_N(\theta; h)$ is the
   $Y$-component of this augmented RK solve started at $(y_0, \partial_\theta y_0)$, and the exact
   flow's $Y$-component is $S$.

Below $C_l$ is the largest of the three constants.

**Lemma 0 (stages stay in $K_\delta$; definition of $H_0$).** Let $L_a$ be a Lipschitz constant of
field 3. By Lady Windermere's fan with Lemma 1, as long as the solve stays in the set, the global
error of fields 1 and 3 on any grid is at most $C_g H^p$ with $C_g = C_l T e^{L_a T}$. Choose $H_0$
with $C_g H_0^p \le \min(\delta/2, 1)$ and $\beta H_0 (F_0 + 1) \le \delta/2$. Induction over $n$
then keeps $y_n$ within $\delta/2$ of $y(t_n)$, every stage within $\delta$, and $Y_n$ within $1$ of
$S(t_n)$, for every grid with $H \le H_0$. $H_0$ depends only on $B$, $T$, $\beta$, $\delta$, $Y_0$.

**Lemma 2 (one-step bounds).** For $h \le H_0$ and $z = y_n$, with $G = D_z\Psi_h(z)$:

- (i) $\|G\| \le 1 + L_\Psi h$, $L_\Psi = \beta L e^{\beta L H_0}$ (stage derivatives).
- (ii) $\|G f(z) - f(\Psi_h(z))\| \le C_d h^{p+1}$, $C_d = C_l(F_0 + L)$. Proof:
  $D\varphi_h(z) f(z) = f(\varphi_h(z))$ (differentiate $\varphi_{s+h} = \varphi_h \circ \varphi_s$ at
  $s=0$), so $Gf(z) - f(\Psi_h z) = D_zl_h(z)f(z) + f(\varphi_h z) - f(\Psi_h z)$; apply field 2 to
  the first term and field 1 with Lipschitz constant $L$ to the second.
- (iii) $\|\partial_h\Psi_h(z) - f(\Psi_h(z))\| \le C_\rho h^p$, $C_\rho = C_l(1 + LH_0)$. Proof:
  $\partial_h\varphi_h = f(\varphi_h)$, so the difference is
  $\partial_h l_h + f(\varphi_h z) - f(\Psi_h z)$.

## 2. Claim (a): smoothness on the fixed-decision set

Fix $\tau$ and $U$ as in the Statement (H2). Index the trial evaluations $k = 1, \dots, K$; on $U$
the decision pattern, hence $K$, $N$, which trials are clipped and which accepted, is constant.
Induction on $k$, with the claim that the trial step $h^{(k)}$, the current node $y_{n(k)}$ and
$t_{n(k)}$ are $C^1$ on $U$:

- $k = 1$: $h^{(1)} = h_{\rm init}(\tau)$ or its clip $T$; constant in $\theta$ (Statement:
  $h_{\rm init}$ independent of $\theta$). $y_0$ is $C^1$ (H1).
- Clip. On $U$ the branch of $\min(h, T - t_n)$ is fixed by the pattern, so the trial step is
  either the propagated value or $T - t_n$, both $C^1$ by induction. A tie $h = T - t_n$ at some
  points of $U$ does no harm: the selected formula is the one the pattern fixes.
- $\varepsilon^{(k)} = \|E(y_n, h^{(k)}, \theta)\|$. $E$ is $C^{p+1}$ (Statement) and the
  Euclidean norm is smooth on $\mathbb{R}^d\setminus\{0\}$, so $\varepsilon^{(k)}$ is $C^1$ wherever
  it is positive. Positivity is needed exactly for the trials whose $\varepsilon$ feeds a later
  trial: unclipped trials have $\varepsilon \ge \kappa\tau > 0$ (Statement's $U$ condition); a
  rejected clipped trial has $\varepsilon > \tau > 0$ (accept rule); an accepted clipped trial ends
  the solve (it lands on $T$) and its $\varepsilon$ feeds nothing. Only positivity of $\kappa\tau$ is
  used here.
- Next trial $h^{(k)}\phi(\tau/\varepsilon^{(k)})$ with $\phi$ smooth (Statement): $C^1$. On
  acceptance $y_{n+1} = \Psi_{h}(y_n,\theta)$, $C^{p+1}$ in $(h, y, \theta)$ since $f$ is (H1).

Hence each accepted step $h_n(\theta)$ is $C^1$ on $U$, and
$y_N(\theta) = \mathcal{F}(\theta, h_0(\theta), \dots, h_{N-1}(\theta))$ with the frozen-grid map
$\mathcal{F}(\theta, h) = y_N(\theta; h)$ jointly $C^1$. This is (a), and the chain rule gives the
split

$$ \frac{d y_N}{d\theta} = \underbrace{\partial_\theta \mathcal{F}}_{\text{frozen}}
 + \underbrace{\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \frac{\partial y_N}{\partial h_n}\,\frac{dh_n}{d\theta}}_{R}. \tag{1} $$

Nothing in (a) uses $H \le H_0$, $\kappa$ beyond positivity, $\phi$'s monotonicity or $\rho$.

## 3. The cancellation (the S1 hint, proved)

Write $G_n = D_z\Psi_{h_n}(y_n)$ and $\Phi_{N,n} = G_{N-1}\cdots G_n$ ($\Phi_{N,N} = I$). By
Lemma 2(i) and discrete Gronwall, $\|\Phi_{N,n}\| \le e^{L_\Psi T}$. The chain rule through the
composition gives $\partial y_N/\partial h_n = \Phi_{N,n+1}\,\partial_h\Psi_{h_n}(y_n)$.

Let $v_k = \Phi_{N,k} f(y_k)$. Then $v_N = f(y_N)$ and
$v_k - v_{k+1} = \Phi_{N,k+1}\,(G_k f(y_k) - f(y_{k+1}))$, so by Lemma 2(ii)

$$ \|v_k - f(y_N)\| \le e^{L_\Psi T} C_d \sum_{j \ge k} h_j^{p+1} \le e^{L_\Psi T} C_d\, T H^p. $$

With Lemma 2(iii), $\partial y_N/\partial h_n = v_{n+1} + \Phi_{N,n+1}(\partial_h\Psi_{h_n}(y_n) - f(y_{n+1}))$,
hence

$$ \frac{\partial y_N}{\partial h_n} = f(y_N) + r_n, \qquad \|r_n\| \le C_1 H^p, \qquad
C_1 = e^{L_\Psi T}\,(C_\rho + C_d T). \tag{2} $$

On $U$ the solve stops at $t_N = T$ with $N$ fixed (H2, stop rule), so $\sum_n h_n(\theta) \equiv T$
and $\sum_n dh_n/d\theta = 0$. This holds whether or not the last step was clipped. Therefore

$$ R = f(y_N)\sum_n \frac{dh_n}{d\theta} + \sum_n r_n \frac{dh_n}{d\theta}
 = \sum_n r_n \frac{dh_n}{d\theta}, \qquad \|R\| \le C_1 H^p \sum_{n} \Big\|\frac{dh_n}{d\theta}\Big\|. \tag{3} $$

$C_1$ depends only on $B$, $T$, $\beta$, $p$, $H_0$; uses H1, Lemma 0 ($H \le H_0$), the stop rule.
It does not depend on $\tau$, $U$, $\phi$ or $\kappa$.

## 4. Claim (c), frozen half

By field 3 of Lemma 1 and Lemma 0, $\|\partial_\theta\mathcal{F} - S(T;\theta)\| \le C_g H^p$ for
every grid with $H \le H_0$, adaptive or not, $C_g = C_lTe^{L_aT}$. The full-derivative half of (c)
is this plus (b), so it stands or falls with (b).

## 5. The gap: claim (b) needs $\sum_n\|dh_n/d\theta\| \le C_2$

By (1) to (3), (b) holds with $C' = C_1C_2$ whenever $\sum_n \|dh_n/d\theta\| \le C_2$ uniformly in
$\tau \le \tau_0$ and $U$. Differentiating the controller,
$a_{k+1} = (1 - g_k q_k)\,a_k - g_k c_k$ for $a_k = d\log h^{(k)}/d\theta$, where
$g(x) = x\phi'(x)/\phi(x) \ge 0$, $x_k = \tau/\varepsilon_k$,
$q_k = E^{\!\top}\!E_h h/\varepsilon^2 \approx p+1$ and $c_k = E^{\!\top}(E_y\,dy_n + E_\theta)/\varepsilon^2$.
The multiplier $1 - g_kq_k$ is the linearization of the step-size map in $\log h$. The hypotheses
bound $g$ only through $\phi$'s smoothness and monotonicity; nothing keeps $|1 - g_kq_k| \le 1$ on
average. When the accepted-step map $z \mapsto z - (p+1)\log\phi(e^z)$ ($z = \log x$) expands along
every orbit, $a_k$ grows geometrically in the step count $N \sim \tau^{-1/(p+1)}$ and
$\sum\|dh_n/d\theta\|$ is unbounded as $\tau \to 0$. The hypothesis $\varepsilon \ge \kappa\tau$
keeps $\varepsilon$ away from zero but does not tame this linearization. A second, independent
weakness: $c_k$ and $q_k - (p+1)$ carry the ratio $h^{p+1}/\varepsilon$, which no hypothesis bounds
(the estimate may be degenerate, $\|E\| \ll h^{p+1}$, while $\varepsilon \ge \kappa\tau$ holds).

## 6. Witness that the gap is real (numeric)

Every hypothesis holds: $d = m = p = 1$, $f(y,\theta) = -\theta y$, $y_0 = 1$, $\Theta = (1/2, 3/2)$,
$\theta = 1$, $T = 2$; explicit Euler ($A = 0$, $b = 1$, order 1); $E$ = Heun minus Euler
$= \theta^2h^2y/2$, $C^\infty$, $\|E\| \le C_Eh^2$; $h_{\rm init} = \tau^{1/2}$.
$\phi(x) = \exp\psi(\log x)$ with
$\psi(z) = -\delta/2 + \alpha w\,[\mathrm{sp}((z-1)/w) - \mathrm{sp}((z-1-\delta)/w)]$,
$\mathrm{sp}(u) = \log(1+e^u)$, $\delta = 0.2$, $\alpha = 5/2$, $w = 0.002$: smooth, nondecreasing,
$\phi \in (0.905, 1.492)$, $\phi(x) \le \rho = 0.905$ for $x < 1$. The accepted-step map in
$z = \log(\tau/\varepsilon)$ has slope $1$ below $z = 1$ and slope $1 - 2\alpha = -4$ on $[1, 1.2]$;
orbits climb by $0.2$ per step and every orbit enters $[1, 1.2]$ at least once per four steps, so
$dz_n/d\theta$ grows like $4^{n/4}$ along every orbit. Measured: no rejection, unclipped
$\varepsilon/\tau \in [0.289, 0.663]$, so $\kappa = 1/4$ holds. For this example (3) is exact in
closed form: $R = -\theta^2 y_N\sum_n \frac{h_n}{1-\theta h_n}\frac{dh_n}{d\theta}$.

## 7. What a revision needs

The missing hypothesis is one that forces $\sum_n\|dh_n/d\theta\| \le C_2$. Candidates, neither
proved sufficient here:

- step-size stability: $\sup_{x} (p+1)\,x\phi'(x)/\phi(x) \le 2 - \gamma$ for some $\gamma > 0$
  (the linearized map in $\log h$ is then a convex combination or a damped reflection, so $a_k$
  stays bounded by $\sup|c_k|/\gamma$ up to constants), together with
- a nondegenerate estimate, $\|E(y,h,\theta)\| \ge c_E h^{p+1}$ on $K_\delta$, to bound $c_k$ and
  $q_k$; making $q_k - (p+1)$ small may further need $E \in C^{p+2}$.

With $\|a_k\| \le C$ for all trials, $\|dh_n/d\theta\| \le Ch_n$ for unclipped steps and
$\|dh_{N-1}/d\theta\| = \|\sum_{n<N-1}dh_n/d\theta\| \le CT$ for a clipped last step, so
$C_2 = 2CT$ and (b) follows from (3). Choosing the hypothesis is the lead's call: per protocol it is
a new entry carrying `revises: MD_0020`.

## Formalization blockers

- The claims as stated: (b) and the full half of (c) appear false (section 6); nothing to formalize
  until a revision fixes the hypothesis of section 7.
- Mathlib carries no Runge-Kutta method, Butcher tableau or order conditions, no smooth dependence
  of an ODE flow on initial data or parameters, and no variational-equation theorem (S0 row). Lemma 1
  fields 2 and 3 and Lemma 2(ii) rest on exactly those.
- Formalizable today, and worth doing for any revision: the algebra of section 3 for an abstract
  sequence of linear maps, i.e. given $\|G_k\| \le 1 + L_\Psi h_k$, $\|G_kf_k - f_{k+1}\| \le C_dh_k^{p+1}$,
  $\|w_n - f_{n+1}\| \le C_\rho h_n^p$ and $\sum_n \eta_n = 0$, the bound
  $\|\sum_n \Phi_{N,n+1}w_n\eta_n\| \le C_1H^p\sum\|\eta_n\|$. It uses only `discrete_gronwall`-style
  product bounds and finite sums.
- Claim (a)'s finite-induction smoothness is formalizable with `ContDiffAt.comp` and the
  smoothness of the norm away from zero, once the controller is encoded as a fixed finite
  composition for a given decision pattern.
