# MD_0011: the cross-diffusivity gain function collapses on a single geometry
<!-- role: record | status: current 2026-09-16 | entry: MD_0011 -->

The canonical setting for MD_0011 to MD_0015, the derivation of the continuity equation the
consumer adopts, and the proof of MD_0011's Statement. Sections 1 and 2 are the shared setting;
MD_0012 to MD_0015 cite them rather than restating them.

## 1. The canonical setting

Time convention $e^{i\omega t}$ throughout, so $\partial_t \mapsto i\omega$ and $\omega > 0$.

A pore of cross-section $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$, a bounded Lipschitz domain, with rigid
isothermal walls. $\nabla_\perp^2$ is the transverse Laplacian, $A = |\Omega|$ the gas area, and
$\langle \cdot \rangle = |\Omega|^{-1}\int_\Omega \cdot \, dA$ the cross-section average. The mean
temperature $T_m(x)$ varies slowly along the pore and the mean pressure is uniform, so
$d\rho_m/dx = -\rho_m \beta \, dT_m/dx$.

The fluid coefficients are real-fluid, taken at the local mean state and not from an ideal gas:
$\beta = -\rho^{-1}(\partial\rho/\partial T)_P$, $c_p$, sound speed $a$, with

$$ \gamma - 1 \;=\; \frac{T_m \beta^2 a^2}{c_p}, \qquad
\frac{1}{K_T} - \frac{1}{K_s} \;=\; \frac{T_m \beta^2}{\rho_m c_p}, \qquad K_s = \rho_m a^2 . \tag{1} $$

**Viscous problem.** $i\omega \rho_m u_1 = -\,dp_1/dx + \mu \nabla_\perp^2 u_1$ with $u_1 = 0$ on
$\partial\Omega$. Writing $V := \dfrac{i}{\omega\rho_m}\dfrac{dp_1}{dx}$, the solution is
$u_1 = V(1 - h_v)$ where

$$ i\omega h_v = \nu \nabla_\perp^2 h_v \ \text{ on } \Omega, \qquad h_v = 1 \ \text{ on } \partial\Omega,
\qquad f_v := \langle h_v \rangle . \tag{2} $$

Substituting $u_1 = V(1-h_v)$ gives $i\omega\rho_m V(1-h_v) = -dp_1/dx - \mu V \nabla_\perp^2 h_v$, and
$i\omega\rho_m V = -dp_1/dx$ by the definition of $V$, so what remains is
$-i\omega\rho_m V h_v = -\mu V\nabla_\perp^2 h_v$, which is (2). Hence
$U_1 := \int_\Omega u_1\,dA = A V (1 - f_v)$.

**Thermal problem.** $i\omega\rho_m c_p T_1 - i\omega T_m \beta p_1
= k \nabla_\perp^2 T_1 - \rho_m c_p u_1 \, dT_m/dx$ with $T_1 = 0$ on $\partial\Omega$. Dividing by
$\rho_m c_p$ and writing $\alpha = k/(\rho_m c_p)$,

$$ (i\omega - \alpha\nabla_\perp^2)\,T_1 \;=\; \frac{i\omega T_m\beta}{\rho_m c_p}\,p_1
\;-\; V(1-h_v)\,\frac{dT_m}{dx} . \tag{3} $$

The right side is a sum of two sources, so $T_1$ splits accordingly.

*Pressure-driven part.* $(i\omega - \alpha\nabla_\perp^2)T_1^{p} = i\omega c\,$ with
$c = T_m\beta p_1/(\rho_m c_p)$ constant on $\Omega$ and $T_1^p = 0$ on $\partial\Omega$ is solved by
$T_1^p = c(1 - h_\kappa)$, since $(i\omega - \alpha\nabla_\perp^2)(1-h_\kappa) = i\omega$ by

$$ i\omega h_\kappa = \alpha\nabla_\perp^2 h_\kappa \ \text{ on } \Omega, \qquad
h_\kappa = 1 \ \text{ on } \partial\Omega, \qquad f_\kappa := \langle h_\kappa \rangle , \tag{4} $$

and $1 - h_\kappa$ vanishes on the wall.

*Gradient-driven part.* With $S := -\dfrac{dT_m}{dx} V$ the second source is $S(1-h_v)$, so
$T_1^{g} = (S/i\omega)\,\Theta$ where

$$ (i\omega - \alpha\nabla_\perp^2)\,\Theta \;=\; i\omega\,(1 - h_v), \qquad
\Theta = 0 \ \text{ on } \partial\Omega, \qquad g_{v\kappa}(\omega) := \langle\Theta\rangle . \tag{5} $$

**Prandtl number.** $\mathrm{Pr} = \nu/\alpha$, with $\nu, \alpha > 0$.

Problems (2), (4) and (5) are the only place geometry enters. Each is a Helmholtz problem with
spectral parameter $i\omega/D$ off the negative real axis for $\omega > 0$ and $D > 0$, so
$i\omega - D\nabla_\perp^2$ is an isomorphism $H^1_0(\Omega) \to H^{-1}(\Omega)$ and each solution is
unique. That is H2.

## 2. The continuity equation

The linearised equation of state at uniform mean pressure gives
$\rho_1 = (\rho_m/K_T)p_1 - \rho_m\beta T_1$, and $p_1$ is uniform on $\Omega$, so

$$ \langle\rho_1\rangle \;=\; \frac{\rho_m}{K_T}p_1 \;-\; \rho_m\beta\,\langle T_1\rangle,
\qquad \langle T_1\rangle = \frac{T_m\beta}{\rho_m c_p}p_1\,(1-f_\kappa) + \frac{S}{i\omega}g_{v\kappa}. \tag{6} $$

Mass conservation averaged over the cross-section is
$i\omega A \langle \rho_1\rangle + d(\rho_m U_1)/dx = 0$. Take the two pieces of (6) in turn.

*Pressure piece.* With $\Delta := T_m\beta^2/(\rho_m c_p)$,

$$ \frac{1}{K_T} - \Delta(1-f_\kappa) \;=\; \Big(\frac{1}{K_T} - \Delta\Big) + \Delta f_\kappa
\;=\; \frac{1}{K_s} + \Delta f_\kappa \;=\; \frac{1}{\rho_m a^2}\big[1 + (\gamma-1)f_\kappa\big], \tag{7} $$

using both identities of (1): the second gives $1/K_T - \Delta = 1/K_s = 1/(\rho_m a^2)$, and the
first gives $\Delta = (\gamma-1)/(\rho_m a^2)$.

*Gradient piece.* $-\rho_m\beta (S/i\omega) g_{v\kappa}$ contributes
$-i\omega A \rho_m\beta (S/i\omega) g_{v\kappa} = -A\rho_m\beta S g_{v\kappa}$, and with
$S = -(dT_m/dx)V$ and $V = U_1/(A(1-f_v))$, which needs H3,

$$ -A\rho_m\beta S\,g_{v\kappa} \;=\; \rho_m \beta \frac{dT_m}{dx}\,U_1\,\frac{g_{v\kappa}}{1-f_v}. \tag{8} $$

*The mean-density gradient.* $d(\rho_m U_1)/dx = \rho_m\,dU_1/dx + U_1\,d\rho_m/dx
= \rho_m\,dU_1/dx - \rho_m\beta (dT_m/dx) U_1$.

Adding the three and dividing by $\rho_m$,

$$ \boxed{\ \frac{dU_1}{dx} \;=\; -\,\frac{i\omega A}{\rho_m a^2}\Big[1 + (\gamma-1)f_\kappa\Big]p_1
\;+\; \beta\frac{dT_m}{dx}U_1\left[1 - \frac{g_{v\kappa}}{1-f_v}\right] . \ } \tag{9} $$

This is the canonical form. The bracket $1 - g_{v\kappa}/(1-f_v)$ is the entire gain term: it is the
only place $dT_m/dx$ enters, and it is what MD_0011 to MD_0015 are about. Nothing above assumed an
ideal gas, and nothing above assumed anything about $\Omega$ beyond (2), (4) and (5) being solvable.

## 3. The substitution (Statement, first clause)

Let $\mathrm{Pr} \neq 1$ and set

$$ \Theta \;:=\; 1 - h_\kappa + \frac{\mathrm{Pr}}{1-\mathrm{Pr}}\,(h_v - h_\kappa). \tag{10} $$

**Boundary condition.** On $\partial\Omega$, $h_v = h_\kappa = 1$ by H1, which is the clause that
gives both channel problems the *same* domain and the *same* boundary data. So
$\Theta|_{\partial\Omega} = 1 - 1 + \tfrac{\mathrm{Pr}}{1-\mathrm{Pr}}(1-1) = 0$.

**The equation.** Rewrite (2) and (4) with the *thermal* diffusivity in front, which is the whole
trick: $\alpha\nabla_\perp^2 h_v = \frac{\alpha}{\nu}\,\nu\nabla_\perp^2 h_v
= \frac{1}{\mathrm{Pr}}\,i\omega h_v$, and $\alpha\nabla_\perp^2 h_\kappa = i\omega h_\kappa$. With
$c := \mathrm{Pr}/(1-\mathrm{Pr})$,

$$ \alpha\nabla_\perp^2\Theta \;=\; -\,i\omega h_\kappa + c\Big(\frac{i\omega}{\mathrm{Pr}}h_v - i\omega h_\kappa\Big),
\qquad i\omega\Theta \;=\; i\omega\big(1 - h_\kappa + c h_v - c h_\kappa\big), $$

so, subtracting, every $h_\kappa$ cancels and

$$ (i\omega - \alpha\nabla_\perp^2)\Theta \;=\; i\omega\Big[1 + c\,h_v\Big(1 - \frac{1}{\mathrm{Pr}}\Big)\Big]. \tag{11} $$

Now

$$ c\Big(1 - \frac{1}{\mathrm{Pr}}\Big) \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{Pr}}{1-\mathrm{Pr}}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{Pr}-1}{\mathrm{Pr}}
\;=\; \frac{\mathrm{Pr}-1}{1-\mathrm{Pr}} \;=\; -1, \tag{12} $$

which needs exactly $\mathrm{Pr} \neq 1$, and (11) becomes
$(i\omega - \alpha\nabla_\perp^2)\Theta = i\omega(1 - h_v)$. So (10) solves (5).

**Uniqueness.** $\Theta \in H^1_0(\Omega)$ because $1-h_\kappa$ and $h_v - h_\kappa$ both are, and by
H2 the operator $i\omega - \alpha\nabla_\perp^2$ is injective on $H^1_0(\Omega)$: if
$(i\omega - \alpha\nabla_\perp^2)w = 0$ with $w \in H^1_0$, pairing with $\bar w$ gives
$i\omega\|w\|_{L^2}^2 + \alpha\|\nabla_\perp w\|_{L^2}^2 = 0$, and since $\omega > 0$ and $\alpha > 0$
the imaginary part forces $\|w\|_{L^2} = 0$. So (10) is *the* solution, which is the first clause.

## 4. The averaged form (Statement, second clause)

Average (10). Averaging is linear and $\langle 1 \rangle = 1$, so

$$ g_{v\kappa} \;=\; (1-f_\kappa) + \frac{\mathrm{Pr}}{1-\mathrm{Pr}}\,(f_v - f_\kappa). \tag{13} $$

Multiply by $1-\mathrm{Pr}$:

$$ (1-\mathrm{Pr})(1-f_\kappa) + \mathrm{Pr}(f_v - f_\kappa)
= (1-f_\kappa) - \mathrm{Pr} + \mathrm{Pr}f_\kappa + \mathrm{Pr}f_v - \mathrm{Pr}f_\kappa
= (1-f_\kappa) - \mathrm{Pr}(1-f_v), $$

so

$$ g_{v\kappa} \;=\; \frac{(1-f_\kappa) - \mathrm{Pr}(1-f_v)}{1-\mathrm{Pr}} . \tag{14} $$

## 5. Rott's bracket (Statement, third clause)

Assume H3, $f_v \neq 1$. Then

$$ 1 - \frac{g_{v\kappa}}{1-f_v}
= \frac{(1-\mathrm{Pr})(1-f_v) - (1-f_\kappa) + \mathrm{Pr}(1-f_v)}{(1-\mathrm{Pr})(1-f_v)}
= \frac{(1-f_v) - (1-f_\kappa)}{(1-\mathrm{Pr})(1-f_v)}
= \frac{f_\kappa - f_v}{(1-f_v)(1-\mathrm{Pr})} , \tag{15} $$

the $\mathrm{Pr}$ terms in the numerator cancelling because $(1-\mathrm{Pr}) + \mathrm{Pr} = 1$. That
is Rott's bracket, and MD_0011 is proved.

## 6. The modal form, and why the analytic structure is forced

This section is not part of the Statement. It is what the consumer needs, and it is the cleanest
route to MD_0012, MD_0014 and MD_0015, each of which reads a limit off it.

Let $-\nabla_\perp^2 e_n = \mu_n e_n$ on $H^1_0(\Omega)$ with $\{e_n\}$ orthonormal in $L^2(\Omega)$
and $0 < \mu_1 \le \mu_2 \le \cdots \to \infty$, and set $c_n := \int_\Omega e_n \, dA$ and
$w_n := c_n^2/|\Omega|$. Parseval on the constant function $1$ gives $\sum_n w_n = 1$, and
$w_n \ge 0$, with $w_n = 0$ exactly on a mode of zero mean. That case is the rule and not the
exception: every antisymmetric mode of the slab and every non-radial mode of the disc has
$c_n = 0$, so half the slab's weights vanish. On a degenerate eigenvalue which members vanish is
basis dependent, since the eigenspace can be rotated so that at most one member has nonzero mean;
the eigenvalue's total weight is what does not depend on the basis.

Write $s := i\omega$ and $u_D := 1 - h_D$, where $h_D$ solves (2) or (4) at diffusivity $D$. Then
$u_D \in H^1_0$ and $(s - D\nabla_\perp^2)u_D = s$, so $u_D = \sum_n s c_n e_n/(s + D\mu_n)$ and

$$ 1 - F(D) \;:=\; \langle u_D\rangle \;=\; \sum_n \frac{w_n\, s}{s + D\mu_n},
\qquad f_v = F(\nu), \quad f_\kappa = F(\alpha). \tag{16} $$

Substituting (16) into (14) and writing $\nu = \mathrm{Pr}\,\alpha$,

$$ (1-f_\kappa) - \mathrm{Pr}(1-f_v)
= \sum_n w_n s\left[\frac{1}{s+\alpha\mu_n} - \frac{\mathrm{Pr}}{s+\nu\mu_n}\right]
= \sum_n w_n s\,\frac{(s+\nu\mu_n) - \mathrm{Pr}(s+\alpha\mu_n)}{(s+\nu\mu_n)(s+\alpha\mu_n)}, $$

and the $\mu_n$ terms cancel in the numerator, $\nu\mu_n - \mathrm{Pr}\,\alpha\mu_n = 0$, leaving
$s(1-\mathrm{Pr})$. Dividing by $1-\mathrm{Pr}$,

$$ \boxed{\ g_{v\kappa}(s) \;=\; s^2 \sum_{n} \frac{w_n}{(s+\nu\mu_n)(s+\alpha\mu_n)},
\qquad w_n \ge 0, \quad \textstyle\sum_n w_n = 1. \ } \tag{17} $$

Four consequences, all used downstream.

1. **The $\mathrm{Pr} = 1$ pole is an artefact of the algebra, not of the function.** (17) has no
   $1-\mathrm{Pr}$ anywhere. At $\nu = \alpha$ the two poles of each term merge into a double pole and
   nothing diverges. The singular-looking form (14) is a divided difference in disguise: with
   $W(D) := (D/\alpha)(1-F(D))$ one has $g_{v\kappa} = [W(\nu) - W(\alpha)]/(\nu - \alpha)\cdot\alpha$,
   finite because it is a difference quotient of ONE function. MD_0012 is that observation.

2. **The analytic structure is the one a kernel bank can carry, but the sum is infinite.** Be
   precise about this, because the request made finite rationality a hard requirement. (17) is
   real MEROMORPHIC in $s$, not real-rational: it has infinitely many poles, at
   $s = -\nu\mu_n$ and $s = -\alpha\mu_n$ for the modes carrying $w_n \neq 0$ and at no other
   point, all real and strictly negative, accumulating only at $s = -\infty$ since
   $\mu_n \to \infty$. Restricting the pole set to the modes of nonzero mean is not decoration:
   $j_{1,1}^2$ is a Dirichlet eigenvalue of the disc, and $g_{v\kappa}$ is analytic at
   $s = -D\,j_{1,1}^2$, because that mode has zero mean. There are no branch points and no
   complex-conjugate pairs, and $g_{v\kappa} \to \sum_n w_n = 1$ as $\omega \to \infty$ along the
   imaginary axis, which is the feedthrough a bank realises. There is no limit at $\infty$ in
   $\mathbb{C}$, the poles accumulating there. Partial fractions give
   $g_{v\kappa} = 1 + \sum_n w_n\mu_n\big[\nu^2/((\alpha-\nu)(s+\nu\mu_n)) - \alpha^2/((\alpha-\nu)(s+\alpha\mu_n))\big]$,
   which is the form $d + \sum_j \gamma_j/(s+\xi_j^2)$ a first-order relaxation bank realises. The
   residues blow up individually as $\mathrm{Pr}\to 1$ while their sum does not, so a march near the
   crossing uses the product form or the merged double pole, never the partial fractions.

   **What a march therefore gets is a truncation, with a budget.** Keeping $N$ modes keeps $2N$
   real poles (one pair per mode, $-\nu\mu_n$ and $-\alpha\mu_n$), and that truncation IS a real
   rational function of $s$ with real negative poles, so the requirement is met by the truncate,
   not by the exact object. The error is controlled: $w_n = O(n^{-2})$ and $\mu_n = \Theta(n^2)$ in
   one transverse dimension, so the discarded terms are
   $\sum_{n>N} w_n s^2/((s+\nu\mu_n)(s+\alpha\mu_n)) = O(|s|^2 N^{-5}/(\nu\alpha))$ at fixed $s$,
   a fifth-order tail. Measured at the top of a four-decade band centred on the viscous corner
   $\omega_c = 2\nu/L^2$, that is, at the worst frequency in the band:

   | $N$ per diffusivity | slab, $\mathrm{Pr}=1$ | disc, $\mathrm{Pr}=1$ | disc, $\mathrm{Pr}=5$ |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 4 | $9.0\times10^{-3}$ | $1.6\times10^{-2}$ | $2.7\times10^{-2}$ |
   | 8 | $5.1\times10^{-4}$ | $9.4\times10^{-4}$ | $3.2\times10^{-3}$ |
   | 16 | $1.7\times10^{-5}$ | $3.4\times10^{-5}$ | $1.6\times10^{-4}$ |
   | 32 | $5.3\times10^{-7}$ | $1.1\times10^{-6}$ | $5.3\times10^{-6}$ |

   Each doubling of $N$ buys a factor approaching $32 = 2^5$, which is the $N^{-5}$ law; the
   first doubling buys about $18$ and the later ones about $32$, so the law is asymptotic. So
   $N = 8$ modes per component per diffusivity holds the whole band to $10^{-2}$ and $N = 16$ to
   $10^{-3}$, at $16$ and $32$ real poles respectively, and the error is worst at the top of the
   band and at large $\mathrm{Pr}$, which is where a budget should be checked.

   **That budget is band-conditional, and the condition is the clause a consumer has to carry.**
   $N^{-5}$ is a statement at fixed $s$. Above $\omega \approx \nu\mu_N$ the truncation stops
   decaying and saturates at the discarded tail mass $\sum_{n>N} w_n$, which for the slab at
   $N = 8$ is $2.53\times10^{-2}$. Measured on the slab at $N = 8$: $5.0\times10^{-8}$ at
   $\omega_c$, $4.7\times10^{-4}$ at the top of the band, then $9.2\times10^{-3}$ one decade
   above it, $1.9\times10^{-2}$ two decades above, and the $2.5\times10^{-2}$ plateau beyond. A
   bank sized on the $N^{-5}$ law and then marched above its band holds the tail mass, not the
   number the law promises.

3. **The low-frequency coefficients are Poisson moments.** Expanding (17) for $|s| < \alpha\mu_1$
   gives $g_{v\kappa} = s^2 (\nu\alpha)^{-1}\sum_n w_n/\mu_n^2 + O(s^3)$, and
   $\sum_n w_n/\mu_n = \langle\Phi\rangle$, $\sum_n w_n/\mu_n^2 = \langle\Phi^2\rangle$ where
   $\nabla_\perp^2\Phi = -1$, $\Phi|_{\partial\Omega} = 0$. That is MD_0014.

4. **A single mode set forces $\alpha_\infty = 1$ and $k_0 = k_0'$.** From (16), $1-f_v$ and
   $1-f_\kappa$ are the SAME $\{(w_n,\mu_n)\}$ read at two diffusivities. Their high-frequency limits
   are both $\sum w_n = 1$ and their low-frequency slopes are both $\langle\Phi\rangle$ divided by the
   respective diffusivity. A medium whose viscous channel has $\alpha_\infty \neq 1$, or whose two
   static permeabilities differ, therefore has no single cross-section behind it. That is the
   precise claim: (2) and (4) carry no $x$ dependence, so what this argument excludes is one
   straight pore of uniform cross-section, which is what "single geometry" means throughout this
   file.

   This argument reaches TWO of MD_0013's three conditions and not the third. $\alpha_\infty = 1$
   is the zeroth moment $\sum w_n$ and $k_0 = k_0'$ is the first moment $\sum w_n/\mu_n$; a shared
   mode set does make the two channels one function, so $\Lambda = \Lambda'$ follows for a medium
   that HAS a mode set, but that is a consequence of sharing the whole function and not of matching
   a moment, and it is not derived here. MD_0013 derives all three from the published closed forms,
   and reads $\Lambda = \Lambda'$ off the $\omega^{-1/2}$ coefficient, which is the argument that
   actually settles it.

## 7. What H1 carries

H1 is load-bearing twice and in two different ways.

- In section 3 it gives $h_v$ and $h_\kappa$ the same boundary value, which is what makes
  $\Theta|_{\partial\Omega} = 0$ hold term by term, and the same domain, which is what lets
  $\alpha\nabla_\perp^2$ act on both through their own equations.
- In section 6 it gives them the same $\{(w_n, \mu_n)\}$, which is what makes $g_{v\kappa}$ a divided
  difference of one function rather than a difference of two.

Drop H1 and both fail together. That is the content of the request: the defect the consumer found is
not an implementation error and not a numerical one, it is H1 being false for the medium in use.

## Formalization blockers

The algebra of sections 3 to 5 is trivial in Lean; the setting is not.

- Stating (5) needs the Dirichlet problem for $i\omega - D\nabla_\perp^2$ on a bounded Lipschitz
  domain in $\mathbb{C}$-valued $H^1_0$. Mathlib has `MeasureTheory`, `InnerProductSpace` and
  `LaxMilgram` (`IsCoercive.continuousLinearEquivOfBilin`), but no Dirichlet Laplacian on a domain and
  no trace theorem, so "$h = 1$ on $\partial\Omega$" has to be encoded as $h - 1 \in H^1_0(\Omega)$,
  which is the standard workaround and changes nothing mathematically. `H^1_0(\Omega)` itself is not
  in Mathlib as a named object; it would be built as the closure of `C_c^\infty` in a Sobolev norm,
  and that construction is most of the work.
- Given that setting, the proof is: a substitution, one cancellation (12) requiring
  `Pr \neq 1`, and injectivity from the imaginary part of a sesquilinear form. All of it is
  `field_simp` plus one `norm_num` on the coefficient identity, and one three-line lemma for
  uniqueness. None of it needs a named theorem.
- A statement-faithful but cheap alternative exists and is worth doing first: formalize the ALGEBRA
  alone, taking $f_v, f_\kappa \in \mathbb{C}$ and $\mathrm{Pr} \in \mathbb{C}\setminus\{1\}$ as given
  and proving (13) $\Rightarrow$ (14) $\Rightarrow$ (15). That is sections 4 and 5 verbatim, it is a
  ten-line Lean proof over `Complex`, and it covers the two identities the consumer actually
  substitutes into. What it does not cover is that $\Theta$ of (10) is the solution, which is the part
  carrying the PDE.
- Section 6 would additionally need the Dirichlet eigenbasis and Parseval for the constant function,
  which is `MeasureTheory.L2` plus a spectral theorem for a compact self-adjoint resolvent. Mathlib has
  `IsCompactOperator` and the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators on a Hilbert space,
  so this is reachable, but it sits behind the same missing $H^1_0(\Omega)$.
