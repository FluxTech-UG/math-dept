# MD_0015: the high-frequency cross term carries a third geometric length
<!-- role: record | status: current 2026-09-16 | entry: MD_0015 -->

The setting extends `proofs/MD_0011.md` section 1 from a two-dimensional pore cross-section to a
three-dimensional pore space. $s := i\omega$, $\delta_v = \sqrt{2\nu/\omega}$,
$\delta_\kappa = \sqrt{2\alpha/\omega}$, $\mathrm{Pr} = \nu/\alpha$.

**Verdict up front, by clause.**

| clause | verdict |
|---|---|
| the coefficient $(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}+\mathrm{Pr})/(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}})$, finite at $\mathrm{Pr}=1$ where it is $3/2$ | proved, with one formal-asymptotic step (section 2) |
| the cross term is LINEAR rather than quadratic in $v$, and carries a third weighted length | proved, same step; the weight is $v\cdot\hat e$, not $|v|$ (section 3) |
| $g_{v\kappa}$ is the printed single-length expression with that one length $\Lambda''$ | **refuted**, on an exhibited pore, by `counterexamples/MD_0015_single_length_high_frequency.py` (sections 4 and 5a) |
| reduction to $\Lambda = \Lambda' = \Lambda'' = 2V/S$ on a straight uniform pore, hence on tube and plates | proved and numerically certified (section 5) |
| $\Lambda''$ lies between $\Lambda$ and $\Lambda'$ | **refuted**, on a smooth pore with a solved potential field, in both directions, by `counterexamples/MD_0015_shape_varying_duct.py` (section 6); it does hold on the slowly varying classes |

So MD_0015 as frozen is not proved. Two separate things have to be kept apart when saying why,
because the entry's Statement does leave real slack.

**The slack.** The Statement defines neither $g_{v\kappa}$ on a three-dimensional pore nor a
normalization for $v$; this proof supplies both at (2) and (3). That slack is confined to ONE overall
multiplicative constant, and its whole effect is the $1/\alpha_\infty$ prefactor. It is not a defect
of the printed form, and section 4 does not treat it as one.

**What is decided in spite of the slack.** An overall constant cancels out of the RATIO of the
$O(\omega^{-1/2})$ deficit to the $O(1)$ term, so the SHAPE of the correction, which is what the
single-length clause is about, is convention-free. Section 5a exhibits a pore on which that
normalization-free ratio misses the printed single-length coefficient by 10 to 46 percent across the
Prandtl range while (11) matches it to parts in $10^4$. The single-length clause is therefore false,
with a verifying witness, and not merely unsupported. Betweenness, section 6, is well posed for a
different and simpler reason: $\Lambda_w$ is homogeneous of degree zero in $w$, so the three lengths
need neither a normalization nor a PDE. That clause is now refuted too, section 6, on a smooth pore
whose potential field is solved rather than assumed. So both of the Statement's two contentful claims
are false, each with its own verifying witness, and what survives is the Prandtl coefficient, the
linearity of the cross term, and the $2V/S$ reduction. What is proved is a corrected statement, (12) below, which is what
the consumer should carry.

## 1. Definitions and normalization

$\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ is the pore space with smooth walls $\partial\Omega$; $\hat e$ is the
macroscopic axis, the direction of $\nabla T_m$ and of the mean flow. For a weight $w \ge 0$,

$$ \frac{2}{\Lambda_w} \;:=\; \frac{\int_{\partial\Omega} w\,dS}{\int_\Omega w\,dV}. \tag{1} $$

Let $v$ be the leading-order inviscid (potential) velocity field driven along $\hat e$, and $V$ the
scalar reference amplitude, so $v = V\tilde v$ with the standard tortuosity normalization

$$ \langle \tilde v\cdot\hat e\rangle \;=\; \langle|\tilde v|^2\rangle \;=\; \frac{1}{\alpha_\infty},
\qquad \alpha_\infty = \frac{\langle|\tilde v|^2\rangle}{\langle\tilde v\cdot\hat e\rangle^2}, \tag{2} $$

and $\tilde v = \hat e$, $\alpha_\infty = 1$ on a straight uniform pore. Put

$$ \tilde u := \frac{u\cdot\hat e}{V}, \qquad 1 - f_v := \langle\tilde u\rangle,
\qquad (i\omega - \alpha\nabla^2)\Theta = i\omega\,\tilde u \ \text{ on } \Omega, \quad \Theta|_{\partial\Omega} = 0,
\qquad g_{v\kappa} := \langle\Theta\rangle, \tag{3} $$

which is MD_0011 (5) with $1-h_v$ replaced by the normalized velocity component, the only sensible
generalization once no single channel function exists. On a straight uniform pore
$\tilde u = 1-h_v$ and (3) is MD_0011 (5) verbatim.

**Two things here are supplied by this proof, not by the frozen Statement, and the reader should
know which.** The Statement names $v$ and the three weighted lengths but does not define
$g_{v\kappa}$ in three dimensions and does not fix a normalization. (3) is this proof's DEFINITION
of $g_{v\kappa}$ on a general pore, and (2) is this proof's normalization convention. Both reduce to
the frozen objects on a straight uniform pore, where $\alpha_\infty = 1$ and $\tilde v = \hat e$, so
nothing is changed where the Statement is unambiguous. But the $1/\alpha_\infty$ prefactor that
section 4 finds, and the sign of the weight in (9), are consequences of these choices as much as of
the asymptotics, and a different but equally defensible normalization would move the prefactor. What
does NOT depend on the choice is that two distinct weighted lengths appear where the Statement has
one; that is the finding.

The two published lengths are $\Lambda = \Lambda_{w=|\tilde v|^2}$ and $\Lambda' = \Lambda_{w=1}$, and
the Johnson, Koplik and Dashen result used below is

$$ 1 - f_v \;=\; \frac{1}{\alpha_\infty}\Big[1 - (1-i)\frac{\delta_v}{\Lambda}\Big] + O(\omega^{-1}),
\qquad 1 - f_\kappa \;=\; 1 - (1-i)\frac{\delta_\kappa}{\Lambda'} + O(\omega^{-1}). \tag{4} $$

## 2. The local layer problem, solved exactly

H1 is the step that is formal asymptotics rather than proof: the walls are smooth enough that the
oscillatory layer is locally one-dimensional, so at each wall point $\xi$ with inward normal
coordinate $n$ the fields depend on $n$ alone at leading order, with the curvature entering at
$O(\delta/\mathcal{R})$ where $\mathcal{R}$ is the smallest radius of curvature. Granting that, the
local problem is an ordinary differential equation and is solved in closed form, with no further
approximation.

Write $a := \sqrt{s/\nu} = (1+i)/\delta_v$ and $b := \sqrt{s/\alpha} = (1+i)/\delta_\kappa$, so

$$ \frac{a}{b} = \sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\nu}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}}, \qquad \text{i.e.}\quad a = \frac{b}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}}. \tag{5} $$

Let $\sigma(\xi) := \tilde v(\xi)\cdot\hat e$ be the slip value the inviscid field leaves at the wall.
The viscous layer is $\tilde u = \sigma(1 - e^{-an})$, which is the Stokes solution of
$s\,\tilde u = \nu\,\partial_n^2\tilde u + s\sigma$ with $\tilde u(0) = 0$ and $\tilde u \to \sigma$.

For $\Theta$, solve $(s - \alpha\partial_n^2)\Theta = s\sigma(1 - e^{-an})$, $\Theta(0) = 0$,
$\Theta\to\sigma$. The particular solution for the constant source is $\sigma$; for the source
$-s\sigma e^{-an}$ try $C\sigma e^{-an}$, which gives
$(s - \alpha a^2)C = s(1 - 1/\mathrm{Pr})C = -s$, so $C = \mathrm{Pr}/(1-\mathrm{Pr})$. The
homogeneous solution decaying into the fluid is $e^{-bn}$, and $\Theta(0) = 0$ fixes its coefficient:

$$ \Theta \;=\; \sigma\left[1 + \frac{\mathrm{Pr}}{1-\mathrm{Pr}}e^{-an} - \frac{1}{1-\mathrm{Pr}}e^{-bn}\right], \tag{6} $$

which is MD_0011 (10) read locally, with $h_v = e^{-an}$ and $h_\kappa = e^{-bn}$, so the two
derivations agree where they overlap.

**The full deficit.** $\int_0^\infty(\sigma - \Theta)\,dn
= \sigma\big[-\tfrac{\mathrm{Pr}}{1-\mathrm{Pr}}\tfrac1a + \tfrac{1}{1-\mathrm{Pr}}\tfrac1b\big]
= \tfrac{\sigma}{(1-\mathrm{Pr})b}\big(1 - \mathrm{Pr}^{3/2}\big)$ by (5), and factoring
$1 - \mathrm{Pr}^{3/2} = (1-\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}})(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}+\mathrm{Pr})$,
$1-\mathrm{Pr} = (1-\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}})(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}})$,

$$ \int_0^\infty(\sigma-\Theta)\,dn \;=\; \frac{\sigma}{b}\cdot\frac{1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}+\mathrm{Pr}}{1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}}. \tag{7} $$

The factor $(1-\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}})$ cancels, so (7) is a ratio of two polynomials in
$\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}$ with no zero of the denominator at $\mathrm{Pr}=1$, where the value is
$3/2$. That is the coefficient clause, proved, and its finiteness at $\mathrm{Pr}=1$ is the same
removable cancellation MD_0012 found at the other end of the band. With
$1/b = (1-i)\delta_\kappa/2$, (7) is $(1-i)(\delta_\kappa/2)\sigma\,(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}+\mathrm{Pr})/(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}})$.

## 3. The cross term is linear in $v$, and why the naive argument for the viscous term is not

**The trap.** Applying the same layer integral to $\tilde u$ alone gives
$\int_0^\infty(\sigma - \tilde u)dn = \sigma/a$, suggesting that the viscous deficit is also linear in
$\tilde v$, with weight $\tilde v\cdot\hat e$. That is wrong, and (4) says so: the viscous length is
$|\tilde v|^2$-weighted. The reason is that the layer's mass defect is not divergence free, so it
drives an $O(\delta_v)$ irrotational correction to the bulk field, which contributes to
$\langle\tilde u\rangle$ at the same order; the Johnson, Koplik and Dashen derivation handles this by
pairing the momentum equation against $\tilde v^*$, and the wall shear dotted into the slip velocity
is what produces $\oint|\tilde v|^2 dS$.

**Why the cross term escapes the trap.** Do not compute $\langle\Theta\rangle$ directly. Compute
$\langle\Theta\rangle - \langle\tilde u\rangle$.

Three steps below are FORMAL ASYMPTOTICS, in the same sense as section 2's H1 and with no error
bound supplied here. They are flagged **[F1]**, **[F2]**, **[F3]** where they occur, and the verdict
table's "proved, with one formal-asymptotic step" should be read as "proved modulo these, which are
the standard matched-asymptotic steps for this problem and are not proved anywhere in this file".

**[F1] The bulk remainder.** In the bulk, $\alpha\nabla^2\Theta$ is smaller than
$s\Theta$ by $O(\alpha/(\omega \ell^2)) = O(\delta_\kappa^2/\ell^2)$, where $\ell$ is the pore
scale, so from (3) $\Theta = \tilde u + O(\omega^{-1})$ pointwise in the bulk, **whatever
$\tilde u$ is**, including its $O(\delta_v)$ irrotational correction. Asserted, not bounded.

**[F2] The displacement-effect cancellation.** Granting [F1], $\Theta - \tilde u$ is supported in
the layer to the order that matters. This is the step that does the real work, and it is a corollary
of [F1] rather than a separate assumption. Put $w := \Theta - \tilde u$. No-slip gives
$\tilde u|_{\partial\Omega} = 0$ as well as $\Theta|_{\partial\Omega} = 0$, so subtracting (3) from
itself gives

$$ (s - \alpha\nabla^2)\,w \;=\; \alpha\nabla^2\tilde u, \qquad w|_{\partial\Omega} = 0, $$

and the source $\alpha\nabla^2\tilde u$ is $O(s\sigma)$ only INSIDE the layer, falling to
$O(\alpha/\ell^2)$ relative in the bulk, which is [F1]. So the $O(\delta_v)$ irrotational
displacement correction reaches $w$ only through its own Laplacian, at
$O(\delta_v\delta_\kappa^2/\ell^3)$, two orders below the layer term $O(\delta_\kappa/\ell)$: it does
not survive at the order claimed. Solving that one-dimensional problem directly reproduces (8),
sign included. What remains asserted rather than bounded is [F1] itself.

**[F3] The summation over the wall.** Integrating the local layer profile over $dS$ and calling the
result the volume average treats the layer as a product of a one-dimensional profile and the wall
measure, discarding curvature corrections at $O(\delta/\mathcal{R})$ and any contribution from
regions where the local layer picture fails, such as corners, edges and stagnation points of
$\tilde v$. H2's requirement that $|v|$ be bounded away from zero is exactly what excludes
stagnation points; corners are excluded by H1's smoothness, and a wire screen has neither smooth
walls nor a stagnation-free field, so [F3] is the step most exposed for the medium that raised the
request.

With those flagged, in the layer (6) gives:

$$ \Theta - \tilde u \;=\; \sigma\,\frac{e^{-an} - e^{-bn}}{1-\mathrm{Pr}},
\qquad \int_0^\infty(\Theta-\tilde u)\,dn = \frac{\sigma}{1-\mathrm{Pr}}\Big(\frac1a - \frac1b\Big)
= \frac{\sigma(\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}-1)}{(1-\mathrm{Pr})b} = -\frac{\sigma}{(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}})\,b}. \tag{8} $$

The displacement effect does not reach $w$ at this order, by [F2]. That is the structural point of
this proof, and it is what makes the cross weighting genuinely linear.

Summing (8) over the wall, $\langle\Theta\rangle - \langle\tilde u\rangle
= -\frac{1}{(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}})b}\cdot\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\oint_{\partial\Omega}\sigma\,dS$, and with

$$ \frac{2}{\Lambda''} \;:=\; \frac{\displaystyle\oint_{\partial\Omega}(\tilde v\cdot\hat e)\,dS}{\displaystyle\int_\Omega(\tilde v\cdot\hat e)\,dV},
\qquad \frac{1}{|\Omega|}\oint\sigma\,dS = \frac{2}{\Lambda''}\,\langle\tilde v\cdot\hat e\rangle
= \frac{2}{\alpha_\infty\Lambda''} \ \text{ by (2)}, \tag{9} $$

$$ g_{v\kappa} \;=\; (1-f_v) \;-\; \frac{(1-i)\,\delta_\kappa}{(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}})\,\alpha_\infty\,\Lambda''} + O(\omega^{-1}). \tag{10} $$

**The weight is $\tilde v\cdot\hat e$, not $|\tilde v|$.** The source of (3) is the velocity component
along $\hat e$, because that is the component that advects the mean temperature gradient, so $\sigma$
is a signed scalar. The two agree wherever $\tilde v$ is parallel to $\hat e$, in particular on any
straight pore, and differ on a tortuous one, where $\tilde v\cdot\hat e$ can even be negative and the
Statement's requirement $w \ge 0$ fails. This is a correction to the Statement, not a gap in it.

## 4. Assembly, and where the printed form is wrong

Insert (4) into (10) and use $\delta_v = \sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}\,\delta_\kappa$:

$$ \boxed{\ g_{v\kappa} \;=\; \frac{1}{\alpha_\infty}\left[1 \;-\; (1-i)\,\delta_\kappa\left(\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}}{\Lambda} + \frac{1}{(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}})\,\Lambda''}\right)\right] \;+\; O(\omega^{-1}). \ } \tag{11} $$

Two lengths, not one. The prefactor $1/\alpha_\infty$ is this proof's own convention, produced by
(2) and (3), and it is NOT a defect of the printed form; the last paragraph of this section is where
it matters. What is convention-free is the two-length structure, because every admissible
normalization differs by a single multiplicative constant that scales the $O(1)$ and
$O(\omega^{-1/2})$ terms alike, so the ratio of deficit to leading term carries no convention at all.
When $\Lambda = \Lambda'' =: \Lambda_0$,

$$ \frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}}{\Lambda_0} + \frac{1}{(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}})\Lambda_0}
= \frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}) + 1}{(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}})\Lambda_0}
= \frac{1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}+\mathrm{Pr}}{(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}})\Lambda_0}, \tag{12} $$

and (11) becomes the Statement's expression, with $\alpha_\infty = 1$. So the printed form is the
case $\alpha_\infty = 1$, $\Lambda = \Lambda''$, and elsewhere it charges the whole deficit, viscous
part included, to the linearly weighted length.

**Under this proof's own normalization the second condition is implied by the first, and that
collapses the printed form's domain of validity onto section 5.** By (2) and Cauchy to Schwarz,
$\langle\tilde v\cdot\hat e\rangle^2 \le \langle(\tilde v\cdot\hat e)^2\rangle \le \langle|\tilde v|^2\rangle$,
so $\alpha_\infty \ge 1$ with equality only when $\tilde v \equiv \hat e$; and then $|\tilde v|$ is
constant, so $\Lambda = \Lambda' = \Lambda'' = 2V/S$ by (13). The printed single-length form is
therefore valid exactly on the straight uniform pore of section 5 and nowhere else, which means
$\Lambda''$ never appears as the sole length anywhere. The entry's title, that the cross term carries
a THIRD geometric length, is not supported by this proof: the cross term carries a third length
alongside $\Lambda$, never instead of it.

The prefactor matters for the consumer and is easy to misread. Under the normalization (3),
$g_{v\kappa}\to 1/\alpha_\infty$ and $1-f_v \to 1/\alpha_\infty$ at high frequency, so the gain bracket
$1 - g_{v\kappa}/(1-f_v) \to 0$, which is the physically correct statement that a pore much wider than
both penetration depths has no gain. A form with $g_{v\kappa}\to1$ and $1-f_v\to1/\alpha_\infty$ would
send the bracket to $1-\alpha_\infty < 0$, a spurious sign flip at the top of the band.

## 5. Reduction to a single length on a straight uniform pore

If $|\tilde v|$ is constant on $\Omega$, which for a straight pore of uniform cross-section it is
(with $\tilde v = \hat e$, $\alpha_\infty = 1$), then the weight cancels out of (1) for every $w$ that
is a power of $|\tilde v|$:

$$ \Lambda = \Lambda' = \Lambda'' = \frac{2\int_\Omega dV}{\oint_{\partial\Omega}dS} = \frac{2V}{S}. \tag{13} $$

Two cases, with the numbers the probe uses.

- **Circular tube** of radius $R$: $2V/S = 2\pi R^2 L/(2\pi R L) = R$. Check against the exact
  channel function. With $z = R\sqrt{s/\alpha}$ and $I_1(z)/I_0(z) = 1 - 1/(2z) + O(z^{-2})$,

  $$ f_\kappa = \frac{2I_1(z)}{zI_0(z)} = \frac{2}{z} - \frac{1}{z^2} + O(z^{-3}) = \frac{2}{z} + O(z^{-2}),
  \qquad\text{so}\qquad 1 - f_\kappa = 1 - \frac{2}{z} + O(z^{-2}) \;\longrightarrow\; 1, $$

  and therefore $1/(1-f_\kappa) = 1 + 2/z + O(z^{-2})$. Since $z = R(1+i)/\delta_\kappa$, we have
  $2/z = (1-i)\delta_\kappa/R$, so $\tilde\alpha' = 1 + (1-i)\delta_\kappa/R$ and
  $\Lambda' = R = 2V/S$.
- **Parallel plates** of half-gap $y_0$: $2V/S = 2(2y_0 A)/(2A) = 2y_0$, the FULL gap, not the half
  gap. Check: $1-f_\kappa = 1 - \tanh z/z \to 1 - 1/z$ with $z = y_0\sqrt{s/\alpha}$, so
  $1/(1-f_\kappa) \to 1 + 1/z = 1 + (1-i)\delta_\kappa/(2y_0)$ and $\Lambda' = 2y_0$. A closure that
  inserts $\Lambda = y_0$ for a plate stack is a factor of two wrong in the high-frequency deficit.

Both reductions are certified numerically, and the certificate has to be a ratio on the DEFICIT
rather than on $g_{v\kappa}$ itself. At $\omega = 10^8\omega_c$ the whole deficit is about $10^{-4}$ of
$g_{v\kappa}$, so a test that grades $g_{v\kappa}$ against the leading form at a tolerance of
$3\times10^{-3}$ passes with the leading term deleted outright, and certifies nothing.
`triage/MD_0011_interpolant_probe.py` test T3 therefore grades
$D := (1 - g_{v\kappa})/((1-i)\delta_\kappa)$ against $C(\mathrm{Pr})/\Lambda_0$, evaluating
$1 - g_{v\kappa}$ in the cancellation-free form $(f_\kappa - \mathrm{Pr}f_v)/(1-\mathrm{Pr})$ and using
the exact channel functions rather than a truncated modal ladder. On the slab the agreement is
machine zero at every $\mathrm{Pr}$ tested, the deficit being exactly
$C(\mathrm{Pr})(1-i)\delta_\kappa/(2y_0)$ once $\tanh$ saturates; on the tube it is $1.6\times10^{-4}$
at $\mathrm{Pr} = 0.05$ falling to $3.6\times10^{-5}$ at $\mathrm{Pr} = 20$, decaying by a factor ten
per two decades of $\omega$, which is the $O(\omega^{-1})$ remainder against an $O(\omega^{-1/2})$
leading term. The test carries its own discrimination check: substituting the half-gap $y_0$ for
$2y_0$, or the coefficient $1$ for $C(\mathrm{Pr})$, must FAIL it, and does. $\mathrm{Pr} = 1$ is
included, where the coefficient is $3/2$, so H3 is exercised directly.

## 5a. A pore on which $\Lambda \ne \Lambda''$, and what it decides

Sections 5 and 2 cannot separate $\Lambda$ from $\Lambda''$, because on every straight uniform pore
they are equal, and tilting the pore does not help: under (2) an inclined straight pore has
$\tilde v = c\,\hat t$ with $c(\hat t\cdot\hat e) = c^2$, so $c = \cos\theta$ and
$|\tilde v|^2 = \cos^2\theta = \tilde v\cdot\hat e$ pointwise, and the two lengths still coincide. A
parallel bundle of straight pores of different sizes collapses them too, since $\tilde v = \hat e$ in
every channel. **Separating them requires $|\tilde v|$ to be non-constant on the wall, which for a
potential flow in a rigid pore means the pore must constrict, expand or bend.**

Take the slowly varying axisymmetric duct of radius $R(x)$ over one period, in the limit of large
period over radius, where the quasi-one-dimensional reduction is the leading-order exact solution and
$\tilde v \approx (c/A(x))\hat e$ with $A = \pi R^2$. Writing $\langle\cdot\rangle$ for the period
average in $x$, and noting $dS = 2\pi R\,dx$ against $dV = \pi R^2 dx$,

$$ \alpha_\infty = \langle R^2\rangle\langle R^{-2}\rangle, \qquad
\frac{1}{\Lambda} = \frac{\langle R^{-3}\rangle}{\langle R^{-2}\rangle}, \qquad
\frac{1}{\Lambda''} = \langle R^{-1}\rangle, \qquad
\Lambda' = \frac{\langle R^2\rangle}{\langle R\rangle}. \tag{13b} $$

Every one is a period average, so all four are independent of the period and the long-duct limit may
be taken freely. At $R(x) = 1 + 0.6\cos(2\pi x/L)$ they are
$\alpha_\infty = 2.30469$, $\Lambda = 0.54237$, $\Lambda'' = 0.80000$, $\Lambda' = 1.18000$, so
$\Lambda''/\Lambda = 1.475$ and the two candidate coefficients are far apart. H1 holds, the wall
being smooth with radii of curvature $O(R)$ and $O(L)$ against a vanishing $\delta_\kappa$; H2 holds,
$|\tilde v| = c/(\pi R^2)$ being bounded above and below and $\tilde v\cdot\hat e = |\tilde v| > 0$,
so the signed and unsigned weights agree and the witness bears on MD_0015's own $\Lambda_{w=|v|}$.

The reference carries no boundary-layer asymptotics at all: $g_{v\kappa}$ is assembled from the EXACT
circular-tube channel functions station by station, closed by the incompressibility constraint
$Q = V_{\text{loc}}A(x)(1 - f_{v,\text{loc}}) = \text{const}$. So the quantity being graded is not
produced by the expansion it grades. That flux constraint is the displacement effect of section 3 in
concrete form, and it is precisely what makes the viscous deficit carry $\Lambda$: dropping it returns
$\Lambda''$, which is (6.1)'s naive subtraction seen from the other side.

Grading the normalization-free ratio
$K := (g(\infty) - g(\omega))/((1-i)\delta_\kappa\,g(\infty))$ at $\omega = 10^8\omega_c$:

| $\mathrm{Pr}$ | 0.05 | 0.5 | 1 | 3 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| printed single length misses by | 10.2 % | 26.0 % | 31.7 % | 39.2 % | 45.6 % |
| two-length (11) misses by | 2.6e-4 | 9.1e-5 | 7.1e-5 | 5.5e-5 | 4.6e-5 |

The witness is `counterexamples/MD_0015_single_length_high_frequency.py`, and it carries a guard: it
RAISES rather than returning a verdict unless (11) itself matches at the same point, so a refutation
there can only mean the single-length form is wrong and never that the reference is. What this does
not do is settle betweenness: on this duct $\Lambda \le \Lambda'' \le \Lambda'$ holds comfortably.

## 6. Betweenness: proved for slowly varying ducts, not in general

Write $R(p) := \oint|\tilde v|^p dS / \int|\tilde v|^p dV$, so $\Lambda_{w=|\tilde v|^p} = 2/R(p)$ and
the three lengths are $\Lambda' = 2/R(0)$, $\Lambda'' = 2/R(1)$, $\Lambda = 2/R(2)$ (taking $\hat e$
aligned with $\tilde v$, so the two candidate weights coincide). Betweenness of $\Lambda''$ is exactly
monotonicity of $R$ across $p = 0,1,2$.

**A criterion first.** $R(p) = \mathbb{E}_p[r]$, the expectation of the LOCAL wall-to-volume ratio
$r$ under the $|\tilde v|^p$-tilted volume measure, so

$$ \frac{dR}{dp} \;=\; \operatorname{Cov}_p\big(r, \log|\tilde v|\big). \tag{13a} $$

Betweenness holds exactly when the local wall-to-volume ratio is positively correlated with speed
across $p \in [0,2]$. That is the whole mechanism, and it says where to look for a failure: a pore in
which the SLOW part of the volume carries the large surface-to-volume ratio and the intermediate-speed
part a small one.

**Proved for a slowly varying duct.** Let the pore be a duct whose transverse scale varies slowly
along its axis, so that continuity gives $|\tilde v| = c/H(x)$ with $H$ the local half-gap, and, per
unit axial length, $dV = 2H\,dx$ and $dS = 2\,dx$. Then

$$ R(p) = \frac{\int H^{-p}dx}{\int H^{1-p}dx}. $$

Substitute $H = e^{-t}$ and let $\mu$ be the distribution of $t$ along the duct, so
$R(p) = M(p)/M(p-1)$ with $M(q) = \int e^{qt}d\mu$. $\log M$ is convex by Holder, so
$q \mapsto \log M(q) - \log M(q-1)$ is nondecreasing, hence $R$ is nondecreasing in $p$ and

$$ \Lambda \;\le\; \Lambda'' \;\le\; \Lambda', \tag{14} $$

with equality throughout only if $H$ is constant, which is (13). The mechanism is (13a): in this
class $|\tilde v|$ and the local $S/V$ ratio are the SAME function of $H$, up to a power, so $r$ and
$\log|\tilde v|$ are perfectly correlated and the covariance cannot change sign.

The class is wider than the two-dimensional slot the algebra above is written for. The
three-dimensional self-similar case, a circular section with $|\tilde v| = c/H^2$, gives
$R(p) = 2M(2p-1)/M(2p-2)$, still an increment of $\log M$ along an increasing reparametrisation, so
the same proof covers it. The axisymmetric duct of section 5a is covered a second way and more
cheaply: by (13b), $\Lambda \le \Lambda''$ is exactly
$\langle R^{-3}\rangle \ge \langle R^{-2}\rangle\langle R^{-1}\rangle$, which is Chebyshev's sum
inequality on the similarly ordered $R^{-2}$ and $R^{-1}$, with equality only for constant $R$. Self-similar families cannot violate betweenness in either dimension:
varying the SIZE of a fixed shape is exactly what ties $r$ to $|\tilde v|$. Only varying the SHAPE at
fixed size can decouple them.

**Not proved in general, and the reason is that the tie above is a property of the class.** For a pore
whose SHAPE varies, not only its size, $|\tilde v|$ and the local $S/V$ ratio decouple, and $R$ need
not be monotone. A three-region weight witness realises the $U$-shaped pattern (13a) asks for: regions carrying
speeds $|\tilde v| = (1, 3, 100)$ and local ratios $S/V = (10, 1, 10)$, so the slow region and the
fast region both carry the large ratio and the intermediate one carries the small ratio. Its volumes
are not free, and getting them right is what makes the witness a candidate geometry rather than a set
of numbers.

**The isoperimetric constraint binds but does not contradict.** With $|\tilde v| = c/A$ on a section
of area $A$ and perimeter $P \ge 2\sqrt{\pi A}$, the local ratio $r = P/A$ obeys
$r \ge 2\sqrt{\pi/A} = 2\sqrt{\pi |\tilde v|/c}$, that is $c \ge 4\pi|\tilde v|/r^2$. On the pattern
above that is $c \ge (0.1257, 37.699, 12.566)$, so $c \ge 37.699$, binding on the MIDDLE region, which
must then be an exactly circular cylinder of area $4\pi$ and radius $2$, with the outer two regions
slots of width $2/r = 0.2$. Binding is not contradiction, and there is no obstruction here.

**The volumes are where a careless witness fails.** Taking the volumes $(1, 0.3, 5\times10^{-4})$
forces areas $(37.70, 12.57, 0.377)$ against axial extents $(0.0265, 0.0239, 0.00133)$ and transverse
scales $(6.14, 3.55, 0.61)$: the outer regions are shorter along the flow than their own slot widths,
so the flow direction is their shortest dimension, they are not duct segments, and $|\tilde v| = c/A$
is not their kinematics. The two area steps between regions then total $37.32$ of surface against the
$10.305$ of lateral wall the weights count, so the neglected shoulders are $362$ percent of the
counted surface and H1 and H2 both fail at a $100{:}1$ area step. That volume choice is not a
geometry, and it also inverts the standard ordering $\Lambda \le \Lambda'$, which is the tell.

**The pattern survives the repair.** Keep the speeds and the ratios, and give each region the minimum
duct-like volume $V_i = \kappa\,(c/|\tilde v|_i)^{3/2}$, that is axial length equal to the square root
of the area at $\kappa = 1$. Then

$$ R(0) = 8.5487, \qquad R(1) = 6.9022, \qquad R(2) = 8.7757, $$

that is $\Lambda' = 0.2340$, $\Lambda'' = 0.2898$, $\Lambda = 0.2279$. $\Lambda''$ exceeds both of the
others by about $24$ percent, betweenness fails, the shoulders fall to $1.6$ percent of $dS$, and
$\Lambda \le \Lambda'$ is restored. The covariances (13a) are $(-1.751, -1.967, +5.276)$ at
$p = 0, 1, 2$. The construction is scale invariant in $\kappa$. What this settles is that betweenness
is NOT a consequence of definition (1) plus positivity, and that the obstruction is not isoperimetric
either: it needs a relation between the surface and volume distributions of $|\tilde v|$ that only a
restricted class of pores supplies.

**A smooth pore realises the pattern, so betweenness is false.** The experiment this section once
called for has been run: a periodic duct
$\Omega = \{0 \le x < L_x,\ |y| < w(x)/2,\ |z| < h(x)/2\}$ whose AREA profile and ASPECT-RATIO
profile vary independently, with $\log w$ and $\log h$ piecewise constant on three plateaus joined by
compactly supported $C^\infty$ blends, so $w$ and $h$ are exactly smooth and exactly periodic. The
potential problem $\nabla^2\varphi = 0$, $\nabla\varphi\cdot n = 0$ on the wall, is solved on it by
$Q_1$ finite elements on a mapped mesh, periodic in $x$ under an imposed mean gradient, with the
surface integrals taken on the true curved wall. At $L_x = 130.1$,

$$ \Lambda' = 0.2588096, \qquad \Lambda = 0.2449689, \qquad \Lambda'' = 0.2311194, $$

so $\Lambda''$ lies $0.0138495$ BELOW the whole interval $[\Lambda, \Lambda']$, whose width is
$0.0138407$: a margin of $1.001$ interval widths, or $5.99$ percent of $\Lambda''$ itself. A second
pore violates in the OTHER direction, putting $\Lambda''$ above both by $3.24$ percent. H2 holds on
the computed field ($|\tilde v| \in [0.1727, 1.5996]$, and $\min \tilde v\cdot\hat e = 0.1644 > 0$ on
the wall, so there is no stagnation point anywhere on the closure). The witness is
`counterexamples/MD_0015_shape_varying_duct.py`, which re-runs its own validations and raises rather
than returning a stale verdict.

Three checks make the verdict robust rather than a discretisation story. The solver returns
$\Lambda = \Lambda' = \Lambda'' = 2V/S$ to twelve digits on a straight uniform duct and reproduces the
lubrication field on a slowly varying one to parts in $10^4$. Refining the grid moves the violation by
$1.4$ percent, against a violation claimed at $100$ percent of the interval width, and a four-grid
ladder extrapolates to $0.01379$. And H1 is not the loophole: the only non-smooth set is the four
edges of the rectangular section, which is $dS$-null, and replacing the section by a genuinely
$C^\infty$ superellipse STRENGTHENS the violation, from $6.07$ to $7.74$ percent.

So (14) holds on its class and fails in general, and the clean statement of the obstruction is (13a):
betweenness holds if and only if $\operatorname{Cov}_p(r, \log|\tilde v|) \ge 0$ across
$p \in [0,2]$. Isoperimetry forces FAST sections to carry a LARGE local ratio and constrains nothing
else, which is why both a $U$-shaped and a hump-shaped $r(|\tilde v|)$ are constructible and both
signs of the violation are available. A duct whose aspect ratio varies independently of its area is
exactly the freedom that decouples $r$ from $|\tilde v|$.

## 7. What this decides for the consumer

The high-frequency end of a kernel bank for $g_{v\kappa}$ is NOT pinned by $(\Lambda, \Lambda')$.
(11) needs $\Lambda''$, a linearly weighted wall length that no transport correlation in use supplies,
and section 6 shows $(\Lambda, \Lambda')$ does not even BRACKET it: on the witness pore $\Lambda''$
sits $5.99$ percent outside the pair, and the violation is available in both directions. So no mean of
the two published lengths is a bound on $\Lambda''$, let alone an estimate of it. That is a
measurement requirement, not a re-fit.

The requirement is regime dependent, and that is the mitigation. $\Lambda''$ enters only the
$O(\omega^{-1/2})$ deficit of (11), which is small precisely when the layers are thin, that is when
the pore scale over the thermal penetration depth is large. In the OPPOSITE regime, pore scale over
penetration depth well below one, the deficit is not the leading behaviour at all and the static
parameter $\langle\Psi\rangle$ of MD_0014 is what controls the gain. So a closure that pins the
static end exactly and interpolates the high-frequency end through some mean of the two published
lengths, for instance $\Lambda_e = \sqrt{\Lambda\Lambda'}$, remains usable for a medium operated at
small pore-scale-to-penetration-depth ratio, where the term it controls is not the leading behaviour.
What section 6 removes is the JUSTIFICATION that such a mean brackets the truth: on the witness pore
$\sqrt{\Lambda\Lambda'} = 0.2518$ sits $8.9$ percent above the true $\Lambda'' = 0.2311$. The choice
is a modelling convenience with a quantified error, not an interpolation between bounds, and it is
indefensible for a medium operated at thin layers, where $\Lambda''$ has to be measured or computed
from the pore geometry. Which regime a given medium is in is a question for whoever operates it, not
for this file.

## Formalization blockers

MD_0015 is the least formalizable of the five, and the obstruction is in the statement rather than in
the proof.

- Section 2's H1 is a matched-asymptotic step: "the layer is locally one dimensional and the
  correction is $O(\delta/\mathcal{R})$" is a statement about an outer and an inner expansion of a
  three-dimensional elliptic problem, with no error bound given here and none available from Mathlib.
  Formalizing MD_0015 as printed would mean first stating a boundary-layer theorem with explicit
  remainder for $(s - \alpha\nabla^2)\Theta = s\tilde u$ on a smooth domain, which is a research-level
  formalization, not an exercise.
- Section 4's (4), the Johnson, Koplik and Dashen expansion, is cited, not proved here. Any honest
  formalization must either prove it (the same boundary-layer machinery, plus the energy pairing) or
  carry it as an explicit hypothesis. Carrying it as a hypothesis is the right move and makes
  section 4 a two-line algebraic consequence.
- What IS formalizable now, cheaply and usefully, is section 2's algebra and section 6's proved half:
  (a) for $a = b/\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}$ and $\mathrm{Pr} > 0$, the deficit (7) equals
  $b^{-1}(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}+\mathrm{Pr})/(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}})$, including the removable
  cancellation at $\mathrm{Pr}=1$, which is `field_simp` plus the factorization of $1-x^3$ over
  $x = \sqrt{\mathrm{Pr}}$; and (b) monotonicity of $M(q)/M(q-1)$ for a moment generating function,
  which is convexity of $\log M$, available through `inner_le_nnorm_mul_nnorm` style Holder or
  directly `MeasureTheory.inner_le_weight_mul_Lp_of_norm_le`. Both are self-contained and neither
  needs a PDE.
- The betweenness clause should not be formalized in its printed form at all until section 6's open
  question is settled, since the witness there makes it likely false as stated.
